# lgl-employment

**Summary:** Employment Counsel responsible for advising the organization on employment and labor law matters across all jurisdictions, including worker classification, policy development, employee relations, and regulatory compliance.

**Level:** IC (Attorney)
**Reports to:** General Counsel / Associate General Counsel
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Advise HR leadership and people managers on employment law matters including hiring, discipline, termination, reductions in force, and restructuring across all operating jurisdictions
- Manage worker classification analyses for employee vs. independent contractor determinations, including IC reclassification risk assessments and compliance programs
- Review, draft, and maintain employment policies, employee handbooks, and workplace agreements including offer letters, separation agreements, restrictive covenant agreements, and arbitration agreements
- Handle employment-related claims, demand letters, agency charges, and litigation working with internal stakeholders and external counsel
- Support mergers, acquisitions, and divestitures by conducting employment due diligence, advising on integration strategies, and managing workforce transition issues
- Advise on leave administration including FMLA, ADA accommodations, state paid family leave, military leave, and international statutory leave requirements
- Manage workplace accommodation interactive process and provide legal guidance on reasonable accommodation determinations
- Monitor legislative and regulatory developments affecting the workforce including wage and hour, paid leave, pay equity, and non-compete reform
- Develop and deliver employment law training for HR professionals, managers, and executives on high-risk topics
- Advise on employee mobility issues including restrictive covenants, non-solicitation agreements, garden leave, and trade secret protection in the employment context
- Manage immigration compliance including H-1B, L-1, TN, and other work visa sponsorship programs and I-9 compliance
- Advise on employee benefits legal issues in coordination with benefits team and external benefits counsel

## Core Competencies
- Deep expertise in employment and labor law across multiple jurisdictions and regulatory frameworks
- Worker classification and independent contractor compliance including federal, state, and international tests
- Employment policy drafting, implementation, and handbook creation
- Workplace investigation methodology — discrimination, harassment, retaliation, and whistleblower complaints
- Dispute resolution including mediation, arbitration, and administrative agency practice
- Cross-functional partnership with HR, benefits, payroll, and talent acquisition teams
- Multi-jurisdictional awareness — federal, state, local, and international employment law
- Risk assessment and practical business counseling on employment decisions
- Litigation management for employment claims including wage and hour class/collective actions
- Negotiation for separation agreements, settlement terms, and restrictive covenant disputes
- Transactional employment law including M&A due diligence and integration
- Communication skills for training delivery, executive advisory, and dispute resolution

## Career Progression
- **Junior Employment Associate (0-3 years):** Supports employment litigation, policy reviews, and research. Typically law firm trained. Gains administrative agency practice and investigation experience. Learns employment law fundamentals across multiple areas.
- **Employment Counsel (3-7 years):** Independently manages employment matters. Handles agency charges, separation negotiations, and employee relations advice. Reviews policies. Supports M&A employment due diligence. Begins training delivery.
- **Senior Employment Counsel (7-12 years):** Leads employment strategy for the organization. Manages complex litigation portfolio. Handles large-scale reductions in force. Advises C-suite on employment risk. Manages immigration function. May supervise junior counsel.
- **Director - Employment Law (12-15 years):** Owns the entire employment legal function. Manages employment litigation budget. Serves on People leadership team. Develops global employment policy framework. Partners with HR executives on workforce strategy.
- **VP / Associate GC - Employment (15+ years):** Executive-level ownership of employment legal. Advises board on employment risk. Sets global employment compliance strategy. Manages external counsel relationships and budget. Represents organization in high-profile employment matters.

- Morning review of incoming employment requests — termination consultations, policy questions, and claim intakes from HR business partners
- Employee relations consultations with HR — advise on discipline, performance management, and termination decisions for compliance and litigation risk

- Agency charge response management — EEOC, state fair employment agencies, NLRB — including position statements and investigative responses

- Weekly claims review with HR — update on active litigation, demand letters, and settlement authority requests
- Monthly compliance monitoring — pay equity analysis, legislative tracking, training completion rates
- Quarterly training delivery — harassment prevention, leave management, manager employment law fundamentals
- Communication rhythm combines rapid-response advisory with structured reporting cadences

## Regional Nuances
- **NA:** Complex multi-jurisdictional environment — federal laws (Title VII, ADEA, ADA, FLSA, FMLA, WARN, NLRA) plus 50 state law variations. California, New York, Illinois, and Massachusetts have particularly stringent employee protections. Non-compete restrictions are increasing (FTC proposed ban, state-level limitations). At-will employment is the default (except Montana). Wage and hour class actions are a significant risk. Compensation $180K-$350K.
- **EMEA:** Strong employee protections with strict termination laws — statutory notice periods, severance requirements, and unfair dismissal protections. Works Councils (Germany, Netherlands, France, Austria) have significant consultation and codetermination rights for operational changes. Collective bargaining agreements may apply even to non-unionized employers in certain sectors. Employee privacy and monitoring is heavily regulated under GDPR. UK has a less restrictive but still employee-protective framework.
- **APAC:** Highly variable — China's labor law is employee-protective with mandatory severance, social insurance requirements, and strict termination restrictions. Japan's lifetime employment norms create unique restructuring challenges. India has complex labor codes with both central and state regulation. Australia has a strong Fair Work system. Singapore is more employer-friendly. Employee privacy and surveillance laws vary dramatically.
- **LATAM:** Brazil has the most employee-protective framework globally — severe termination costs, mandatory profit sharing, union-driven collective bargaining. Labor litigation is extremely common and active. Mexico's labor justice reform strengthened unions and employee protections. Argentina has strong employee protections with high inflation complicating compensation adjustments. Chile has a more balanced framework. Colombia and Peru have moderate protections.

## Industry Variations
- **SaaS:** Focus on contractor classification (many IC engineers and consultants), equity compensation compliance, at-will employment, remote work and multi-state nexus issues, global hiring compliance for distributed teams. Restrictive covenants heavily used for technology employees.
- **Fintech:** Highly regulated — background check requirements for licensed employees, FINRA compliance for registered roles, compensation approval processes, conflicts of interest and personal trading policies, heightened scrutiny for lending and payments roles.
- **Healthcare:** Compliance-focused — licensure verification, credentialing requirements for clinical roles, background check depth specific to healthcare (OIG exclusion screening), patient privacy training requirements, mandatory reporting obligations.
- **Manufacturing:** Union and labor relations focus — collective bargaining agreements, union organizing campaigns, grievance procedures, OSHA compliance integration, wage and hour compliance for hourly/shift workers, WARN Act compliance for plant closures and mass layoffs.
- **E-commerce:** Large variable workforce with heavy IC and gig worker classification exposure, warehouse and logistics worker wage and hour compliance, multi-state and multi-country workforce, safety compliance for warehouse operations, flexible scheduling laws.

## Common Challenges
- Managing worker classification risk with evolving regulatory tests (federal, state, and international) and enforcement priorities
- Navigating the patchwork of restrictive covenant laws as non-compete enforcement continues to be restricted and challenged
- Keeping policies and practices current across 50+ US states and numerous international jurisdictions simultaneously
- Advising on terminations and reductions in force while managing litigation risk, reputational concerns, and employee morale
- Responding to increasing pay equity scrutiny with proactive reporting, analysis, and remediation
- Managing global workforce compliance for a distributed/remote organization where employees may work from any location
