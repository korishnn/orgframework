# ops-vendor

**Summary:** Manages ongoing third-party vendor and supplier relationships, monitors service level performance, governs vendor risk, and drives continuous improvement through structured governance frameworks.

**Level:** IC or Manager
**Reports to:** COO / Procurement
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Manage ongoing relationships with third-party vendors, service providers, and strategic partners across all categories and spend tiers.
- Monitor vendor performance against contractual SLAs and KPIs, conducting regular business reviews and performance scorecards.
- Identify and mitigate vendor-related risks including financial instability, concentration risk, compliance gaps, data security exposure, and geopolitical factors.
- Negotiate contract renewals, amendments, pricing adjustments, and service-level improvements through structured relationship management.
- Maintain the vendor governance framework including tier classification, relationship management approach, escalation paths, and governance meeting cadence.
- Manage the centralized vendor database ensuring accurate records of vendor information, contracts, contacts, certifications, and performance history.
- Conduct vendor business continuity reviews, ensuring critical vendors have robust disaster recovery and business continuity plans.
- Manage vendor offboarding including contract termination notice, transition planning, knowledge transfer, data return/destruction, and system decommissioning.
- Develop and maintain vendor risk profiles including annual risk reassessment for critical and high-risk vendors.
- Facilitate vendor dispute resolution working with procurement, legal, and operational stakeholders to resolve issues.
- Track vendor spending against budget and contract terms, identifying cost optimization and consolidation opportunities.
- Manage vendor diversity program including identification, certification, and tracking spend with diverse suppliers (minority, women-owned, veteran, LGBTQ+).
- Lead vendor consolidation initiatives to reduce the total number of vendors and increase spend leverage with strategic suppliers.

## Core Competencies

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Vendor performance score: average score across critical vendor business reviews
- SLA compliance rate: percentage of SLAs met across all managed vendors
- Vendor risk assessment completion rate: percentage of vendors assessed within risk tier timeline
- Vendor consolidation rate: year-over-year change in total vendor count
- Cost savings from vendor management: renegotiated savings and efficiency gains
- Vendor offboarding compliance: percentage of offboards completed with data destruction certification
- Vendor incident resolution time: average days to resolve critical vendor issues
- Vendor diversity spend percentage: percentage of total vendor spend with diverse suppliers

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Vendor Management Associate or Vendor Coordinator supporting vendor records, onboarding administration, and meeting scheduling.
- Mid level: Vendor Manager managing a portfolio of vendors, conducting business reviews, monitoring SLAs, and managing vendor risk.
- Senior level: Senior Vendor Manager managing strategic and critical vendor relationships, leading governance programs, and negotiating renewals.
- Lead level: Vendor Management Lead or Program Manager overseeing the vendor governance framework, risk program, and team of vendor managers.
- Director level: Director of Vendor Management or Head of Third-Party Risk owning the entire vendor management function, strategy, and risk framework.
- VP level: CPO or VP of Supplier Management responsible for enterprise supplier ecosystem management and strategic partnerships.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as coordinator, 2-3 years as vendor manager, 2-3 years as senior, then lead/director track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Review vendor performance dashboards and alerts, respond to vendor escalations, check contract expiration calendar for upcoming renewals.
- Midday: Vendor business review meetings (monthly or quarterly cadence), internal stakeholder meetings on vendor issues, contract amendment discussions.
- Afternoon: Vendor risk assessment reviews, vendor database updates, contract renewal preparation, vendor performance analysis, dispute resolution coordination.
- Weekly: Vendor performance monitoring, SLA compliance check, vendor issue tracking, new vendor onboarding request review.
- Monthly: Vendor performance scorecards, critical vendor touchpoint meetings, vendor spend analysis, vendor risk review for high-risk vendors.
- Quarterly: Quarterly business reviews (QBRs) with strategic vendors, vendor risk reassessment cycle, vendor portfolio review, vendor diversity reporting.
- Annual: Annual strategic vendor reviews, vendor program health assessment, business continuity plan review for critical vendors, vendor governance framework update.
- Rhythms: Vendor business reviews follow quarterly or monthly schedules for different vendor tiers. Contract renewals create predictable peaks. Risk assessment cycles follow the TPRM calendar. Incident-driven work is reactive to vendor performance issues.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: Vendor management is highly structured with formal governance tiers. Emphasis on risk management in vendor relationships. SLA-driven relationships are standard. Vendor consolidation is a common strategic priority. Supplier diversity programs are a significant focus.
- EMEA: GDPR creates strict vendor data processing requirements and liability allocation. Strong contract law traditions in Germany require formal amendments. Works council rights in vendor selection affecting services involving employee data (Germany, Netherlands). English law contracts are common for international vendors.
- APAC: Relationship-based vendor management cultures (guanxi in China, keiretsu in Japan). Face-saving considerations in vendor performance discussions. Longer payment terms in some markets (Philippines, Indonesia). Government vendor regulations in China require specific compliance.
- LATAM: Relationship-based business culture heavily influences vendor management. Vendor concentration is often higher with fewer qualified suppliers. Contract enforcement can be challenging in some legal systems (Brazil's slow courts). Personal relationships often override contractual terms in vendor interactions.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Focus on SaaS vendor management (subscription oversight, license optimization). Software vendor consolidation and rationalization. Professional services vendor management for consulting partners. Infrastructure vendor management for cloud providers.
- Fintech: Regulatory scrutiny of third-party relationships (outsourcing regulation). Critical vendor designation for business continuity. Enhanced due diligence for vendors with access to financial data. Regulatory approval required for some third-party arrangements.
- Healthcare: HIPAA business associate agreements are a core part of vendor management. Vendor quality audits for medical suppliers. Group purchasing organization (GPO) relationships and compliance. Patient safety impact of vendor performance.
- Manufacturing: Supplier quality management is integrated with vendor management. Long-term supplier partnerships with joint development agreements. Global vendor network with multi-region management complexity. Raw material vendor concentration risk management.
- E-commerce: Logistics and fulfillment vendor management is critical. Last-mile carrier performance management. Seasonal vendor capacity planning and surge support. Technology vendor management for e-commerce platform providers. Multiple vendors in each category creates complex governance.

## Common Challenges
- Getting internal stakeholders to adhere to the vendor governance process when they prefer direct vendor relationships.
- Managing vendor performance when the organization lacks leverage due to low spend or specialized requirements.
- Balancing relationship building with the need to hold vendors accountable for performance gaps.
- Conducting effective vendor risk assessments with limited access to vendor internal information.
- Maintaining accurate vendor data when departments independently engage vendors outside of procurement.
- Managing vendor transitions and offboarding without disrupting business operations or losing institutional knowledge.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: SOX third-party control reliance, FTC safeguarding customer information for vendors with consumer data, state data breach notification laws for vendor incidents, GLBA vendor oversight for financial services, HIPAA business associate agreements for healthcare, CCPA vendor data processing agreements, OFAC sanctions screening for vendors.
- UK: UK GDPR vendor data processing agreements, FCA Outsourcing and Third-Party Risk Management rules (SYSC 8), PRA Supervisory Statement SS2/21 for operational resilience in vendor arrangements, Modern Slavery Act supplier diligence.
- EU: GDPR Article 28 (data processor agreements), EBA Outsourcing Guidelines for financial services, EIOPA Outsourcing Guidelines for insurance, DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act) for ICT vendor management (financial sector), CSDDD Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive supply chain requirements.
- Canada: OSFI Guideline B-10 Third-Party Risk Management for federally regulated financial institutions, PIPEDA vendor data processing agreements, provincial privacy law vendor requirements, OSFI E-21 Operational Resilience and Third-Party Dependencies.
- Brazil: LGPD vendor data processing agreements, BCB Resolution 459/2022 for financial institution vendor management (outsourcing), Bacen outsourcing regulation for financial services, SUSEP regulation for insurance vendor management.
- Australia: APRA CPS 230 Operational Risk Management (including third-party arrangements for regulated entities), ASIC Regulatory Guide 259 (risk management for outsourced services in financial services), Privacy Act 1988 vendor data handling, OAIC vendor data breach notification requirements.
