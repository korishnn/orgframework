# ops-process

**Summary:** Drives operational excellence through process mapping, analysis, improvement, automation, and continuous improvement methodologies to enhance efficiency, quality, and customer experience.

**Level:** IC or Manager
**Reports to:** COO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Map and document current-state business processes using BPMN 2.0, process flowcharts, value stream maps, and process narratives.
- Identify inefficiencies, bottlenecks, redundancies, and failure points in business processes through data analysis, observation, and stakeholder interviews.
- Design and implement future-state process improvements using Lean, Six Sigma, Kaizen, and continuous improvement methodologies.
- Lead automation initiatives using RPA (robotic process automation), workflow automation, and process digitization to reduce manual effort and error rates.
- Establish process metrics and monitoring dashboards to sustain improvements and provide visibility into process performance over time.
- Facilitate process improvement workshops (Kaizen events, rapid improvement sessions, design sprints) with cross-functional teams.
- Conduct root cause analysis on process failures, quality issues, and operational bottlenecks using fishbone diagrams, 5 Whys, and failure mode analysis.
- Develop business requirements documents for process change initiatives, system implementations, and automation projects.
- Manage process documentation and knowledge bases ensuring processes are current, accessible, and used for training and compliance.
- Measure and report process improvement results including cycle time reduction, cost savings, quality improvement, and customer satisfaction impact.
- Drive process standardization across the organization, reducing variation and enabling best practice sharing between business units.
- Support organizational change management for process changes, including stakeholder communication, training, and adoption monitoring.
- Lead process maturity assessments using capability maturity models (CMM, CMMI) to benchmark current state and guide improvement roadmap.
- Conduct time and motion studies, workload analysis, and capacity modeling to optimize resource allocation and staffing levels.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Process cycle time reduction: percentage reduction in end-to-end process duration
- Cost savings from process improvement: total operational cost reduction attributed to projects
- Process automation rate: percentage of manual process steps automated
- Process compliance rate: percentage of process steps executed as designed
- Defect rate reduction: percentage reduction in process errors or rework
- Improvement project pipeline: number of active and queued improvement projects
- Employee engagement in continuous improvement: participation rate in Kaizen or ideation programs
- Process documentation coverage: percentage of business processes documented and current

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Process Analyst or Continuous Improvement Coordinator supporting process mapping, data collection, and Kaizen event logistics.
- Mid level: Process Improvement Specialist or Lean Six Sigma Green Belt leading improvement projects independently, facilitating workshops, and implementing solutions.
- Senior level: Senior Process Improvement Manager or Lean Six Sigma Black Belt managing complex improvement programs, coaching Green Belts, and driving strategic initiatives.
- Lead level: Process Improvement Director or Head of Operational Excellence owning the enterprise improvement strategy, methodology, and governance.
- VP level: VP of Operational Excellence or Chief Process Officer integrating process excellence with digital transformation, innovation, and business strategy.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as analyst, 2-3 years as specialist, 2-3 years as senior manager, then director/VP track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Process discovery phase: Conduct stakeholder interviews, observe process execution, collect process data, map current-state flows, identify pain points.
- Analysis phase: Measure process performance, analyze data for patterns, quantify waste, calculate impact of defects, prioritize improvement opportunities.
- Improvement design phase: Design future-state processes, develop automation requirements, create implementation plan, build business case, obtain stakeholder approval.
- Implementation phase: Execute process changes, configure automation, train process participants, manage rollout, troubleshoot issues.
- Sustainment phase: Monitor process metrics, verify improvement results, document new process, handoff to operations team, celebrate success.
- Weekly: Process improvement team standup, project status tracking, stakeholder updates, obstacle removal, Kaizen event preparation.
- Monthly: Improvement metrics reporting to leadership, project pipeline review, process compliance audits, improvement opportunity identification with operations teams.
- Quarterly: Strategic portfolio review, improvement program results reporting, process mining analysis refresh, Kaizen event schedule planning.
- Rhythms: Each improvement project follows DMAIC or PDCA lifecycle (typically 4-12 weeks). Projects run concurrently with overlapping phases. Kaizen events are intensive 3-5 day workshops. Quarterly reviews align improvement priorities with business strategy.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: Process improvement is widely embraced with strong Lean Six Sigma certification culture. Black Belts and Master Black Belts are common. Process mining is increasingly used for data-driven improvement. Focus on digital transformation and automation. Strong ROI expectations for improvement projects.
- EMEA: Strong quality and process culture in Germany (Qualitat, Ordnung). European Foundation for Quality Management (EFQM) framework is popular. Works council involvement in process changes affecting work conditions (Germany, Netherlands). ISO standards are widely adopted across European operations.
- APAC: Kaizen and Lean originated in Japan and are deeply embedded in Japanese manufacturing culture. Toyota Production System (TPS) is the gold standard in Japan. India has strong process orientation with Six Sigma widely practiced. Singapore emphasizes operational excellence as part of national competitiveness.
- LATAM: Continuous improvement culture is developing but less mature than in NA or Japan. Strong hierarchy can limit front-line employee participation in improvement. Relationship-based culture affects Kaizen workshop dynamics. Improvement projects often face resource constraints. Focus on practical, low-cost improvements rather than automation.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Agile and DevOps process improvement is central. Customer journey and user experience process mapping. Feature delivery process optimization. Onboarding and self-service process automation. Growth experimentation process design.
- Fintech: Compliance process optimization is critical (KYC/CDD, AML screening, reporting). Risk management process improvement. Customer onboarding process streamlining. Regulatory reporting automation. Credit decisioning process optimization.
- Healthcare: Patient care process improvement (clinical pathways). Hospital throughput and discharge process optimization. Revenue cycle process improvement. Regulatory compliance process management (surveys, accreditation). Lean in healthcare (Lean Healthcare, Virginia Mason model).
- Manufacturing: Production process optimization is core. Material flow and plant layout improvement. Set-up time reduction (SMED). Overall equipment effectiveness (OEE) improvement. Quality process control and statistical process control (SPC). Supply chain optimization.
- E-commerce: Order fulfillment process optimization. Warehouse picking and packing process improvement. Returns and reverse logistics process optimization. Customer service process automation. Last-mile delivery efficiency improvement. Seasonal capacity planning process improvement.

## Common Challenges
- Sustaining process improvements after the project team moves on to new initiatives (deterioration of gains).
- Overcoming resistance to process change from employees who are comfortable with existing ways of working.
- Balancing the need for standardization with business unit requirements for flexibility and local adaptation.
- Getting accurate as-is process data when processes are undocumented, inconsistently followed, or vary across locations.
- Demonstrating the value of process improvement in financial terms that leadership finds compelling.
- Managing the scope of improvement initiatives that expand beyond the original process boundary.
- Building a continuous improvement culture where employees at all levels identify and act on improvement opportunities.
