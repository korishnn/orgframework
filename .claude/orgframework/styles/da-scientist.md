# da-scientist

**Summary:** Builds predictive models, runs experiments, and applies advanced statistical methods to solve complex business problems through data-driven approaches.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** Data Science Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Develop predictive models and machine learning algorithms for business applications including recommendation, forecasting, classification, and optimization.
- Design and analyze A/B tests and controlled experiments to measure causal effects of product changes and business interventions.
- Apply advanced statistical methods to solve complex business problems including causal inference, time series analysis, and Bayesian methods.
- Communicate findings and recommendations to technical and non-technical audiences through presentations and written reports.
- Research and evaluate new methodologies, techniques, and algorithms for potential business applications.
- Partner with product and engineering teams to deploy models into production systems and applications.
- Conduct exploratory data analysis to discover patterns, relationships, and business insights in complex datasets.
- Design feature engineering pipelines and data preparation processes for model development.
- Validate model performance, monitor for drift, and iterate on models to maintain accuracy.
- Contribute to data science best practices, code standards, and reproducible research practices.
- Participate in peer reviews of model development, experimental design, and analytical approaches.
- Mentor junior data scientists and contribute to team knowledge development.

## Core Competencies
- Machine learning and predictive modeling across supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning paradigms.
- Experimental design and statistical inference for rigorous causal analysis and hypothesis testing.
- Programming (Python, R) and data manipulation for analytical and model development workflows.
- Scientific thinking and hypothesis testing for structured, evidence-based problem-solving.
- Communication and data storytelling for translating technical findings into business recommendations.
- Research methodology and continuous learning for staying current with the rapidly evolving field.
- Deep learning and neural networks for applications in NLP, computer vision, and sequence modeling.
- Feature engineering and dimensionality reduction for creating effective model inputs.
- Model evaluation and validation including cross-validation, holdout testing, and bias/variance diagnostics.
- Production ML knowledge for deploying and maintaining models in operational environments.
- Bayesian statistics and probabilistic modeling for uncertainty quantification.
- Domain expertise for applying appropriate methods to specific business contexts and problem types.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Model performance metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, RMSE, AUC-ROC) against baseline and targets.
- Business impact of deployed models measured through revenue lift, cost savings, or efficiency gains.
- Experiment velocity — number of A/B tests designed and analyzed per quarter.
- Model uptime and reliability in production including drift monitoring and retraining frequency.
- Time from research question to deployed model (end-to-end cycle time).
- Reproducibility rate — percentage of analyses that can be independently reproduced.
- Team knowledge contribution through documentation, code reviews, and mentoring.

## Career Progression
Data scientists typically start as junior/associate (0-2 years), progress to mid-level (2-4 years), senior (4-7 years), and staff/principal (7+ years). Growth involves expanding from individual modeling to strategic problem definition and organizational influence. Senior data scientists lead complex projects and define methodological approaches. Staff/principal scientists set technical strategy and drive innovation across the organization. Career branching includes MLOps specialization, research scientist roles (emphasis on novel methods), or data science management. PhDs are common at senior levels but not universally required. Advancement depends on business impact, technical depth, and thought leadership. Distinguished data scientist and fellow tracks exist in larger organizations for IC career progression.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A data scientist's week blends research, development, and collaboration. Project work follows an iterative cycle: problem definition and stakeholder alignment, exploratory data analysis, feature engineering, model development and evaluation, and deployment support. Mornings are often deep work time for coding, analysis, and experimentation. Afternoons include team stand-ups, stakeholder meetings, and code reviews. Experiment design involves consulting with product teams on A/B test design, sample size calculation, and success metric definition. Model deployment requires partnering with ML engineers on pipeline implementation and validation. Regular literature reviews and paper readings maintain technical currency. Weekly data science team meetings share findings, discuss methodology, and align on practices. Quarterly reviews present project impact and roadmap to leadership. Conference participation and paper submissions are common for senior researchers.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **Product Managers** — Partners on problem definition, experiment design, and translating model outputs into product features.
- **Data Analysts** — Collaborates on metric definition, data exploration, and insight generation.
- **Data Engineers** — Partners on data pipeline access, feature engineering infrastructure, and data quality.
- **ML Engineers / MLOps** — Works on model deployment, monitoring, and production infrastructure.
- **Software Engineers** — Integrates models into applications and product features.
- **Marketing** — Supports with customer segmentation, propensity modeling, and campaign optimization.
- **Finance** — Collaborates on forecasting models, risk models, and financial predictions.
- **Operations** — Develops optimization models for supply chain, logistics, and resource allocation.
- **Legal / Compliance** — Ensures model fairness, transparency, and regulatory compliance.
- **Academic Partners** — Collaborates on research projects, internships, and methodology development.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Highest concentration of data science roles globally, especially in tech hubs. PhD preferred but not required at senior levels. Strong emphasis on production ML impact. High compensation with significant equity components. Competitive talent market with high mobility.
**EMEA:** Strong emphasis on privacy-preserving ML and ethical AI given GDPR context. PhD is more commonly expected for data scientist roles in Europe. Lower compensation than NA but strong research culture. Academic-industry collaboration is more common. EU AI Act compliance is an emerging focus.
**APAC:** Data science is growing rapidly with large talent pools in India and China. China has strong ML research output and government AI initiatives. Japan values data science skills but the discipline is less established in traditional companies. Singapore is a regional hub for data science and AI. Cost advantages for building data science teams in India.
**LATAM:** Data science is an emerging discipline with growing talent pools. Many data scientists in LATAM are self-taught or from computational backgrounds. Remote work enables access to global employment opportunities. Growing startup ecosystem creates demand for data science skills. Lower salaries but lower cost of living.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Modeling focuses on churn prediction, customer lifetime value, recommendation systems, and pricing optimization. Product analytics and user behavior modeling are core applications. PLG data science emphasizes activation, engagement, and viral coefficient modeling. Fast experimentation cycles with large user bases.
**Fintech:** Credit risk modeling, fraud detection, and algorithmic trading are primary applications. Regulatory compliance for model governance (SR 11-7, model validation). Explainable AI is critical for lending decisions. Time series forecasting for financial markets and economic indicators.
**Healthcare:** Clinical prediction models, medical imaging analysis, drug discovery ML, and patient outcome modeling. HIPAA and patient privacy constraints require federated learning and differential privacy approaches. FDA validation requirements for ML-based diagnostic tools. Genomic data science and personalized medicine.
**E-commerce:** Recommendation systems, personalization algorithms, demand forecasting, and dynamic pricing. Search ranking and relevance models. Fraud detection for transactions and returns. Supply chain optimization and inventory management models. Customer lifetime value and churn models.

## Common Challenges
- Bridging the gap between research-grade models and production-grade systems with reliability, latency, and monitoring requirements.
- Securing clean, labeled, and representative training data when data quality issues and labeling costs are significant.
- Communicating model limitations, uncertainty, and risks to non-technical stakeholders who expect perfect predictions.
- Managing stakeholder expectations about what ML can realistically achieve vs. overhyped AI promises.
- Ensuring model fairness, mitigating bias, and maintaining transparency in automated decision systems.
- Keeping models performant in production when data distributions drift and user behavior evolves.
- Reproducing analyses and models when experiment tracking and data versioning practices are immature.
- Navigating the tension between developing novel methods and delivering practical business value efficiently.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- GDPR — right to explanation for automated decisions, data minimization, and consent for model training data.
- EU AI Act — risk-based classification of AI systems and compliance requirements for high-risk applications.
- SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12 — model risk management standards for financial services models.
- CCPA — consumer rights related to automated decision-making and profiling.
- HIPAA — protected health information handling in model training and inference.
- Fair lending laws (ECOA, FHA) — model fairness and anti-discrimination requirements for credit and lending models.
- FDA regulations for ML-based medical devices (SaMD) and clinical decision support.
- Algorithmic accountability regulations requiring bias testing, transparency reporting, and model documentation.
- ISO/IEC 42001 — AI management system standard for responsible AI governance.
