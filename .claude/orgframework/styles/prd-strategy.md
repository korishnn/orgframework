# prd-strategy

**Summary:** Product Strategist responsible for defining the organization's long-term product vision, competitive positioning, market strategy, and portfolio-level investment decisions.

**Level:** Director / VP+
**Reports to:** CPO / CEO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Define and continuously refine the long-term product vision, strategic north star, and multi-year product direction for the organization
- Conduct comprehensive market analysis including TAM/SAM/SOM assessments, market sizing, growth trajectory modeling, and segment prioritization
- Drive competitive intelligence programs covering competitor product strategies, positioning, feature comparisons, pricing models, and market share analysis
- Identify new product adjacencies, platform expansion opportunities, and business model innovations for growth and diversification
- Develop compelling strategic narratives, business cases, and investment theses for executive leadership and board of directors
- Partner with C-suite executives to align product strategy with overall corporate strategy, financial goals, and operational capabilities
- Monitor industry trends, emerging technologies, regulatory shifts, and macro-economic factors that could impact product direction
- Guide portfolio-level investment decisions including build vs. buy vs. partner analysis, resource allocation across product lines, and market entry sequencing
- Develop and maintain strategic frameworks for product portfolio management including BCG matrix, GE-McKinsey, or custom models
- Lead strategic planning processes including annual strategic reviews, quarterly strategy refreshes, and OKR cascade from strategy to execution
- Represent the product organization's strategic perspective to external audiences including analysts, investors, and industry events
- Foster strategic thinking capability across the product organization through frameworks, training, and coaching

## Core Competencies
- Strategic thinking and vision setting — ability to synthesize complex inputs into a clear, compelling product vision
- Market analysis and sizing methodology including bottoms-up and top-down approaches
- Competitive intelligence gathering and strategic war-gaming
- Business model innovation and portfolio strategy frameworks
- Executive communication and storytelling with board-level presence
- Cross-functional influence without direct authority across engineering, marketing, sales, and finance
- Long-term planning and emerging trend spotting — connecting macro trends to product implications
- Financial acumen for ROI analysis, unit economics, and investment modeling
- Organizational design thinking — structuring teams to execute strategy effectively
- Ecosystem thinking — platform dynamics, network effects, and marketplace strategy
- M&A strategy for product acquisitions, technology acquisitions, and build vs. buy decisions
- Change management for strategic transformations and pivots

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Strategic plan accuracy — product portfolio revenue vs. plan and market share trajectory
- New product and market entry success rate and time to meaningful revenue
- Competitive position improvement — relative market share, brand consideration, win rates
- Portfolio health — revenue concentration, growth contribution from new products, portfolio synergy realization
- Strategic initiative ROI — net present value and internal rate of return on strategic bets
- Organizational strategy alignment score from employee surveys and leadership feedback
- Analyst and investor perception of product strategy strength

## Career Progression
- **Product Manager / Strategist (3-5 years):** Strong PM background begins transitioning to strategy work. Supports strategy documentation and competitive analysis. Learns strategic frameworks. May hold an MBA or equivalent experience.
- **Senior Product Strategist / Director (5-10 years):** Leads strategy for a product line or business unit. Develops business cases and market analyses. Presents to executive leadership. Manages strategic planning processes. Deep domain expertise established.
- **VP Product Strategy (10-15 years):** Owns the enterprise product strategy. Reports to CPO or CEO. Leads strategic planning cycle. Manages a strategy team. Partners with C-suite on corporate strategy. Represents strategy externally.
- **Chief Product Officer / CPO (15+ years):** Executive-level ownership of product strategy and product organization. Sets vision and direction for all product lines. Board-level reporting. Ultimate accountability for product success. Shapes company strategy at the highest level.
- **CEO / Founder (20+ years):** May be a final career step. Full P&L ownership. Sets company vision and strategy. Ultimate accountability for all functions including product, sales, marketing, and operations.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Strategy review cadence with product leadership — revisit strategic assumptions, adjust based on market feedback and results
- Market and competitive intelligence analysis — review analyst reports, competitive product launches, market share data, and industry trends
- Strategic initiative development — build business cases for new product areas, market entries, platform plays, and acquisitions
- Executive stakeholder alignment meetings — present strategy options, secure buy-in and investment, adjust based on feedback
- Portfolio review sessions — analyze product line performance against strategic goals, reallocate resources, decide on underperformers
- Industry engagement — analyst briefings, conference speaking, customer executive meetings for market signal gathering
- Strategy workshop facilitation with product teams and cross-functional leaders
- Quarterly strategy refresh cycle leading up to annual strategic planning
- M&A target identification and evaluation in partnership with corporate development
- Board of directors preparation — strategic narrative development, investment requests, performance reporting
- Communication rhythm combines deep work for analysis with scheduled alignment meetings across leadership

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **Executive (exec-ceo, exec-cfo, exec-cto):** Primary partnership — strategy alignment, resource allocation decisions, board presentation content, investment asks
- **Product (prd-manager, prd-roadmap):** Cascading strategy to roadmaps and execution plans. Ensuring strategic alignment across all product areas. Product-level strategic support
- **Marketing (mkt-growth, mkt-brand):** Go-to-market strategy alignment, product positioning and messaging, competitive differentiation, analyst relations strategy
- **Sales (sls-ae, sls-partner):] Channel strategy input, sales feedback on competitive dynamics, customer win/loss analysis for strategic insights
- **Finance (fin-fpa):** Financial modeling for business cases, revenue forecasting, investment analysis, portfolio ROI tracking
- **Corporate Development (exec-innovation):] M&A strategy alignment, target identification, strategic due diligence, integration planning
- **Engineering (eng-arch, eng-platform):** Technical feasibility assessment for strategic initiatives, platform architecture strategy alignment, innovation roadmap
- **Data/Analytics (da-analyst, prd-analytics):** Market sizing data support, competitive benchmarking analytics, portfolio performance reporting
- **Legal (lgl-ip, lgl-compliance):** IP strategy alignment, regulatory impact on strategic initiatives, antitrust considerations for market strategy
- **Customer Research (prd-research):** Strategic user research for long-term opportunity identification, unmet need discovery, market whitespace analysis
- **Investor Relations (fin-investor):** Product strategy narrative for investor communications, analyst day preparation, strategic milestone reporting

## Regional Nuances
- **NA:** Strategy role is well-established and highly valued with significant influence on company direction. MBA pedigree from top schools is common. Compensation is the highest globally ($2000K-$4000K+ base plus significant equity). Strategy roles at large tech companies (FAANG) command premium compensation.
- **EMEA:** Growing strategic product roles with strong presence in London, Berlin, and Amsterdam. More collaborative and consensus-driven strategy development. European CEOs often prefer more inclusive strategy processes. Stronger regulatory awareness shapes strategy — digital sovereignty, AI regulation. Compensation lower than NA but competitive with strong benefits.
- **APAC:** Strategic roles concentrated in Singapore, Hong Kong, and Sydney. Indian product ecosystem is developing strategic depth. Localization strategy is critical — products must adapt to diverse markets, languages, and payment preferences. China requires separate strategy for market entry and competition (WeChat, Alibaba ecosystem). Fast-paced, growth-first strategy orientation.
- **LATAM:** Strategy roles emerging with startup ecosystem growth in Brazil and Mexico. Often combined with CEO/Founder responsibilities in earlier-stage companies. Market entry strategy for LATAM expansion is a common focus. Strong understanding of macroeconomic volatility for strategic planning. Lower compensation but growing opportunities.

## Industry Variations
- **SaaS:** Land-and-expand strategy, product-led growth vs. sales-led approach, multi-product platform strategy, international expansion sequencing, verticalization strategy, usage-based vs. subscription pricing strategy
- **Fintech:** Regulatory-driven strategy, partnership strategy (bank partnerships, processor relationships), geographic expansion strategy constrained by licensing, embedded finance opportunity, open banking strategy
- **Healthcare:** Regulatory strategy is product strategy — FDA clearance roadmap, HIPAA compliance. Provider vs. payer vs. patient focus. Interoperability strategy. Clinical evidence strategy for market adoption. Reimbursement strategy for success.
- **E-commerce:** Marketplace strategy vs. direct retail, platform economy dynamics, logistics and fulfillment strategy, international expansion, payment strategy, mobile-first vs. web strategy, social commerce integration.
- **Manufacturing/IoT:** Hardware-software strategy integration, servitization (product-as-a-service), ecosystem partnership strategy, industry 4.0 and smart factory positioning, sustainability strategy for product differentiation.

## Common Challenges
- Balancing long-term strategic investments against quarterly revenue pressure and short-term investor expectations
- Maintaining strategic focus when the organization is pulled in multiple directions by tactical demands
- Getting accurate competitive intelligence in fast-moving markets with limited visibility into competitor plans
- Building organizational alignment around strategy when different functions have conflicting priorities
- Adapting strategy quickly enough when market conditions, technology, or competition shifts unexpectedly
- Measuring the ROI of strategic initiatives that are inherently uncertain and long-gestation

- Foreign investment review regulations (CFIUS, FIRRMA) for M&A strategy
