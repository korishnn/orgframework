# eng-arch

**Summary:** Software Architect defines and communicates the technical architecture vision, establishes engineering standards, and drives technology strategy across the engineering organization.

**Level:** IC (Staff/Principal)
**Reports to:** VP Engineering / CTO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Define, document, and communicate the overall technical architecture vision and multi-year roadmap
- Establish engineering standards, best practices, design patterns, and technology selection criteria for the organization
- Lead architectural reviews and facilitate Architecture Decision Record (ADR) processes for significant technical decisions
- Identify cross-cutting concerns and drive platform-level improvements that benefit multiple engineering teams
- Mentor senior engineers through pairing, design discussions, and technical career development
- Evaluate emerging technologies and build-vs-buy decisions, making recommendations to engineering leadership
- Ensure architectural decisions align with business goals, scalability requirements, and operational constraints
- Drive the decomposition of monolithic systems into well-bounded services or modules
- Define and enforce architectural governance including dependency management, API compatibility, and deprecation policies
- Produce architecture documentation, system context diagrams, and decision logs for organizational consumption
- Participate in incident retrospectives to identify systemic architectural improvements
- Represent engineering in cross-functional strategy discussions with product, design, and business leadership
- Foster a culture of technical excellence through internal tech talks, working groups, and guild participation

## Core Competencies
- Extensive experience designing and operating large-scale distributed systems in production
- Deep knowledge of multiple programming paradigms, languages, and runtime characteristics
- Strong understanding of architectural trade-offs across consistency, availability, latency, and cost dimensions
- Excellent written and verbal communication skills for diverse audiences from engineers to executives
- Experience with domain-driven design, microservices, event-driven, and modular monolith architectures
- Ability to balance short-term delivery needs with long-term architectural health and sustainability
- Strong systems thinking and ability to reason about complex interactions across distributed systems
- Experience with cloud-native architectures, containerization, and platform engineering patterns
- Knowledge of security architecture, threat modeling, and secure-by-design principles
- Strategic thinking to align technical investments with business outcomes and product roadmaps

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Architectural debt reduction (measured through ADR adoption and legacy system migration progress)
- System reliability and scalability metrics (SLO attainment, incident frequency trends)
- Engineering velocity impact (wait time reduction, developer satisfaction with architecture)
- Technology standardization percentage across teams
- Architecture review throughput and decision cycle time
- Knowledge sharing effectiveness (tech talks, mentoring, documentation quality)
- Technology cost efficiency relative to architectural choices

## Career Progression
- Senior Engineer transition: Moves from team-level technical leadership to cross-team architecture influence
- Staff Architect: Owns architectural decisions for a major domain or group of services, leads design reviews
- Principal Architect: Drives architecture across the entire engineering organization, sets multi-year technical strategy
- Distinguished/Fellow: Company-wide and industry influence, sets technical vision at the highest level
- Typical progression: Senior/Staff to Architect 2-4 years of cross-team architecture experience
- Note: Most architects remain individual contributors; management and architect tracks are distinct

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- No fixed daily standup; may participate in multiple team standups as an observer
- Architecture reviews and design discussions consume 30-50% of the week
- Deep technical investigation and prototyping for architecture decisions
- Writing ADRs, RFCs, and architecture documentation for organizational visibility
- Code review participation focused on architecture-level concerns rather than line-by-line
- Mentoring sessions with senior engineers through pairing and design discussions
- Executive briefings to communicate architecture strategy and trade-offs
- Guild and working group participation for cross-cutting technical topics
- Conference and industry reading to stay current on technology trends

## Regional Nuances
- NA: Dedicated architect roles common at mid-to-large companies; emphasis on FAANG-derived architecture patterns; strong compensation for principal-level architects
- EMEA: Architect roles more common as organizations scale; greater focus on domain-driven design and event sourcing; works council involvement in change management
- APAC: Architect often combined with engineering manager duties; strong Java-centric architecture in enterprise; contract-based architect consulting common in Singapore
- LATAM: Architect roles evolving as organizations grow; often part-time architecture responsibilities combined with hands-on engineering; remote architecture consulting growing

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Multi-tenant architecture patterns, API versioning strategies, data isolation approaches, scalability architecture for growing customer bases
- Fintech: Transaction integrity, audit architecture, regulatory compliance-driven architecture decisions, legacy system modernization
- Healthcare: HL7/FHIR integration architecture, PHI data flow design, interoperability standards, clinical system integration patterns
- E-commerce: Event-driven architecture for inventory, highly available checkout architecture, personalization infrastructure, content management architecture
- Manufacturing: OT/IT integration architecture, edge computing patterns, SCADA connectivity architecture

## Common Challenges
- Balancing architectural purity with practical delivery constraints and timelines
- Driving architecture change across autonomous teams without direct authority
- Communicating technical complexity and trade-offs to non-technical stakeholders
- Keeping documentation and ADRs current as systems evolve rapidly
- Preventing architecture drift as teams make independent decisions under pressure
- Managing cognitive load of maintaining broad system understanding across many services
