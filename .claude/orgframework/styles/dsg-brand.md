# dsg-brand

**Summary:** Owns visual brand identity, guidelines, and brand asset management, ensuring cohesive and compelling brand expression across all touchpoints.

**Level:** IC or Manager
**Reports to:** Brand / Design Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Define and maintain the visual brand identity, comprehensive brand guidelines, and standards documentation.
- Create and manage brand asset libraries including logos, color palettes, typography systems, and imagery style.
- Ensure brand consistency across all internal and external touchpoints from product to packaging to environmental.
- Partner with marketing and product teams on brand expression in campaigns, launches, and communications.
- Evolve the brand identity over time to stay relevant and competitive while maintaining recognition and equity.
- Review and approve external-facing materials for brand compliance and quality standards.
- Lead brand identity projects including rebrands, brand refreshes, and sub-brand development.
- Develop brand architecture frameworks for companies with multiple products, sub-brands, or acquired brands.
- Educate the organization on brand standards through workshops, documentation, and active governance.
- Conduct brand audits to assess consistency and identify areas for improvement across touchpoints.

## Core Competencies
- Brand identity development and management from strategy through visual execution.
- Visual design and art direction for creating and directing brand visual expression.
- Brand strategy and positioning understanding to translate brand purpose into visual language.
- Attention to detail and consistency for maintaining precise brand execution at scale.
- Cross-functional collaboration and influence for driving brand adoption across autonomous teams.
- Asset management and organizational skills for maintaining scalable brand systems.
- Typography expertise for brand type system definition including print, digital, and environmental applications.
- Color theory and color system management for consistent color reproduction across media.
- Brand governance and decision-making for making judgment calls on brand expression.
- Presentation and persuasion skills for selling brand decisions to leadership and stakeholders.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Brand consistency score measured through regular brand audits across touchpoints.
- Brand awareness metrics including aided and unaided recall in target markets.
- Brand guideline adoption rate by internal teams and external partners.
- Time-to-asset — speed of accessing and deploying brand materials from the asset library.
- Brand equity tracking through Net Promoter Score and brand health surveys.
- Brand compliance exception rate — number of materials requiring correction before approval.
- Brand sentiment analysis from social listening and customer feedback.

## Career Progression
Brand designers typically start as junior designers on brand teams (0-3 years), progress to brand designer (3-6 years), senior brand designer (6-9 years), and brand director or head of brand (9+ years). Growth involves moving from execution to strategy and governance. Senior brand designers lead major brand initiatives and mentor junior team members. Brand directors set brand strategy and partner with C-suite leadership on brand decisions. Some brand designers come from agency backgrounds managing multiple client brands. Transition paths include moving into creative direction, marketing leadership, or design operations. Brand leadership roles increasingly report to CMOs or creative officers in larger organizations. Experience with brand architecture for multi-brand portfolios is a key differentiator for senior roles.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
Brand designers balance governance, creation, and evolution work. Daily governance includes reviewing materials from across the company for brand compliance — a mix of approvals, design guidance, and corrections. Creative work encompasses new brand applications, campaign visual direction, and brand system expansions. Weekly brand team meetings review in-progress work, discuss governance challenges, and prioritize projects. Stakeholder meetings with marketing, product, and leadership align on brand direction and address emerging needs. Brand audit cycles involve quarterly touchpoint reviews and annual comprehensive brand health assessments. Rebrand or brand refresh initiatives become multi-month intensive projects involving research, strategy, creative exploration, and rollout planning. Brand training sessions and documentation updates ensure new team members and partners understand guidelines. External vendor management includes agencies, photographers, and production partners.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **Brand / Design Director** — Reports to; receives brand strategy direction and escalates governance decisions.
- **Marketing Team** — Primary internal client for campaign materials, brand applications, and creative direction.
- **Product Design Team** — Ensures product UI reflects brand identity within platform constraints.
- **Visual Designers** — Provides brand direction, reviews work, and guides visual execution quality.
- **Communications Team** — Coordinates on brand voice, messaging alignment, and PR materials.
- **Digital Marketing (Social, Email, Web)** — Ensures digital channels maintain brand consistency.
- **Sales Team** — Provides brand-compliant pitch decks, proposals, and sales collateral.
- **HR / Employer Brand** — Collaborates on employment brand expression in recruiting and internal materials.
- **Legal Team** — Coordinates on trademark usage, brand protection, and intellectual property matters.
- **External Agencies** — Manages agency relationships for campaigns, content production, and brand research.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Brand design is a well-established corporate function. Strong emphasis on brand strategy and equity measurement. Multicultural brand expression is increasingly important for diverse US audiences. Brand guidelines are comprehensive and extensively documented.
**EMEA:** Multi-language and multi-cultural brand expression is the norm. Brand guidelines must account for cultural visual differences across EU markets. Strong design heritage influences brand expectations (luxury in France, precision in Germany, minimalism in Scandinavia). Brand compliance with local regulations varies significantly.
**APAC:** Brand expression must navigate different color meanings, symbol interpretations, and visual preferences. China requires separate brand strategies and localized visual identity for the domestic market. Japan's brand culture values subtlety, quality, and heritage. India's diverse visual culture requires careful brand localization with sensitivity to regional differences.
**LATAM:** Brand expression often emphasizes warmth, family, and community values visually. Color palettes tend to be more vibrant than NA counterparts. Brand guidelines must account for varying literacy levels and visual communication preferences. Local brand authenticity is critical — foreign brands must adapt genuinely to local visual culture.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Brand must communicate innovation, reliability, and scalability. Developer-focused brands have distinct visual language from enterprise-focused brands. Brand evolution is faster given rapidly changing product landscapes. Rebrands and visual refreshes happen every 3-5 years to stay current.
**Fintech:** Brand must convey trust, security, and stability while appearing modern and innovative. Regulatory constraints limit some brand expression (comparisons, claims, testimonials). Brand colors and visual language are often more conservative. Brand architecture for regulated entities (banking, insurance, investment) requires clear separation.
**Healthcare:** Brand must balance professionalism/trust with approachability/warmth. Patient-facing vs. professional-facing brands may have different visual identities. Regulatory compliance (FDA advertising guidelines) constrains brand expression. Clinical credibility visual signals matter.
**E-commerce:** Brand is primarily expressed through digital channels and packaging. Fast brand iteration to keep pace with seasonal and trend cycles. Brand differentiation increasingly depends on visual distinctiveness in crowded marketplaces. Unboxing experience and packaging design are important brand touchpoints.

## Common Challenges
- Maintaining brand consistency across hundreds of touchpoints, channels, and stakeholders without being a bottleneck.
- Balancing the need for brand evolution with the risk of losing brand recognition and equity during changes.
- Getting buy-in for brand standards from autonomous teams that prioritize speed over compliance.
- Managing brand expression across acquired companies with established brand identities.
- Adapting brand identity for global markets while maintaining core brand recognition.
- Proving brand value and ROI to leadership focused on short-term marketing performance metrics.
- Keeping brand guidelines accessible and usable for non-designers who create brand-facing materials.
- Navigating the tension between brand differentiation and category visual conventions.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- Trademark law — registration, protection, and enforcement of brand marks and logos.
- Copyright law for brand-created visual and written content.
- Advertising standards regulations (FTC, ASA, CAP) for truthful and non-misleading brand communications.
- Comparative advertising regulations affecting brand claims about competitors.
- FDA and regulatory restrictions on brand claims in healthcare, pharma, and food industries.
- GDPR and privacy laws — brand data collection, brand tracking, and personalization compliance.
- Trade dress protection for distinctive brand visual elements.
- International brand registration (Madrid Protocol) for global trademark protection.
- Cultural and religious sensitivity guidelines for visual brand expression in global markets.
