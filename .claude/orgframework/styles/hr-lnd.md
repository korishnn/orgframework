# hr-lnd

**Summary:** Designs, delivers, and measures learning and development programs including technical training, soft skills development, leadership pipelines, and enterprise-wide learning strategies that build organizational capability.

**Level:** IC or Manager
**Reports to:** HR Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Design and deliver training programs across technical skills, soft skills, management fundamentals, and leadership development for all employee levels.
- Develop career development frameworks and competency models that define skill requirements for each role and level within the organization.
- Manage the learning management system (LMS) including content curation, user administration, reporting, and vendor relationship management.
- Partner with department heads and subject matter experts to identify skill gaps, define training needs, and prioritize learning initiatives.
- Assess training effectiveness through Level 1-4 Kirkpatrick evaluation, measuring learner satisfaction, knowledge retention, behavior change, and business impact.
- Build and maintain a content library of eLearning courses, facilitator guides, job aids, videos, and assessment tools.
- Design and manage leadership development programs including emerging leaders, first-time managers, and executive-level cohorts.
- Facilitate live training sessions both in-person and virtually, adapting delivery style to diverse audiences and learning preferences.
- Manage external training vendor relationships, including contract negotiation, quality assurance, and ROI measurement.
- Coordinate certification programs for employees requiring professional credentials (PMP, SHRM-CP, AWS, etc.) including exam preparation and reimbursement.
- Develop onboarding learning paths that accelerate new hire time-to-productivity in partnership with hr-onboarding.
- Support organizational change initiatives through change management training and communication strategies.
- Create and manage the annual learning calendar aligned with performance review cycles, promotion timing, and business priorities.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Training completion rate: percentage of assigned training courses completed on time
- Learner satisfaction score: average post-training survey rating (Kirkpatrick Level 1)
- Knowledge retention rate: assessment score improvement from pre- to post-training (Level 2)
- Behavior application rate: manager-reported application of skills after training (Level 3)
- Training ROI: business impact of training programs minus program cost (Level 4/5)
- Learning hours per employee: average annual training hours per FTE
- Leadership readiness pipeline: percentage of leadership roles filled by internal program graduates
- Compliance training completion rate: percentage of mandatory training completed by deadline

## Career Progression
- Entry level: L&D Coordinator managing LMS administration, training logistics, scheduling, and attendance tracking.
- Mid level: Instructional Designer developing eLearning content, facilitator guides, and assessment tools. Facilitator delivering live training sessions.
- Senior level: Senior L&D Specialist or Lead Instructional Designer owning full program design from needs analysis through evaluation for assigned business units.
- Lead level: L&D Program Manager managing multiple programs, vendor relationships, and the learning calendar for a region or function.
- Manager level: L&D Manager leading a team of instructional designers and facilitators, developing L&D strategy, and managing the learning budget.
- Director level: Director of Learning & Development owning enterprise-wide learning strategy, leadership development, and organizational capability building.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as coordinator, 2-3 years as designer/facilitator, 2-4 years as senior/lead, then management track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Check LMS for pending approvals, course completions, and support tickets. Respond to employee and manager training inquiries. Update training records.
- Midday: Facilitate live training sessions (virtual or in-person) or work on course development projects (storyboarding, script writing, content review with SMEs).
- Afternoon: Design and update eLearning modules in authoring tools, review vendor proposals, analyze training effectiveness data, meet with business partners on learning needs.
- Weekly: Program status meetings with business unit stakeholders, LMS reporting and data quality checks, facilitator team standup, learning calendar updates.
- Monthly: Training effectiveness analysis, vendor performance review, content refresh cycle, learning needs assessment interviews with department heads.
- Quarterly: Learning program review with HR leadership, certification cohort progress check, budget tracking and forecasting, learning calendar for next quarter.
- Rhythms: Program launches follow business rhythms (new manager training after promotion cycles, compliance training annually, onboarding weekly). Content development runs in project sprints. Facilitation is scheduled based on cohort calendars.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: High investment in corporate training budgets. Strong preference for digital and microlearning delivery. Leadership development is a top priority. Professional certification support (PMP, SHRM, HRCI, AWS) is common. Compliance training demands are significant.
- EMEA: Strong government-funded training programs in Germany (Aufstiegs-BAfG), UK (Apprenticeship Levy funds). Works council input on training programs in Germany, Netherlands. Apprenticeship and vocational training systems are well developed. Language training support is common for multilingual workforces.
- APAC: India has strong demand for technical certification training (IT, engineering). Australia's Skills First funding supports vocational training. Singapore SkillsFuture credits for employee development. Training culture in Japan emphasizes on-the-job training (OJT) and cross-functional rotation.
- LATAM: In-person training remains highly valued in Brazil and Mexico. Portuguese-language content availability is critical in Brazil. Training is often used as a retention incentive. Professional development budgets are more constrained compared to NA. Strong demand for English language training.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Product training is critical (internal and external). Continuous learning culture with dedicated learning time. Certification programs for customer-facing roles. Fast-paced content updates require agile development.
- Fintech: Extensive regulatory compliance training requirements. Anti-money laundering (AML), know-your-customer (KYC), and data privacy training mandatory. Ethics training for regulated roles.
- Healthcare: Mandatory continuing education credits (CME, CNE) for clinical staff. HIPAA training required for all patient-facing roles. Clinical skills simulation training. Accreditation-driven training requirements.
- Manufacturing: Safety training is the highest priority (OSHA, site-specific). Technical skills training for equipment operation. Lean and Six Sigma certification programs. Apprenticeship programs for trade skills.
- E-commerce: Warehouse safety training for fulfillment center staff. Rapid training deployment for seasonal workers. Pick/pack process training. Soft skills training for customer service teams.

## Common Challenges
- Demonstrating training ROI to skeptical business leaders who view L&D as a cost center rather than an investment.
- Balancing the demand for customized department-specific training with the efficiency of standardized enterprise programs.
- Keeping content current when business processes, products, and technologies change rapidly.
- Driving learning engagement and completion rates for voluntary (non-mandatory) development programs.
- Building effective leadership development when senior leaders are too busy to participate as facilitators or mentors.
- Adapting training delivery for a globally distributed workforce across time zones, languages, and cultural contexts.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: OSHA training requirements (safety, hazard communication), state-mandated harassment prevention training (CA, NY, IL, CT, DE), EEOC compliance training, FLSA for training time tracking, I-9 training requirements for HR staff.
- UK: Apprenticeship Levy utilization requirements, Health and Safety at Work Act training obligations, FCA training and competence requirements for financial services, Equality Act 2010 training compliance.
- EU/EEA: GDPR training for all employees handling personal data, EU Working Time Directive training on worker rights, country-specific vocational training board requirements (Germany IHK, France OPCO).
- Canada: Provincial occupational health and safety training requirements, AODA accessibility training in Ontario, Bill 168 workplace violence and harassment training in Ontario, provincial human rights code training.
- Australia: WHS (Work Health and Safety) training obligations, Fair Work Act training for managers, AFSL training/compliance requirements in financial services, state-based training levy requirements.
- Brazil: CLT training obligations for occupational safety (NR standards), LGPD data privacy training requirements, SESMT (Specialized Safety and Occupational Medicine) training requirements.
