# hr-relations

**Summary:** Manages workplace conflict resolution, conducts thorough investigations, advises on disciplinary actions, and ensures fair and consistent policy enforcement across the organization.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** HR Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Conduct impartial investigations into employee complaints including harassment, discrimination, retaliation, policy violations, and code of conduct breaches.
- Advise managers and HR business partners on employee relations matters including performance improvement plans, disciplinary actions, terminations, and restructuring.
- Develop, update, and maintain employee policies and the employee handbook to reflect current laws, regulations, and organizational values.
- Mediate workplace disputes between employees, teams, or managers, facilitating productive dialogue and mutually acceptable resolutions.
- Document investigation findings in detailed, legally defensible reports including witness statements, evidence analysis, credibility assessments, and recommended outcomes.
- Manage the performance improvement plan (PIP) process including draft review, manager coaching, and progress monitoring to ensure fair and consistent application.
- Provide guidance on reductions in force (RIF), layoffs, and reorganizations including selection criteria, WARN Act compliance, and severance arrangements.
- Track and analyze employee relations case data to identify trends, systemic issues, and preventive actions.
- Partner with legal counsel on high-risk investigations, complex terminations, and litigation-sensitive matters.
- Deliver training to managers and employees on workplace conduct, harassment prevention, conflict resolution, and company policies.
- Oversee the accommodation process under ADA and state leave laws, engaging in the interactive process with employees and their managers.
- Manage appeals and grievances processes, ensuring employees have fair channels to raise concerns without retaliation.
- Maintain confidential case files and investigation records in compliance with document retention policies and legal hold requirements.

## Core Competencies
- Workplace investigation techniques and best practices (Wicklander-Zulawski, AWI-CH certifications)
- Employment law knowledge across federal, state, and local jurisdictions
- Conflict mediation and de-escalation skills
- Policy drafting and interpretation
- Trauma-informed interviewing and empathy
- Documentation and report writing for legal defensibility
- Judgement and decision-making under ambiguity
- Manager coaching and advisory skills
- Cultural competence and DEI-informed practice
- Data analysis for case trend identification
- Discretion and confidentiality in sensitive matters
- Cross-cultural and global ER knowledge for multinational organizations

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Case resolution time: average days from complaint receipt to investigation closure
- Employee relations case volume: number of cases opened per quarter per 100 employees
- Litigation avoidance rate: percentage of cases resolved without legal escalation
- Manager satisfaction with ER guidance: post-case manager survey score
- Policy acknowledgment completion rate: percentage of employees who have signed current policies
- Investigation quality score: internal and external legal audit of investigation documentation
- Repeat issue rate: percentage of cases involving previously counseled employees or teams

## Career Progression
- Entry level: ER Specialist or Investigator handling low-to-medium risk cases independently, drafting policies, and supporting senior investigators on complex matters.
- Mid level: Senior ER Investigator managing complex investigations, advising business leaders, and coaching managers on performance management.
- Senior level: ER Manager leading a team of investigators, setting case standards, managing caseload allocation, and reporting ER trends to executive leadership.
- Director level: Director of Employee Relations owning the entire ER strategy, policy framework, risk management, and legal partnership relationships.
- VP level: VP of People / Chief Compliance Officer responsible for organizational culture, ethics, and workplace governance.
- Timeline: 2-3 years as specialist, 3-4 years as senior investigator, 2-3 years as manager, then director track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Review new intake of employee complaints and concerns received overnight, triage by severity and risk, assign cases to appropriate investigator.
- Midday: Conduct investigation interviews with complainants, witnesses, and respondents. Review relevant documents (emails, Slack messages, performance records, attendance logs).
- Afternoon: Draft investigation reports, update case management system, advise managers on disciplinary actions or PIPs, respond to manager ER questions.
- Weekly: Case review with legal counsel on high-risk matters, ER trend analysis and reporting, policy review and updates, team case load balancing.
- Monthly: ER metrics reporting to HR leadership, manager training sessions on workplace conduct, policy communication planning.
- Quarterly: Policy review cycle including handbook updates for legal changes, case data deep-dive for systemic trends, risk assessment of emerging issues.
- Rhythms: Casework is reactive to incoming complaints creating variable intensity. Proactive work (training, policy updates, data analysis) fills gaps between investigations. Compliance deadlines for policy distribution follow an annual calendar.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: At-will employment context but increasing restrictions on termination. States like CA, NY, MA have additional employee protections. Mandated harassment prevention training in CA, NY, IL, CT, DE. Non-disclosure agreement restrictions growing in sexual harassment cases. Paid sick leave laws impact attendance policies.
- EMEA: Constructive dismissal risk is higher. Employment protection is significantly stronger. Works council consultation required for disciplinary actions in Germany. France requires specific disciplinary procedure with union representation rights. Notice periods are longer (1-3 months). Probationary period management is critical.
- APAC: China's labor law heavily favors employees in disputes; termination without cause requires substantial severance. India's Industrial Disputes Act requires government permission for layoffs in certain establishments. Singapore is more employer-friendly but has strict tripartite guidelines. Japan's life-time employment norms affect performance management.
- LATAM: Brazil's labor courts are extremely active and employee-friendly; past employment relationships routinely challenged. Mexico's labor reform strengthened union powers and employee protections. Argentina requires administrative authorization for mass layoffs. Severance costs in LATAM are among the highest globally.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Remote work policies create unique ER challenges (jurisdiction disputes, work-from-home investigations). Tech culture clashes with traditional hierarchy. Severance packages often include equity acceleration terms.
- Fintech: Regulatory compliance failures create dual-track investigations (internal + regulatory). Licensing implications for regulated roles under investigation. Strict background check obligation if violations found.
- Healthcare: Union relationships heavily influence ER processes. Mandatory reporting obligations to licensing boards for clinical misconduct. Patient safety creates expedited investigation timelines.
- Manufacturing: Union grievance procedures run parallel to internal ER processes. Workplace safety investigations require OSHA coordination. Seniority-based policies create tension with performance-based decisions.
- E-commerce: High-volume hourly workforce requires scalable ER processes. Attendance and punctuality issues dominate case volume. Temporary worker ER handled by staffing agency creates coordination complexity.

## Common Challenges
- Balancing thorough investigation with timely resolution when employees are waiting for outcomes and morale is affected.
- Maintaining impartiality and perceived fairness when investigating complaints involving senior leadership or high-performing employees.
- Navigating increasing state and local employment law complexity requiring region-specific policies.
- Managing the emotional toll of conducting difficult investigations and delivering termination decisions.
- Addressing retaliation concerns where employees fear reporting even with anti-retaliation policies in place.
- Handling investigations remotely when participants are geographically distributed and video interviews are less effective.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: Title VII of Civil Rights Act (discrimination/harassment), ADEA (age discrimination), ADA (disability, accommodations), FMLA (protected leave), state-specific paid family leave, NLRA (Section 7 protected activity, union contexts), WARN Act (mass layoff notice), state mini-WARN acts, state non-compete and non-disclosure restrictions, Sarbanes-Oxley whistleblower protections.
- UK: Employment Rights Act 1996 (unfair dismissal), Equality Act 2010 (discrimination), ACAS Code of Practice on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures, Employment Tribunals procedure.
- EU/EEA: EU Employment Equality Directives, Works Council Directive, General Data Protection Regulation (employee monitoring and investigation data), country-specific labor codes and notice requirements.
- Canada: Canadian Human Rights Act, provincial employment standards codes, provincial human rights codes, Occupational Health and Safety Acts (workplace violence and harassment provisions).
- Brazil: CLT (labor code), LGPD (data protection for investigation evidence), constitutional protections against unjust dismissal, Labor Court procedures for dispute resolution.
