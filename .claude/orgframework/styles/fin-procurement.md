# fin-procurement

**Summary:** Develops and executes sourcing strategies, negotiates contracts, manages vendor risk, and oversees the end-to-end procurement lifecycle to optimize cost, quality, and service delivery.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** CFO / COO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Develop and execute strategic sourcing strategies for goods and services across all categories including IT, professional services, marketing, facilities, and operations.
- Lead contract negotiations with vendors and suppliers including pricing, terms, service levels, warranties, and intellectual property provisions.
- Manage the full procurement lifecycle from requisition intake, through sourcing and negotiation, to purchase order issuance and contract execution.
- Conduct vendor risk assessments including financial stability, data security, compliance, and operational resilience before onboarding new suppliers.
- Maintain vendor master database ensuring accurate records of vendor information, contracts, pricing, and performance history.
- Monitor and manage spend categories, identifying cost reduction opportunities and driving total cost of ownership (TCO) optimization.
- Develop and enforce procurement policies and procedures to ensure compliance with internal controls and regulatory requirements.
- Lead the RFP and RFQ process including requirements gathering, vendor identification, bid evaluation, and vendor selection recommendations.
- Manage purchase order approval workflows ensuring appropriate authorization levels and budget alignment before commitment.
- Track and report on procurement KPIs including cost savings, supplier performance, procurement cycle time, and spend under management.
- Implement and administer procurement technology including e-procurement, sourcing platforms, and contract management systems.
- Partner with legal on contract terms, risk allocation, and dispute resolution for vendor agreements.
- Manage tail spend and maverick spend reduction through policy compliance, preferred vendor programs, and procurement card optimization.
- Support M&A integration by evaluating supplier contracts, consolidating vendor relationships, and harmonizing procurement processes.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Cost savings achieved: percentage reduction in spend year-over-year for managed categories
- Spend under management (SUM): percentage of total company spend managed by procurement
- Maverick spend rate: percentage of spend outside of preferred vendors and contracts
- Procurement cycle time: average days from requisition to purchase order issuance
- Supplier performance score: average score from quarterly supplier business reviews
- Contract compliance rate: percentage of spend covered by active contracts
- Supplier diversity spend: percentage of spend with diverse suppliers
- Procurement operating cost: procurement department cost as percentage of managed spend

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Procurement Coordinator or Buyer processing purchase orders, managing vendor records, and supporting RFP logistics.
- Mid level: Procurement Specialist or Senior Buyer managing sourcing projects, negotiating contracts, and leading RFP processes for assigned categories.
- Senior level: Category Manager or Senior Procurement Manager owning strategic sourcing for a category (IT, marketing, professional services) and managing supplier relationships.
- Lead level: Procurement Manager or Lead Category Manager overseeing a procurement team, managing the procurement calendar, and developing sourcing strategy.
- Director level: Director of Procurement or Head of Strategic Sourcing owning the entire procurement function, spend strategy, and vendor governance.
- VP level: CPO (Chief Procurement Officer) responsible for enterprise-wide procurement strategy, supplier ecosystem, and third-party risk management.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as coordinator/buyer, 2-3 years as specialist, 3-4 years as category manager, then director/CPO track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Review and approve purchase order requests in the procurement system, respond to urgent requisition inquiries, check contract expirations and renewals coming due.
- Midday: Vendor calls and negotiations, supplier performance review meetings, stakeholder meetings on sourcing needs, RFP evaluation team meetings.
- Afternoon: Contract review and negotiation, spend analysis, supplier research, RFP preparation, vendor risk assessment reviews, procurement policy guidance.
- Weekly: Procurement team standup, supplier review meetings, stakeholder pipeline review, new vendor onboarding approval, contract negotiation status check.
- Monthly: Spend reporting and category analysis, supplier performance scorecard updates, purchase order aging review, procurement card statement review and approval.
- Quarterly: Quarterly business reviews with key suppliers, category strategy reviews, supplier risk reassessment, savings tracking and reporting.
- Annual: Annual supplier evaluation program, strategic sourcing plan development, budget cycle input, supplier diversity program reporting.
- Rhythms: Steady flow of requisitions creates ongoing operational work. Strategic sourcing projects have defined timelines (4-12 weeks for RFPs). Contract renewals create predictable seasonal peaks. Quarterly supplier reviews create periodic reporting cycles.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: Contract law is governed by UCC (Uniform Commercial Code) with state variations. At-will provisions are standard. Sarbanes-Oxley requires controls over procurement. Supplier diversity programs (minority, women-owned businesses) are a significant focus. NASPO and cooperative purchasing are common in public sector.
- EMEA: Strong emphasis on GDPR compliance in all vendor agreements dealing with personal data. German law requires specific procurement procedures for works council involvement. French law imposes payment term requirements (60 days max). VAT treatment of cross-border procurement creates complexity. EU procurement directives govern public sector contracts.
- APAC: China's PRC Contract Law and Procurement Law regulate domestic procurement. Relationships (guanxi) heavily influence vendor selection. India's GST has simplified but still complex cross-state procurement. Japan's keiretsu business relationships affect vendor selection. Anti-bribery compliance (FCPA, UK Bribery Act) is critical for APAC procurement.
- LATAM: Brazil's complex tax system requires specific clauses in contracts regarding tax responsibility. Mexico's procurement law includes specific requirements for government contracts. Relationship-based business culture means personal relationships drive vendor selection. Import tariffs and customs significantly affect international procurement costs.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Primary spend on cloud infrastructure (AWS, Azure, GCP) and SaaS tools. Software licensing and subscription management is a core category. Headcount-driven procurement for equipment and collaboration tools. Professional services procurement for consultants and contractors.
- Fintech: Strict vendor due diligence requirements for financial services (outsourcing regulation). Critical vendor identification for regulated services. Data security requirements in all vendor contracts (bank-grade). Compliance and regulatory vendor spend is significant.
- Healthcare: Supplier quality requirements and FDA compliance in procurement. Group purchasing organizations (GPOs) are dominant in hospital procurement. Medical device and pharmaceutical procurement has specific regulatory requirements. HIPAA business associate agreements required for all vendors with patient data access.
- Manufacturing: Raw material sourcing and commodity price risk management. Capital equipment procurement is a major category. Logistics and freight procurement are critical. Global sourcing requires complex international logistics. Quality certifications (ISO 9001) are vendor requirements.
- E-commerce: Warehousing and fulfillment procurement is a major category. Seasonal procurement planning for peak periods. Packaging and shipping supplies procurement. Last-mile delivery carrier procurement and management. Temporary labor procurement for seasonal hiring.

## Common Challenges
- Managing stakeholder demand for speed and flexibility while maintaining procurement compliance and competitive pricing.
- Measuring and demonstrating procurement's value beyond cost savings (risk reduction, quality improvement, innovation).
- Ensuring supplier diversity without compromising on cost, quality, or delivery performance.
- Navigating global supply chain disruptions and maintaining supplier continuity during crises.
- Managing third-party risk when suppliers have access to sensitive systems and data.
- Balancing standardization and consolidation benefits with business unit desire for specialized vendors.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: SOX procurement controls, UCC Article 2 (sale of goods), FAR for government contractors, OFCCP supplier diversity requirements for federal contractors, state-specific procurement transparency laws, Trade Agreements Act (TAA) for government contracts.
- UK: Public Contracts Regulations 2015, Bribery Act 2010 supplier due diligence, Modern Slavery Act transparency in supply chains (Section 54), UK GDPR supplier data processing requirements.
- EU: EU Procurement Directives, GDPR vendor data processing agreements, EU conflicts minerals regulation, EU Forced Labour Regulation supply chain due diligence, country-specific implementation of procurement directives, EU dual-use regulation for controlled goods.
- Canada: Canadian Free Trade Agreement (CFTA) procurement obligations, Integrity Regime for government suppliers, Accessible Canada Act (vendor accessibility requirements), provincial procurement transparency obligations.
- Brazil: Lei 14.133/2021 (new Procurement Law for public sector), LGPD data protection in vendor agreements, INMETRO certification requirements for certain products, Central Bank outsourcing regulations for financial services.
- Australia: Commonwealth Procurement Rules for government, Modern Slavery Act 2018 reporting requirements, Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (supplier conduct), Privacy Act 1988 vendor data handling, state-based procurement frameworks.
