# lgl-ip

**Summary:** Intellectual Property Counsel responsible for managing the organization's patent, trademark, copyright, and trade secret portfolio, including prosecution, enforcement, and strategic counseling.

**Level:** IC (Attorney)
**Reports to:** General Counsel / Associate General Counsel
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Manage global patent prosecution strategy including drafting, filing, and prosecuting patent applications across multiple jurisdictions
- Oversee trademark portfolio management including clearance searches, registration filings, maintenance, and enforcement against infringement
- Develop and implement comprehensive IP protection strategies covering patents, trademarks, copyrights, trade secrets, and domain names
- Conduct patent and trademark clearance searches and render freedom-to-operate opinions for product and engineering teams
- Advise on IP portfolio development including make-vs-buy decisions, patent landscaping, and competitive intelligence
- Draft and negotiate IP licensing agreements, technology transfer agreements, joint development agreements, and material transfer agreements
- Handle IP enforcement actions including cease-and-desist letters, takedown notices (DMCA), and opposition proceedings
- Manage IP litigation and disputes in coordination with outside counsel, including patent assertion and defense
- Establish and maintain trade secret protection programs including access controls, confidentiality agreements, and exit procedures
- Educate engineering and product teams on IP best practices, invention disclosure processes, and prior art awareness
- Conduct IP due diligence for mergers, acquisitions, and investment transactions
- Monitor competitive IP landscapes and maintain an IP watch program for relevant technology domains

## Core Competencies
- Deep expertise in patent law (USPTO practice, PCT filings, foreign prosecution) and trademark law (TTAB, Madrid Protocol)
- IP portfolio management and strategic asset development across multiple technology domains
- Strategic IP counseling with ability to align IP strategy with business goals and product roadmaps
- Technical literacy sufficient to understand engineering inventions, software architectures, and complex product features
- Written and oral advocacy skills for patent prosecution, trademark opposition, and enforcement communications
- Cross-functional collaboration with engineering, product, and design teams on invention harvesting
- IP licensing negotiation experience including royalty structures, field-of-use restrictions, and grant-back provisions
- Due diligence and transactional IP support for M&A and financing events
- Knowledge of open-source licensing models, copyleft obligations, and license compatibility
- Global IP law awareness including jurisdictional differences in patentability, enforcement, and grace periods

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Number of patents filed, allowed, and granted per year across jurisdictions
- Trademark registration success rate and time to registration
- IP licensing revenue generated or cost savings from avoidance of third-party IP
- Patent prosecution timeline — average time from filing to allowance
- Number of enforceable IP assets relative to industry peers and competitive position
- IP dispute resolution outcomes including favorable settlements and enforcement wins
- Open source compliance incidents and their resolution time
- Time-to-response for freedom-to-operate requests from product teams

## Career Progression
- **Junior IP Associate / Patent Agent (0-3 years):** Drafts and prosecutes patent applications under supervision. Conducts prior art searches. Learns trademark prosecution. Typically holds a technical degree and USPTO registration (patent bar).
- **IP Counsel (3-7 years):** Independently manages a patent and trademark docket. Handles freedom-to-operate analyses. Drafts IP licensing agreements. Begins developing portfolio strategy recommendations.
- **Senior IP Counsel (7-12 years):** Leads IP strategy for major product lines. Manages complex licensing negotiations. Handles IP disputes and coordinates with outside litigation counsel. May supervise junior attorneys and agents.
- **Director of IP (12-15 years):** Owns the entire IP portfolio strategy. Manages IP budget including prosecution, maintenance, and enforcement costs. Serves on product leadership team. Provides IP input to corporate strategy.
- **VP / Associate General Counsel - IP (15+ years):** Executive-level ownership of IP function. Sets global IP policy. Advises C-suite and board on IP strategy, risk, and value creation. Manages external counsel relationships budget.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning review of patent and trademark docket deadlines, prioritize filings by statutory bar dates and convention priorities
- Invention disclosure review sessions with engineering teams — typically weekly or biweekly harvesting meetings
- Patent application drafting sessions alternating with prior art search and landscape analysis
- Trademark clearance requests from brand and marketing teams for new product names, features, and campaigns
- IP licensing agreement drafting and negotiation calls with business development partners
- Weekly coordination with outside counsel on active prosecution and enforcement matters
- Monthly competitive landscape report reviewing newly published patents and trademark filings by competitors
- Quarterly IP portfolio review with executive leadership — budget status, filing targets, enforcement opportunities
- Regular open-source compliance reviews with engineering on new third-party library usage and license obligations
- Communication rhythm includes regular written updates to stakeholders and scheduled invention disclosure meetings

## Regional Nuances
- **NA:** Strong patent enforcement environment with well-established USPTO procedures and district court litigation. Software and business method patents are patent-eligible but face Alice/Mayo challenges. Trademark is first-to-use jurisdiction. IP litigation costs are the highest globally. Compensation range $180K-$350K for experienced counsel.
- **EMEA:** European Patent Office (EPO) with centralized examination but national validation. Software patentability is more restrictive than the US. Unitary Patent and Unified Patent Court (UPC) system is now operational. Trademark via EUIPO for EU-wide protection. Employee inventor compensation laws in Germany and UK. Compensation lower than NA but strong social benefits.
- **APAC:** Highly variable — China has become a major patent filing jurisdiction with rapid examination options and growing enforcement. Japan and Korea have sophisticated IP systems with heavy filing cultures. India has stricter patentability criteria (Section 3(k) restricts software patents). Counterfeiting enforcement varies dramatically. First-to-file for trademarks everywhere except Philippines.
- **LATAM:** Brazil has a large and active patent office (INPI) but significant backlog. Patent prosecution is slow (5-10 years not uncommon) unless expedited. Argentina and Mexico have improving but less predictable systems. Trademark counterfeiting is a significant concern in certain markets. Patent linkage for pharmaceuticals is recognized in some countries. Cost-sensitive prosecution strategies are common.

## Industry Variations
- **SaaS:** Focus on software and business method patents, UI/UX design patents, and trade secret protection for algorithms. Open-source compliance is a major concern. Patent portfolio used more defensively. Copyright protection for code base infrastructure.
- **Fintech:** Heavy focus on business method patents, particularly around payments, fraud detection, and blockchain. Regulatory interaction with IP — standard-essential patents in payment systems. Trade secrets critical for proprietary algorithms and risk models.
- **Healthcare/Biotech:** Patent term extensions and regulatory exclusivity periods (Hatch-Waxman, BPCIA). Complex claims around methods of treatment, diagnostics, and biologics. Heavy reliance on patent protection for product market exclusivity. Regulatory data protection for clinical trial data.
- **Manufacturing:** Combination of utility and design patents for physical products. Process patents for manufacturing methods. Trade secrets for proprietary manufacturing processes. Patent marking and packaging compliance.
- **E-commerce:** Design patents for UI elements and product displays. Business method patents for recommendation engines, logistics algorithms, and marketplace mechanics. Trademark enforcement against counterfeit sellers and domain squatters.

## Common Challenges
- Keeping up with rapidly evolving patent eligibility law (software, AI, and business method patents)
- Managing global IP costs against budget constraints while maintaining adequate protection in key markets
- Balancing patent filing breadth against disclosure concerns in competitive technology areas
- Enforcing IP rights in jurisdictions with weak or unpredictable legal systems
- Educating engineering teams to consistently submit invention disclosures without disrupting their workflow
- Navigating open-source license compatibility and avoiding inadvertent copyleft obligations in proprietary products

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US Patent Act (35 U.S.C.) and America Invents Act (AIA) — first-inventor-to-file provisions
- European Patent Convention (EPC) and Unitary Patent / UPC regulations
- Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) for international filings
- Paris Convention for priority claims across member countries
- Madrid Protocol for international trademark registration
- Berne Convention for copyright protection
- Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) for takedown notices and safe harbor
- TRIPS Agreement (WTO) minimum IP protection standards
- Bayh-Dole Act for inventions arising from federal funding (US)
- EU Trade Secrets Directive for trade secret protection frameworks
- Defend Trade Secrets Act (US) for federal trade secret civil claims
- Plant Variety Protection Act and UPOV Convention (if applicable)
- Anti-counterfeiting Trade Agreement (ACTA) enforcement provisions
- USPTO Patent Term Adjustment and Extension regulations
