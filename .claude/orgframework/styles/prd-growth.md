# prd-growth

**Summary:** Growth Product Manager responsible for driving product-led growth through experimentation across the full user lifecycle — acquisition, activation, retention, referral, and revenue expansion.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** Director of Product / VP Growth
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Define and execute the product-led growth (PLG) strategy, establishing a structured experimentation roadmap aligned with growth targets
- Design, prioritize, and run growth experiments across the full AARRR funnel — acquisition, activation, retention, referral, and revenue (monetization)
- Analyze user funnels end-to-end to identify drop-off points, friction areas, and conversion improvement opportunities with the highest potential impact
- Partner with marketing, engineering, data science, and design teams to implement growth initiatives spanning product, channel, and pricing changes
- Optimize onboarding flows to improve time-to-first-value, activation rates, and early user engagement
- Develop, monitor, and own growth metrics including North Star metric, LTV/CAC ratio, activation rate, retention curves, and referral viral coefficient
- Build and evolve shared growth infrastructure including feature flag systems, experimentation platforms, and user segmentation frameworks
- Drive viral loop design, referral program optimization, and network effect amplification through product mechanics
- Conduct growth experiments on pricing and packaging in partnership with the pricing team — free trial, freemium, self-serve conversion optimization
- Perform competitive growth analysis — benchmarking funnels, activation flows, and monetization models against market best practices
- Manage the growth experimentation backlog — scoring and prioritizing experiments based on ICE or PXL frameworks
- Communicate growth results, learnings, and strategy to executive leadership with clear link to business impact

## Core Competencies
- Growth experimentation and hypothesis testing — structured experiment design, test prioritization, and rigorous result analysis
- Funnel analysis and conversion optimization — identifying and removing friction points across the user journey
- Product-led growth strategy — PLG motion design, freemium/free trial mechanics, self-serve conversion optimization
- Cross-functional collaboration with marketing, engineering, data science, and design for integrated growth initiatives
- Data analysis and metric frameworks — cohort analysis, retention curves, funnel metrics, LTV modeling
- UX and behavioral psychology principles — understanding user motivation, ability, and triggers (BJ Fogg behavior model)
- Viral and referral mechanics — viral coefficient calculation, K-factor optimization, network effect design
- Monetization and pricing experimentation — willingness-to-pay analysis, price anchoring, tier structure optimization
- Technical understanding of growth infrastructure — feature flags, experimentation platforms, tracking instrumentation
- SQL and product analytics tool proficiency for self-serve data analysis
- Growth channel knowledge — understanding how product and channel interact (SEO, SEM, social, email, partnerships)
- Statistical literacy for experiment interpretation and avoiding common testing pitfalls

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Activation rate — percentage of new users reaching the aha moment within defined time window
- Free-to-paid conversion rate — trial or freemium to paid subscription conversion
- LTV/CAC ratio and payback period for growth-acquired users
- Retention curves — day 7, day 30, day 90 retention rates and cohort trends
- Referral viral coefficient (K-factor) and referral-generated revenue
- North Star metric movement attributable to growth experiments
- Experiment velocity — number of growth tests launched per month and percentage producing statistically significant results
- Net revenue retention and expansion MRR from growth initiatives

## Career Progression
- **Growth Product Manager / Associate (0-2 years):** Runs growth experiments under supervision. Analyzes funnel data. Supports growth initiatives with analysis and execution. Learns experimentation methodology. Often starts in marketing, analytics, or PM before specializing in growth.
- **Growth PM / Growth Manager (2-5 years):** Independently owns the growth experimentation roadmap. Designs and runs experiments across the funnel. Partners with marketing and engineering on growth initiatives. Leads growth for a product area or growth lever.
- **Senior Growth PM (5-8 years):** Owns growth strategy for a significant product area or business line. Leads complex, multi-workstream growth initiatives. Builds growth infrastructure. Mentors junior growth PMs. Deep expertise in PLG mechanics and experimentation.
- **Director of Growth / Head of Growth (8-12 years):** Leads the growth organization. Sets enterprise growth strategy. Manages a team of growth PMs, engineers, and data scientists. Owns growth OKRs and results. Partners with C-suite on growth strategy.
- **VP Growth / Chief Growth Officer (12+ years):** Executive-level ownership of all growth functions including product, marketing, and revenue. Sets overall growth strategy and investment thesis. Board-level reporting on growth metrics. Ultimate accountability for revenue growth.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Start with growth metrics review — check the growth dashboard for North Star metric movement, funnel trends, and experiment results
- Experiment results analysis — review tests that have reached significance, extract learnings, decide on rollout, iterate, or kill
- Ideation and hypothesis generation — develop new experiment ideas based on data analysis, user research, competitive insights, and behavioral frameworks
- Experiment design and prioritization — write experiment specs, score using ICE/PXL framework, build and maintain the growth backlog
- Cross-functional experiment execution — coordinate with engineering on implementation, design on creative, data on measurement
- Funnel deep-dives — analyze specific funnel stages for friction, drop-off, and improvement opportunities
- Growth team stand-up — align on experiment status, blockers, and upcoming launches
- Weekly experiment review — review active experiments, prioritize new tests, discuss learnings
- Quarterly growth planning — define growth strategy, set OKRs, allocate resources to growth levers
- Competitive growth analysis — monitor competitor onboarding, pricing, and viral mechanics
- Communication rhythm is fast-paced and data-rich with emphasis on rapid iteration and learning velocity

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **Product (prd-manager, prd-owner):** Integration of growth initiatives with core product development. Feature prioritization balancing growth and product goals. Growth experiments touch core product flows.
- **Marketing (mkt-growth, mkt-email, mkt-ppc):] Tight partnership on channel-to-product handoff, campaign-meets-product experience, email lifecycle integration with product behavior, paid acquisition funnel optimization
- **Engineering (eng-frontend, eng-backend, eng-platform):] Growth experiment implementation, feature flag infrastructure, experimentation platform development, tracking instrumentation for growth metrics
- **Data Science (da-scientist, da-analytics):** Advanced experiment design, causal inference studies, LTV modeling, churn prediction, personalization algorithms
- **Design (dsg-ux, dsg-ui):** Conversion-focused design, onboarding flow design, experiment creative development, behavioral design patterns
- **Customer Success (cs-success):** Customer health data for retention and expansion analysis, at-risk customer identification, growth opportunities in existing customer base
- **Pricing (prd-pricing, fin-fpa):] Pricing experiment design, packaging optimization for conversion, willingness-to-pay research coordination
- **Sales (sls-ae, sls-bdr):] PLG-to-sales handoff optimization, lead scoring from product behavior, sales outreach timing based on product engagement
- **Customer Support (cs-tier1, cs-docs):] Support friction as growth opportunity — self-service improvements reduce churn, onboarding support optimization
- **Legal (lgl-privacy):** Privacy compliance for growth experiments, consent requirements for behavioral tracking, compliance for referral program mechanics

## Regional Nuances
- **NA:** PLG is a mature and well-established model with sophisticated experimentation culture. Growth roles are highly valued with significant compensation ($1400K-$2500K+). Strong emphasis on data rigor and statistical methodology. Reforge and similar growth education programs are standard.
- **EMEA:** PLG is growing with strong adoption in SaaS hubs (London, Berlin, Amsterdam). Privacy regulations (GDPR) significantly constrain growth experiments — tracking limitations, consent requirements, and data minimization affect funnel analysis and personalization. Growth roles less established than NA.
- **APAC:** Mobile-first PLG is dominant in many markets (India, Southeast Asia). WhatsApp and other messaging-based growth mechanics are common. Social commerce and referral-based growth are powerful. Lower tolerance for email-heavy growth strategies. Fast growth cadence in high-growth markets.
- **LATAM:** PLG is emerging with growing experimentation culture in Brazil and Mexico. Mobile-dominant markets require mobile-first growth strategies. Social media and WhatsApp-based growth channels are powerful. Lower disposable income affects freemium and pricing strategies. Cash-based and local payment methods affect monetization growth.

## Industry Variations
- **SaaS:** PLG native — free trials, freemium models, self-serve conversion, product-qualified leads (PQLs), in-app upsells, expansion revenue through feature adoption. Growth infrastructure is critical. Experimentation is deeply embedded.
- **Fintech:** Growth is constrained by regulation — compliance affects onboarding flow speed, KYC requirements create friction, licensing limits acquisition channels. Trust is the primary growth driver. Referral programs are powerful due to trust mechanics. Conversion optimization focuses on reducing verification friction.
- **Healthcare:** Long decision cycles constrain growth velocity. Clinical validation and trust are growth drivers. Provider adoption requires different growth mechanics than consumer. Regulatory constraints limit growth experimentation in patient-facing products.
- **E-commerce:** Growth is high-velocity with aggressive experimentation. Free shipping, discounts, and promotions are primary growth levers. Abandoned cart recovery, email/SMS lifecycle, and personalization are critical growth engines. Seasonal growth planning around shopping peaks. Mobile commerce optimization is central.
- **Manufacturing/IoT:** Growth cycles are longer with B2B buying processes. Product-led growth is less established. Trial and demo models exist for software components. Installation base expansion and ecosystem lock-in are growth strategies.

## Common Challenges
- Disentangling growth experiment effects from external factors — seasonality, marketing campaigns, and competitive activity
- Achieving statistical significance fast enough in low-traffic products or narrow segments
- Avoiding growth metric myopia — optimizing short-term metrics (conversion) at the expense of long-term health (retention, satisfaction)
- Balancing growth initiatives with core product experience — growth tactics should not degrade the user experience
- Maintaining experiment velocity while ensuring engineering support for growth infrastructure and instrumentation
- Navigating privacy regulations that restrict behavioral tracking and personalization — GDPR, CCPA, cookie consent
- Aligning the organization around a North Star metric when different teams have conflicting priorities
- Managing growth experiment fatigue for users who are frequently exposed to tests

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- GDPR data protection and consent requirements for growth experiment tracking and personalization
- CCPA/CPRA data subject rights — growth data collection must support deletion and opt-out
- ePrivacy Directive and cookie consent regulations for web-based growth experiments and tracking
- CAN-SPAM Act and CASL (Canada Anti-Spam Law) for growth-focused email campaigns
- COPPA compliance if growth experiments involve users under 13
- FTC truth-in-advertising standards for growth claims and promotional messaging
- State automatic renewal laws for free trial to paid conversion flows
- AI Act compliance for growth experiments involving automated decision-making or personalization
- Financial regulations for fintech growth — fair lending, truth-in-savings, disclosure requirements
- Professional regulation for health-related growth claims (HIPAA, FDA)
- Data retention policies for growth experiment data
- Accessibility compliance (ADA, WCAG) for growth flows including onboarding and conversion
