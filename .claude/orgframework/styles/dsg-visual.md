# dsg-visual

**Summary:** Produces illustrations, iconography, marketing graphics, and visual assets that communicate brand identity and support product and marketing objectives.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** Design Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Create original illustrations, icons, and graphic assets for use across product UI, marketing, and communications.
- Develop visual narratives, infographics, and data visualizations to communicate complex ideas graphically.
- Produce marketing collateral including social media graphics, digital ads, email banners, and presentation materials.
- Maintain visual consistency across diverse media including digital, print, environmental, and motion formats.
- Collaborate with brand and marketing teams on campaign visuals, seasonal promotions, and launch assets.
- Manage and organize visual asset libraries for reuse, scalability, and team accessibility.
- Create custom typography and lettering for specific brand applications.
- Execute photo retouching, image compositing, and digital asset production.
- Present visual concepts to stakeholders and iterate based on creative direction feedback.
- Maintain awareness of visual design trends, illustration styles, and cultural visual language evolution.

## Core Competencies
- Illustration and iconography across multiple styles (flat, isometric, 3D, hand-drawn, line art).
- Graphic design and visual communication for both digital and print media.
- Typography and composition for creating visually balanced and hierarchically clear layouts.
- Digital and print production with understanding of file formats, color spaces, and output specifications.
- Brand visual language adherence for maintaining consistent visual expression across applications.
- Creative problem-solving and concept development from brief to final execution.
- Photo editing, retouching, and image manipulation proficiency.
- Layout design and grid systems for creating structured, professional visual compositions.
- Cultural visual literacy for creating globally appropriate visual content.
- Critique and feedback skills for giving and receiving constructive design direction.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Asset production velocity — number of visual assets delivered per sprint or week.
- Reuse rate of visual assets across campaigns and channels (reducing redundant work).
- Stakeholder satisfaction score from post-project creative reviews.
- Brand consistency score measured through visual audits of marketing and product materials.
- Campaign performance lift attributed to visual creative testing (A/B test results).
- Asset library organization and usability measured by team search and find rates.
- Time from creative brief to final approved asset.

## Career Progression
Visual designers typically begin as junior graphic designers (0-2 years), advance to mid-level (2-5 years), senior (5-8 years), and art director or principal levels (8+ years). Growth involves expanding from execution to creative direction and strategy. Senior visual designers lead campaigns and manage complex multi-asset projects. Art directors set visual direction for brands and campaigns. Some visual designers transition into UI design, brand design, or creative direction. Others specialize in areas like illustration, typography, or information design. Portfolio quality and creative range are primary advancement factors. Freelance and agency experience is common in this career path, with many visual designers moving between in-house and agency roles.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A visual designer's day is hands-on and production-oriented. Mornings start with checking creative briefs, reviewing feedback from stakeholders, and planning the day's design work. Core production time is spent creating illustrations, compositing assets, or refining layouts. Feedback reviews happen mid-day with creative directors or marketing stakeholders. Revisions and iterations follow feedback cycles — typically 2-3 rounds before final approval. Afternoon time includes asset exports, file organization, and updating asset libraries. Campaign launch periods involve higher volume and faster turnaround. Regular check-ins with marketing and brand teams ensure visual work is on-brief and on-brand. Weekly creative reviews build team skills and maintain quality standards. Project management time includes updating status boards, organizing files, and documenting design decisions.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **Brand Designer** — Ensures visual assets are consistent with brand identity, guidelines, and evolution.
- **Marketing Manager** — Receives creative briefs and requirements for campaign assets and collateral.
- **Copywriter / Content Strategist** — Collaborates on visual-content integration for campaigns and communications.
- **UI Designer** — Produces icons, illustrations, and visual elements for product interfaces.
- **Motion Designer** — Provides static design files and storyboard frames for animation production.
- **Art Director** — Receives creative direction and feedback on visual quality and brand alignment.
- **Social Media Manager** — Creates platform-optimized visuals for social channels.
- **Growth Marketer** — Produces A/B test creative variants for ad campaigns and landing pages.
- **Product Manager** — Provides briefs for product marketing assets and feature announcement graphics.
- **External Vendors** — Coordinates with photographers, illustrators, and production partners for outsourced work.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Large in-house creative teams with specialized roles. High compensation for senior visual designers. Strong emphasis on portfolio and personal brand. Agency-to-in-house career flow is common. Diverse visual styles reflecting multicultural audiences.
**EMEA:** Strong graphic design heritage (Swiss, Scandinavian, German design traditions). Typography expertise is highly valued given multi-language requirements. Lower salaries than NA but strong creative culture and design appreciation. Government and public sector design opportunities are more prevalent.
**APAC:** Visual designers in China often specialize in e-commerce visuals (Tmall, JD.com banners) and WeChat ecosystem content. Japanese visual design emphasizes detail, aesthetics, and brand consistency. India has a large design talent pool serving global markets through studios. Color cultural meanings vary significantly across APAC markets, requiring localization expertise.
**LATAM:** Vibrant, colorful visual design traditions influence brand expression. Many visual designers work as freelancers through global platforms. Emerging market brands increasingly invest in professional visual identity. Art schools and design education are growing, producing new talent. Lower rates than NA but growing demand.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Visual assets focus on product screenshots, UI illustrations, and technical concept visualization. Developer-focused content requires clean, precise visual communication. Infographics for product comparisons and data-driven marketing. Complex B2B concepts need clear visual explanation.
**Fintech:** Trust and security visual signals are essential. Compliance-regulated visual design with legal review requirements. Financial data visualizations and infographics for investor communications. Conservative, professional visual style emphasizing stability and credibility.
**Healthcare:** Medical illustration skills are valuable for anatomical and procedural visualization. Patient-facing materials require clear, accessible visual communication with health literacy considerations. Regulatory review of all visual materials for claims compliance.
**E-commerce:** High-volume, fast-turnaround visual production for product pages and campaigns. Product photography art direction and retouching. Seasonal and promotional visual asset creation at scale. User-generated content curation and integration into visual marketing.

## Common Challenges
- High-volume production demands with tight turnaround times that can compromise creative quality.
- Maintaining brand consistency across a growing number of channels, formats, and regional variations.
- Getting accurate, complete creative briefs that provide clear direction without over-constraining creativity.
- Managing multiple stakeholder feedback rounds and competing opinions on visual direction.
- Balancing creative innovation with brand guidelines and proven effective patterns.
- Keeping visual work fresh and trend-aware while maintaining distinct brand identity.
- Protecting creative time from meeting overload that reduces production capacity.
- Managing file organization and version control across large numbers of asset variants.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- Copyright and intellectual property law for use of imagery, fonts, and design elements.
- Trademark law — proper use of logos, brand marks, and trademarked visual elements.
- FTC endorsement guidelines — clear disclosure requirements for social media and influencer visuals.
- Image right and model release requirements for photography featuring identifiable individuals.
- ADA and WCAG accessibility standards for visual marketing materials including color contrast.
- GDPR — consent requirements for marketing imagery featuring identifiable people in EU markets.
- Cultural and religious sensitivity standards for visual content across global markets.
- Stock image licensing compliance — understanding license types (royalty-free, rights-managed, editorial) and usage restrictions.
