# exec-board

**Summary:** Provides governance, strategic guidance, and fiduciary oversight to the company, representing shareholder interests and ensuring responsible corporate stewardship.

**Level:** VP+
**Reports to:** Shareholders
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Provide strategic guidance and oversight to the CEO and executive team on company direction and major decisions.
- Fulfill fiduciary duties including duty of care and duty of loyalty to the company and its shareholders.
- Approve major corporate transactions including M&A, capital raises, dividends, and share repurchases.
- Oversee financial reporting integrity, external audit quality, and internal control effectiveness.
- Participate in board committees including audit, compensation, nominating/governance, and risk.
- Evaluate and support CEO performance, compensation, succession planning, and leadership development.
- Review and approve annual budgets, strategic plans, and long-range financial forecasts.
- Monitor risk management frameworks including cybersecurity, compliance, operational, and reputational risk.
- Ensure compliance with applicable laws, regulations, and corporate governance standards.
- Represent shareholder interests while considering stakeholders including employees, customers, and communities.

## Core Competencies
- Strategic governance and fiduciary oversight with deep understanding of board responsibilities and legal duties.
- Industry and domain expertise relevant to the company's business, markets, and competitive landscape.
- Financial literacy and ability to read and challenge financial statements, budgets, and valuation analyses.
- Independence and objective judgment in evaluating management proposals and company performance.
- Boardroom dynamics and consensus-building skills for productive board discussions and decision-making.
- Executive evaluation and mentorship for assessing and developing CEO and senior leadership talent.
- Risk oversight and crisis management experience including cybersecurity, litigation, and regulatory matters.
- Committee expertise (audit, compensation, nominating, risk) with relevant qualifications.
- Ethics and integrity with unwavering commitment to ethical business practices and compliance.
- Stakeholder perspective balancing shareholder, employee, customer, and community interests.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Total shareholder return (TSR) relative to peer group and market indices.
- Board effectiveness assessed through annual self-evaluation scores.
- CEO succession readiness and leadership pipeline depth.
- Audit and compliance outcomes including absence of material weaknesses and regulatory findings.
- Strategic plan achievement rate against board-approved objectives.
- Board composition and diversity metrics including skills matrix coverage.
- Meeting attendance and participation rates across board and committee meetings.
- Risk oversight effectiveness measured through risk event tracking and mitigation progress.

## Career Progression
Board membership is typically a capstone role following a successful executive or professional career. Most independent directors have 20-30+ years of senior leadership experience as CEOs, CFOs, or other C-suite executives. Some board members come from professional services (law, consulting, accounting) or academic backgrounds. First-time directors often join private company boards or non-profit boards before seeking public company board seats. Board committees provide specialization opportunities — audit committee chairs typically have accounting or finance backgrounds. NASDAQ and NYSE listing standards require audit committee financial experts. Board tenure varies with many companies adopting term limits (10-15 years) and mandatory retirement ages (72-75). Active CEOs often serve on 1-2 external boards; retired executives may serve on 3-5.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
Board service is part-time but intensive during preparation and meeting periods. Full board meetings typically occur 4-8 times per year, lasting 4-8 hours each. Committee meetings add 2-4 additional meetings per year. Between meetings, directors receive board materials (board packs) typically 5-7 days in advance for review. Committee chairs spend additional time with management and advisors between meetings. Annual activities include board self-evaluation, CEO evaluation, strategic planning review, and board composition assessment. Crisis situations (acquisitions, CEO succession, activist investors, cybersecurity incidents, litigation) require special board meetings and unscheduled director engagement. Directors must maintain independent judgment while being available for consultation between meetings on matters within their expertise.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **CEO** — Primary management interface; CEO reports to the board and receives guidance, evaluation, and support.
- **Board Chair/Lead Independent Director** — Sets board agenda, leads executive sessions, and serves as primary board leader.
- **CFO** — Presents financial results, forecasts, and capital plans to the board and audit committee.
- **General Counsel** — Provides legal advice, regulatory updates, and litigation status to the board.
- **External Auditors** — Reports audit findings directly to the audit committee independent of management.
- **Compensation Consultant** — Advises the compensation committee on executive pay benchmarking and program design.
- **Internal Audit Head** — Reports to the audit committee on control testing results and risk assessment.
- **Investor Relations** — Supports board understanding of shareholder perspectives and market expectations.
- **Risk Management** — Provides risk dashboards and updates to the risk committee or full board.
- **External Counsel** — Engaged for special investigations, M&A advice, and governance guidance.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** One-tier board structure is standard (combined board with independent directors). Sarbanes-Oxley and exchange listing rules impose strict independence requirements. Majority of directors must be independent. Annual director elections typical. Director compensation averages USD 250K-350K for S&P 500 companies including equity and retainers.
**EMEA:** Two-tier board structure common in Germany, Austria, and Netherlands (separate supervisory board and management board). Mandatory employee representation on supervisory boards in Germany (Co-Determination Act). Works Council consultation rights are extensive. Lower director compensation than NA. UK follows one-tier structure similar to US but with UK Corporate Governance Code requirements. Diversity quotas apply in many EU countries (France, Norway, Italy require 40% female board representation).
**APAC:** Japan has seen governance reform with more independent directors required (at least one-third for Prime listed companies). Korea has strengthened shareholder rights and board independence. China's listed companies have significant state ownership influence and party committee involvement. India mandates at least one independent director and one woman director. Family-controlled board structures remain common across APAC.
**LATAM:** Board independence is less established than in NA and Europe. Family control and founder influence remain strong in many companies. Pyramidal ownership structures (holdings companies) are common. Board meetings are often less frequent and formal than in NA or Europe. Director compensation is significantly lower than NA. Securities regulators are strengthening governance requirements, particularly in Brazil and Mexico.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Board composition often includes venture capital partners for growth-stage companies. Focus on growth metrics (ARR, NRR, CAC payback) and path to profitability. Public company SaaS boards emphasize recurring revenue quality and market share.
**Fintech:** Regulatory expertise on the board is critical — former regulators or compliance executives are valued. Audit and risk committees have enhanced responsibilities. Focus on capital adequacy, credit quality, and regulatory compliance.
**Healthcare:** Scientific and clinical expertise on the board is valuable for credibility and strategy. FDA regulatory pathway oversight requires specialized knowledge. Hospital boards have different composition (often including physicians and community leaders).
**Manufacturing:** Board members with supply chain, industrial, and international operations expertise are valued. Environmental and sustainability oversight is increasingly prominent. Union relations experience is relevant for heavily unionized companies.

## Common Challenges
- Maintaining independent oversight while being supportive of management and engaged with the company.
- Ensuring board composition keeps pace with evolving business needs, technology shifts, and diversity expectations.
- Managing activist investor campaigns and proxy fights that challenge board decisions or director positions.
- Navigating conflicts of interest between management, large shareholders, and minority shareholder interests.
- Keeping pace with rapidly evolving ESG expectations and stakeholder governance demands.
- Overseeing cybersecurity risk when most board members lack deep technical expertise.
- Board refreshment — balancing institutional knowledge with need for fresh perspectives and new skills.
- Time commitment management, especially for active executives serving on multiple boards.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) — audit committee independence, financial expert requirements, whistleblower protections.
- Dodd-Frank Act — say-on-pay votes, clawback policies, pay-ratio disclosure, and compensation committee independence.
- SEC proxy rules — disclosure requirements for director qualifications, compensation, and related-party transactions.
- Exchange listing standards (NYSE, NASDAQ) — director independence, audit committee composition, and shareholder meeting requirements.
- FCPA and anti-bribery compliance oversight responsibility under the duty of care.
- GDPR and data privacy — board-level accountability for privacy compliance and breach notification.
- ESG reporting frameworks (TCFD, SASB, GRI) for climate, sustainability, and governance disclosure.
- Insider trading regulations — Section 16 reporting, trading window policies, and Rule 10b5-1 trading plans.
- State corporate law (primarily Delaware General Corporation Law for US companies) governing fiduciary duties.
