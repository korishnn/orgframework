# prd-pricing

**Summary:** Pricing Strategist responsible for developing and optimizing the organization's pricing models, packaging structures, and monetization strategy to maximize revenue growth and market competitiveness.

**Level:** Manager / Director
**Reports to:** CPO / CFO / Head of Revenue
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Design, test, and optimize pricing models including subscription tiers, usage-based pricing, per-seat models, hybrid structures, and consumption-based pricing
- Conduct comprehensive pricing research including willingness-to-pay analysis (Van Westendorp, Gabor-Granger, conjoint analysis), price sensitivity measurement, and competitive benchmarking
- Build financial models to project revenue impact of pricing changes, packaging decisions, and new pricing model introductions
- Develop monetization strategies for new products, features, and market expansions ensuring alignment with value proposition and customer willingness to pay
- Partner with finance, product, marketing, and sales teams to execute pricing changes across all go-to-market channels
- Monitor pricing performance, market response, and competitive pricing movements, iterating on pricing strategy as needed
- Build and maintain pricing infrastructure, tools, processes, and governance for consistent pricing operations
- Define and manage packaging strategy — what features go in which tier, how to bundle vs. unbundle, add-on product structure
- Conduct price elasticity analysis to understand revenue-maximizing price points and optimal discounting strategies
- Manage grandfathered pricing transitions, price increase migrations, and legacy pricing rationalization
- Develop discounting guidelines, deal desk pricing authority, and approval workflows for sales-driven pricing exceptions
- Partner with marketing on pricing communication, value messaging, and price page optimization for self-serve channels

## Core Competencies
- Pricing strategy and economic modeling — deep understanding of value-based, cost-plus, and competition-based pricing
- Quantitative analysis and financial modeling — building complex pricing models with scenario analysis and revenue projections
- Market research methods for pricing — conjoint analysis, Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter, Gabor-Granger, monadic testing
- Cross-functional collaboration with finance, product, sales, and marketing on pricing decisions and execution
- Strategic thinking and business acumen — connecting pricing strategy to overall business strategy and competitive positioning
- Data-driven decision-making using pricing analytics, elasticity measurement, and segmentation analysis
- Packaging strategy — understanding feature bundling, tier structuring, and product line architecture
- Behavioral economics knowledge — price anchoring, decoy effects, framing, and loss aversion in pricing design
- Commercial negotiation and sales support — discounting guidelines, deal economics, and sales enablement
- Experimentation design for pricing tests — willingness-to-pay research, A/B price testing, in-market price experiments
- International pricing — PPP adjustments, regional pricing strategy, currency management, and tax compliance
- Communication skills for executive presentations on pricing strategy, revenue impact, and competitive response

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Revenue per customer / ACV (Annual Contract Value) movement attributable to pricing changes
- Price realization rate — actual price achieved vs. list price, net discount percentage
- Price elasticity measurement — demand response to price changes across segments
- Conversion rate impact — how pricing changes affect free-to-paid, trial-to-paid, and upgrade conversion rates
- Churn rate impact from price changes — downgrade and cancellation rates following pricing adjustments
- Revenue growth contribution from pricing improvements vs. volume growth
- Competitive price positioning — relative price index vs. key competitors by segment
- Customer satisfaction impact of pricing changes — NPS/CSAT correlation with pricing

## Career Progression
- **Pricing Analyst (0-3 years):** Supports pricing research, market analysis, competitive pricing tracking, and data modeling. Builds and maintains pricing dashboards. Learns pricing methodologies and tools. Background in finance, economics, or analytics.
- **Pricing Manager / Strategist (3-6 years):** Independently manages pricing for a product line or segment. Conducts pricing research and modeling. Drives pricing experiments. Partners with product and sales on pricing execution. Develops discounting guidelines.
- **Senior Pricing Manager (6-10 years):** Leads pricing strategy for a business unit or portfolio. Manages complex pricing model design. Drives packaging strategy. Mentors junior team members. Participates in deal desk decisions on high-value opportunities.
- **Director of Pricing (10-15 years):** Owns enterprise pricing strategy. Manages a pricing team. Partners with CPO and CFO on pricing architecture. Drives pricing transformation. Leads pricing for new market entry and product launches.
- **VP Pricing / Chief Revenue Officer (15+ years):** Executive-level ownership of pricing, packaging, and monetization strategy. Reports to CEO. Sets overall revenue strategy. Board-level reporting on pricing performance and strategy.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning review of pricing performance dashboards — monitor ASP trends, discount rates, price realization, and competitive price movements
- Pricing research analysis — analyze conjoint or Van Westendorp results, review willingness-to-pay data, update pricing models
- Financial modeling — build and update revenue impact models for proposed pricing changes with scenario analysis
- Cross-functional pricing meetings — align with product on packaging decisions, with finance on P&L impact, with sales on discounting strategy
- Competitive price monitoring — review competitive pricing moves, market intelligence, and positioning changes
- Pricing change management — plan grandfathering approaches, price increase migration strategies, and customer communication
- Deal desk support — review high-value or non-standard pricing requests, approve discount exceptions within authority
- Packaging strategy development — evaluate bundling opportunities, tier feature allocation, and add-on product pricing
- Price page optimization — collaborate with marketing on self-serve pricing page messaging, tier display, and conversion optimization
- Quarterly pricing review — assess pricing strategy effectiveness, market dynamics, and pricing roadmap for coming quarters
- Communication rhythm balances deep analytical work with regular cross-functional syncs and executive updates

## Regional Nuances
- **NA:** Value-based pricing is the dominant philosophy with sophisticated willingness-to-pay research. Subscription pricing is standard with annual prepay discounts. High tolerance for price increases in growing markets. Self-serve and sales-led pricing coexist. Compensation $1300K-$2500K.
- **EMEA:** More price-sensitive markets than NA with higher expectations of transparency. VAT inclusion in displayed prices is standard. Stronger consumer protection laws affect pricing communication. GDPR affects personalized pricing capabilities. Collective purchasing power in EU markets influences pricing. Compensation lower than NA.
- **APAC:** High price sensitivity in most markets with strong preference for smaller, more frequent payments. Mobile-first and pay-as-you-go models are more accepted than annual subscriptions. Local competition requires territorial pricing. India and Southeast Asia require significantly lower price points. China has unique ecosystem pricing dynamics (WeChat, Alibaba).
- **LATAM:** High price sensitivity with inflation-indexed pricing adjustments common. Currency volatility requires frequent price reviews. Local payment method preferences (boleto, OXXO, PIX) affect pricing collection strategies. Longer payment terms are standard (60-90 days B2B). Lower absolute price points but high growth potential. Purchasing power parity-based pricing is common.

## Industry Variations
- **SaaS:** Subscription pricing is dominant — monthly, annual, and usage-based models. Feature-based tiering, per-seat pricing, and consumption models. Free trial and freemium are standard acquisition tools. PLG pricing vs. enterprise pricing. Usage-based pricing is a growing trend. Churn management through pricing optimization.
- **Fintech:** Transaction-based pricing, interchange fees, monthly account fees, and percentage-based pricing. Regulatory constraints on pricing — fee caps, disclosure requirements. Interest rate-based pricing for lending products. Compliance costs embedded in pricing. Transparency requirements for financial product pricing.
- **Healthcare:** Value-based pricing models for clinical outcomes. Reimbursement-driven pricing for provider markets. Payer and channel pricing complexity. HIPAA and regulatory constraints on pricing. GPO and group purchasing pricing dynamics. Tiered pricing by provider size.
- **E-commerce:** Dynamic and personalized pricing are common. Promotional pricing and discount-driven. Free shipping threshold pricing. Subscription (subscribe-and-save) models. Marketplace pricing dynamics with seller competition. Price matching policies. Seasonal and event-based pricing.
- **Manufacturing:** Cost-plus pricing with value-based overlay. Tiered pricing by volume. Regional pricing for global products. Service and spare parts pricing separate from equipment pricing. Channel partner pricing and margin structures. Multi-year pricing agreements for enterprise customers.

## Common Challenges
- Determining true customer willingness to pay when market research and actual purchase behavior diverge
- Managing pricing transitions for existing customers — grandfathering strategies, price increase communication, minimizing churn from pricing changes
- Aligning pricing strategy across product, sales, marketing, and finance when each function has different objectives and incentives
- Pricing new and innovative products where no clear market benchmarks exist
- Navigating the tension between revenue-maximizing pricing and market share growth objectives
- Implementing and maintaining pricing infrastructure (CPQ, billing, metering) that supports complex pricing models at scale

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- Antitrust and price fixing laws (Sherman Act, EU Competition Law, national competition laws)
- Robinson-Patman Act (US) — price discrimination prohibitions for interstate commerce
- State deceptive pricing laws and FTC truth-in-advertising guidelines
- EU Consumer Rights Directive — price transparency requirements
- GDPR implications for personalized pricing based on behavioral data
- CCPA/CPRA implications for price discrimination based on consumer personal information
- Price gouging prohibitions during declared emergencies
- Tax compliance — VAT/GST/Sales tax inclusion in pricing, cross-border pricing tax implications
- Financial regulation — fee disclosure requirements for financial products, usury laws for lending
- Health insurance pricing regulations (if applicable) — community rating, risk adjustment
- Automatic renewal laws in various US states affecting subscription pricing and cancellation
- Export/import pricing regulations for cross-border sales
- Currency controls and transfer pricing regulations for international pricing
- Truth-in-Lending Act (TILA) for consumer credit product pricing disclosure
- Industry-specific pricing regulations (healthcare, telecom, insurance, utilities)
