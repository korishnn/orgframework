# lgl-litigation

**Summary:** Litigation Counsel responsible for managing the organization's dispute portfolio, including pre-litigation strategy, civil litigation, regulatory defense, alternative dispute resolution, and external counsel coordination.

**Level:** IC (Attorney)
**Reports to:** General Counsel / Deputy General Counsel
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Manage the full lifecycle of pre-litigation disputes including demand letters, cease-and-desist communications, and mediation demands
- Oversee discovery processes including document preservation, collection, review, and production in complex litigation matters
- Manage e-discovery processes including technology-assisted review strategies, data forensics, and privilege review workflows
- Select, retain, and manage external counsel relationships including budget negotiation, staffing plans, and performance evaluation
- Develop case strategy, litigation budgets, and risk assessments for each material matter in the portfolio
- Represent the organization in mediations, arbitrations, and settlement negotiations with counterparties
- Track and analyze litigation trends, patterns, and emerging areas of legal risk to inform business decisions and dispute prevention strategies
- Provide regular litigation portfolio reporting to senior leadership and board of directors including financial exposure and trend analysis
- Manage regulatory investigations and inquiries from government agencies, including subpoenas, civil investigative demands, and enforcement actions
- Advise business teams on litigation risk in product launches, marketing campaigns, contractual disputes, and operational decisions
- Manage litigation hold programs and ensure preservation obligations are communicated and enforced across the organization
- Coordinate with insurance carriers and brokers on claims reporting, coverage analysis, and litigation cost recovery

## Core Competencies
- Comprehensive litigation management and case strategy experience across multiple practice areas
- Discovery and e-discovery expertise including technology-assisted review, predictive coding, and privilege log proficiency
- External counsel management including budget accountability and legal spend optimization
- Negotiation and alternative dispute resolution skills for mediations, arbitrations, and pre-suit resolutions
- Risk assessment, exposure analysis, and reserve-setting methodology
- Written and oral advocacy for motions, briefs, hearings, and settlement presentations
- Business acumen to align litigation strategy with organizational goals and public perception
- Vendor management for e-discovery providers, investigators, and expert witnesses
- Project management for complex, multi-party, multi-jurisdiction litigation portfolios
- Crisis management and high-stakes communication under time pressure
- Familiarity with insurance coverage litigation and claims management
- Data privacy and cybersecurity incident response litigation preparedness

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Litigation cost as percentage of revenue and variance from budget
- Case resolution time from filing to disposition
- Settlement ratio and average settlement amount relative to initial demand
- Percentage of cases resolved favorably (dismissal, favorable settlement, defense verdict)
- External counsel spend management and rate compliance with billing guidelines
- Number of disputes avoided through early intervention and non-litigation resolution
- Litigation hold compliance rate and preservation audit results
- Time to production for discovery requests and regulatory responses

## Career Progression
- **Junior Litigation Associate (0-3 years):** Supports discovery review, legal research, brief drafting, and court appearances under supervision. Typically at a law firm before in-house transition. Gains deposition experience and motion practice fundamentals.
- **Litigation Counsel (3-7 years):** Independently manages a docket of cases. Handles mediations and settlement negotiations. Manages discovery strategy. Begins managing outside counsel relationships. Experience with regulatory investigations and government inquiries.
- **Senior Litigation Counsel (7-12 years):** Handles the most complex, high-exposure litigation matters. Leads trial strategy and may serve as first-chair for select cases. Manages multi-district litigation, class actions, or multi-jurisdictional regulatory matters. Mentors junior team members.
- **Director / Deputy GC - Litigation (12-15 years):** Oversees the entire litigation portfolio. Manages litigation budget and outside counsel network. Partners with business leaders on litigation risk strategy. Serves on disclosure committee.
- **VP / General Counsel (15+ years):** Executive-level ownership of all legal matters including litigation. Board reporting on material cases. Ultimate responsibility for legal risk management. May lead the entire legal department.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning review of case docket updates, court filings, and correspondence from opposing counsel across active matters
- Conference calls with outside counsel on active litigation — case status, strategy decisions, budget reviews, and settlement authority
- Discovery management — review document collection progress, privilege log review, meet-and-confer session preparation
- Internal client meetings with business teams affected by litigation — understanding facts, identifying key witnesses, preserving evidence
- Settlement negotiation sessions and mediation preparation
- Strategy sessions on pre-litigation disputes and potential claims — evaluate exposure, recommend response
- Weekly litigation hold monitoring — confirm hold notices are active, track custodian compliance
- Monthly litigation financial review — actual vs. budget, update accrual estimates, approve invoices
- Quarterly litigation portfolio report to senior leadership and board — case updates, financial exposure, trend analysis
- Regulatory investigation preparation and response coordination
- Communication rhythm is fast-paced with urgent deadlines, court-imposed timelines, and high-stakes correspondence

## Regional Nuances
- **NA:** Most litigious environment globally with class actions, mass torts, and plaintiff-friendly contingency fee system. US discovery is broad and expensive. Pro bono publico obligations for legal departments. Alternative fee arrangements common with outside counsel. Compensation range $2000K-$4000K+.
- **EMEA:** Less litigious culture with loser-pays rules in most jurisdictions (England, Germany, France). Discovery is more limited. Shorter statutes of limitations. Works Council involvement in certain disputes. Regulatory investigations are common through national regulators. UK has a significant litigation hub with collective actions growing. Compensation lower than NA.
- **APAC:** Highly variable — Singapore and Hong Kong are preferred arbitration hubs. China has a growing litigation culture but enforcement of judgments can be unpredictable. India's court system has significant backlogs. Japan has a low litigation rate with preference for mediation. Australia has robust litigation practice with class actions active.
- **LATAM:** Brazilian litigation is extremely active with high caseload volume. Labor litigation is particularly heavy — Brazil leads globally in labor claims. Court congestion is a significant issue. Arbitration is growing in commercial disputes. Enforcement of US judgments is mixed. Less developed e-discovery practice but growing. Compensation significantly lower than NA.

## Industry Variations
- **SaaS:** IP litigation (patent assertion, trademark infringement), commercial contract disputes, data breach class actions, DMCA safe harbor litigation, software licensing disputes. Lower claim frequency but high severity per case.
- **Fintech:** Consumer class actions (unauthorized transactions, fee disputes), regulatory enforcement actions, AML/KYC compliance challenges, intellectual property for payment technology, securities litigation for publicly traded fintechs.
- **Healthcare:** Product liability litigation, medical malpractice (if patient-facing), False Claims Act / qui tam litigation, HIPAA class actions, FDA enforcement defense, antitrust in provider consolidation.
- **Manufacturing:** Product liability, personal injury, property damage class actions, environmental litigation, OSHA enforcement, patent infringement for physical products, supply chain disruption claims.
- **E-commerce:** Consumer class actions (false advertising, warranty, consumer protection), Section 230 litigation, platform liability claims, marketplace seller disputes, ADA accessibility class actions, IP counterfeiting enforcement.

## Common Challenges
- Managing litigation costs while maintaining aggressive defense posture and high-quality counsel
- Balancing business reputation concerns with aggressive legal defense strategy in high-profile cases
- Coordinating discovery across multiple jurisdictions with different data privacy and preservation laws
- Handling the pressure of high-stakes litigation with significant financial exposure for the company
- Managing litigious plaintiffs and serial class action firms targeting the industry
- Predicting litigation outcomes accurately for reserve-setting and financial reporting purposes

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP) for US federal court litigation
- E-discovery rules including FRCP Rule 37(e) for spoliation sanctions
- State civil procedure variations across US jurisdictions
- Sarbanes-Oxley Act litigation-related certification requirements
- Dodd-Frank whistleblower provisions and anti-retaliation litigation
- Class Action Fairness Act (CAFA) for class action jurisdiction and settlement review
- Alternative Dispute Resolution statutes (Federal Arbitration Act, state ADR laws)
- Civil Rights Act employment litigation (Title VII, ADEA, ADA)
- State consumer protection laws (Unfair and Deceptive Acts and Practices statutes)
- Data privacy litigation provisions (CCPA private right of action for breach)
- Securities Exchange Act for shareholder litigation
- Anti-SLAPP statutes for strategic lawsuits against public participation
- Artificial Intelligence Act litigation implications (new and emerging)
- Evidence laws and privilege protections (attorney-client privilege, work product doctrine)
- Spoliation of evidence and preservation obligations across jurisdictions
