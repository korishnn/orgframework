# exec-cfo

**Summary:** Oversees financial strategy, capital allocation, investor relations, and enterprise risk management to ensure long-term financial health.

**Level:** VP+
**Reports to:** CEO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Develop and execute the company's financial strategy including long-range planning, annual budgeting, and quarterly forecasting.
- Manage capital structure, fundraising activities, and capital allocation decisions across organic investments, M&A, and shareholder returns.
- Lead investor relations including quarterly earnings calls, investor conferences, roadshows, and shareholder communications.
- Oversee financial reporting, accounting operations, internal controls, and external audit relationships.
- Drive financial planning and analysis (FP&A) including revenue forecasting, expense management, and profitability analysis.
- Assess and manage financial risks including foreign exchange exposure, interest rate risk, liquidity, and credit risk.
- Lead treasury operations including cash management, banking relationships, and working capital optimization.
- Establish and maintain corporate governance and internal control frameworks (SOX compliance for public companies).
- Evaluate M&A targets, lead financial due diligence, structure transactions, and manage integration.
- Partner with business leaders across the organization to drive financial accountability and P&L ownership.

## Core Competencies
- Financial strategy and corporate finance including valuation, capital markets, and capital structure optimization.
- Strategic planning and analysis with the ability to model complex business scenarios and long-range forecasts.
- Risk management and internal controls including enterprise risk frameworks and SOX compliance.
- Executive communication and board presentation skills for communicating financial performance and strategy.
- Leadership and organizational development for building high-performing finance teams across accounting, FP&A, tax, and treasury.
- Investor relations and stakeholder management with expertise in sell-side and buy-side relationships.
- M&A and corporate development including deal sourcing, valuation, negotiation, and post-merger integration.
- Technology and systems acumen for modernizing finance systems and driving automation.
- Treasury and capital markets expertise covering debt and equity financing, hedging, and cash management.
- International finance knowledge including transfer pricing, foreign currency management, and multi-jurisdictional tax planning.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Revenue growth rate, gross margin, operating margin, and EBITDA margin versus plan and prior periods.
- Free cash flow generation and conversion rate against net income.
- Return on invested capital (ROIC) for capital allocation decisions.
- Debt-to-equity ratio, interest coverage ratio, and credit rating trajectory.
- Days sales outstanding (DSO) and cash conversion cycle for working capital efficiency.
- Forecast accuracy measured by variance between budget/forecast and actual results.
- Internal control effectiveness — number of material weaknesses and significant deficiencies.

## Career Progression
The CFO role typically comes after 15-20 years of progressive finance leadership. Common feeder roles include VP of Finance, CAO (Chief Accounting Officer), FP&A Director, or Treasurer. Public company CFOs often have prior experience as a divisional CFO or corporate controller. Timeframe in role is typically 4-6 years, though some CFOs serve for a decade or more. Preparation includes CPA or CFA certification, often an MBA from a top program, and experience presenting to boards and investors. Next steps include CEO roles, board seats on other companies, private equity operating partner positions, or retirement. Rotation through multiple finance disciplines (accounting, FP&A, treasury, tax) is critical for well-rounded CFO readiness.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
The CFO's week is structured around financial cycles and stakeholder interactions. Monday typically includes a finance leadership team stand-up and cash position review. Weekly cadence includes one-on-ones with direct reports, revenue review with sales leadership, and an executive staff meeting with the CEO. Monthly close process involves reviewing preliminary results, variance analysis, and updating forecasts. Quarterly cycles are dominated by board preparation, earnings release readiness, and investor messaging. Annual rhythms include strategic planning and budgeting (3-4 months), audit cycle, tax planning, and compensation benchmarking. The CFO spends significant time on external relationships — analysts, investors, banks, and auditors. During M&A activity, the CFO becomes deeply involved in due diligence, valuation, and integration planning, often working evenings and weekends during active deal periods.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **CEO** — Reports directly; serves as strategic partner on all financial, investment, and capital allocation decisions.
- **Board Audit Committee** — Provides financial reporting updates, audit results, and internal control assessments at quarterly meetings.
- **COO** — Partners on operational budgeting, cost optimization initiatives, and capital expenditure planning.
- **VP of FP&A** — Drives financial planning, forecasting, and analytical support for business decisions.
- **Corporate Controller** — Oversees accounting operations, month-end close, and external audit coordination.
- **Treasurer** — Manages cash, banking relationships, debt facilities, and investment portfolio.
- **General Counsel** — Collaborates on regulatory filings, securities law compliance, M&A legal structure, and litigation reserves.
- **Head of Tax** — Directs tax planning, compliance, transfer pricing, and tax authority relationships.
- **VP of Investor Relations** — Develops and executes IR strategy including earnings materials, investor targeting, and messaging.
- **VP of People** — Partners on compensation strategy, equity administration, benefits costing, and headcount planning.
- **CISO** — Coordinates on cyber risk quantification, cyber insurance, and security investment ROI analysis.
- **Head of Internal Audit** — Directs audit plan, risk assessment, and SOX compliance program.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Highest CFO compensation globally with significant equity components. Strong emphasis on quarterly earnings and Wall Street expectations. SOX compliance is mandatory for public companies. At-will employment with severance arrangements typical in executive contracts.
**EMEA:** Dual-board governance affects reporting lines (management board vs. supervisory board). Works Council involvement in financial restructuring decisions. IFRS accounting standards required. Strong labor protections impact restructuring costs and timing. VAT expertise is critical for EU operations.
**APAC:** Government relations are integral to the CFO role in China and India — regulatory approvals and foreign investment rules are significant. Japan's keiretsu relationships influence banking and capital structures. Multiple GAAP requirements (local GAAP, IFRS, US GAAP) increase complexity. Currency controls in China affect cash management strategies.
**LATAM:** Hyperinflation accounting (IAS 29) is required in high-inflation countries like Argentina and Venezuela. Complex tax regimes with multiple federal, state, and municipal taxes. Currency volatility and capital controls require active treasury management. Transfer pricing is heavily scrutinized by tax authorities.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Recurring revenue recognition (ASC 606) complexity, usage-based pricing models, and subscription metrics (ARR, NRR, churn) are core focus areas. Gross margin analysis distinguishes hosting costs from COGS. Customer acquisition cost payback periods drive investment decisions.
**Fintech:** Regulatory capital requirements (Basel III), liquidity coverage ratios, and stress testing are critical. Revenue recognition for interchange fees, interest income, and service charges is highly regulated. Provisioning for credit losses (CECL) requires sophisticated modeling.
**Healthcare:** R&D capitalization rules, revenue recognition for value-based care, and payer reimbursement complexity. Grant accounting and compliance for research organizations. Medicare/Medicaid cost reporting for providers.
**Manufacturing:** Cost accounting (standard costing, absorption), inventory valuation, and supply chain finance are dominant. Capex planning for facilities and equipment. Commodity hedging for raw material price risk. Long-term contract accounting for large projects.
**E-commerce:** Transaction volume scale requires robust payment reconciliation. Returns and refunds accounting, gift card breakage, and loyalty program liability. Sales tax nexus complexity (Wayfair decision) across thousands of jurisdictions globally.

## Common Challenges
- Balancing investment in growth with profitability expectations from investors during high-growth periods.
- Managing investor expectations when the company misses quarterly guidance due to market or operational factors.
- Maintaining effective internal controls while scaling the finance organization and systems.
- Navigating currency volatility, interest rate changes, and geopolitical risks affecting global operations.
- Integrating acquired companies' financial systems and processes quickly to realize deal synergies.
- Building a finance team with sufficient depth while managing cost structure and efficiency ratios.
- Keeping pace with evolving accounting standards (ASC 842 leases, ASC 606 revenue recognition) and regulatory requirements.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) — Sections 302 (CEO/CFO certification), 404 (internal controls assessment), and 409 (real-time issuer disclosures).
- SEC Regulations S-X and S-K — financial statement requirements and MD&A disclosure rules.
- Dodd-Frank Act — executive compensation clawback rules, whistleblower protections, and Volcker Rule compliance.
- IFRS vs. US GAAP — dual reporting requirements for cross-listed companies.
- FATCA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) — foreign asset and account reporting.
- Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC) requirements for fintech and financial services.
- Tax regulations including BEPS (OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting), transfer pricing documentation, and country-by-country reporting.
