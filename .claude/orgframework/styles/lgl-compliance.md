# lgl-compliance

**Summary:** Legal Compliance Officer responsible for designing, implementing, and overseeing the organization's regulatory compliance programs across all jurisdictions in which the company operates.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** General Counsel / Chief Compliance Officer
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Design, implement, and continuously improve compliance programs covering anti-corruption, trade sanctions, antitrust, data privacy, and industry-specific regulations
- Monitor regulatory developments across all operating jurisdictions and assess impact on business operations, products, and go-to-market strategies
- Conduct compliance risk assessments and regulatory gap analyses using structured frameworks to identify and prioritize compliance exposures
- Develop and deliver compliance training programs tailored to employee roles, risk levels, and regional requirements
- Manage regulatory examinations, audits, and reporting obligations from government agencies and self-regulatory bodies
- Advise business teams on compliance requirements for new products, market expansions, partnerships, and strategic initiatives
- Coordinate with international counsel and local advisors on multi-jurisdictional compliance obligations
- Establish and maintain compliance policies, procedures, and internal controls including code of conduct, conflicts of interest, and whistleblower programs
- Investigate compliance violations, prepare remedial action plans, and report findings to senior management and board committees
- Manage third-party due diligence programs including anti-corruption screening, sanctions checks, and ongoing monitoring
- Administer regulatory licensing and registration requirements where applicable to the organization's activities

## Core Competencies
- Deep regulatory compliance expertise across multiple practice areas and jurisdictions
- Risk assessment methodology and structured framework application (COSO, ISO 31000)
- Policy development, implementation, and governance documentation
- Investigative skills including root cause analysis and corrective action planning
- Training program design and adult learning principles
- Stakeholder communication and executive reporting with board-level presentation skills
- Audit coordination and regulatory examination management
- Cross-functional collaboration with legal, security, finance, and HR teams
- Third-party risk management and due diligence methodology
- Regulatory horizon scanning and emerging trend analysis
- Project management for multi-workstream compliance initiatives
- Ethical judgment and professional independence in compliance decision-making

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Compliance training completion rate and pass scores across the organization
- Number and severity of compliance incidents detected and remediated
- Time to resolution for reported compliance concerns and whistleblower complaints
- Audit findings and regulatory examination outcomes (number and severity of findings)
- Percentage of third parties screened and cleared within established timelines
- Regulatory filing accuracy and timeliness (zero missed deadlines)
- Employee awareness scores from compliance culture surveys
- Number of proactive compliance improvements identified and implemented per quarter

## Career Progression
- **Compliance Analyst (0-3 years):** Assists with policy documentation, training logistics, and screening reviews. Performs research on regulatory requirements. Learns compliance frameworks and risk assessment methodology. Certifications: CAMS, CRCM.
- **Compliance Officer (3-6 years):** Manages specific compliance domains (e.g., anti-corruption, sanctions, privacy). Conducts investigations. Develops training content. Leads risk assessments. Reports to CCO or General Counsel.
- **Senior Compliance Officer / Manager (6-10 years):** Oversees multiple compliance programs or a regional compliance function. Manages regulatory examinations. Advises business teams on complex compliance matters. May manage a team of analysts.
- **Director of Compliance (10-15 years):** Owns the enterprise compliance program strategy. Manages a team of compliance professionals. Serves on risk committee. Reports to CCO. Partners with business unit leaders on compliance strategy.
- **Chief Compliance Officer / VP Compliance (15+ years):** Executive-level ownership of compliance function. Reports to board or audit committee. Sets compliance culture and tone from the top. Manages regulatory relationships. Oversees global compliance operations.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning review of regulatory news alerts and updates filtered by relevant jurisdictions and industry sectors
- Investigate and respond to reported compliance incidents — triage severity, assign investigation resources
- Compliance training monitoring — review completion dashboards, send escalation reminders to non-compliant teams
- Policy management — review and approve policy exception requests, refresh policies on annual or event-driven cycles
- Regular meetings with business units on compliance requirements for new initiatives (product launches, market entry, partnerships)
- Third-party screening — review flagged results, make risk-based decisions on onboarding or remediation
- Weekly risk assessment sessions — update risk register, score new risks, track mitigation action items
- Monthly compliance committee meeting — report on program metrics, review material incidents, escalate to leadership
- Quarterly regulatory filing preparation and submission across applicable jurisdictions
- Annual compliance program effectiveness assessment using DOJ evaluation guidelines and regulatory expectations
- Communication rhythm combines structured reporting cadence with ad hoc business advisory interactions

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **Security/Privacy (sec-grc, lgl-privacy):** Coordination on overlapping compliance areas — data privacy, information security, breach notification. Shared risk assessment framework alignment
- **Internal Audit (sec-audit):** Coordination on audit planning, compliance testing scope, and findings remediation. Regulatory exam preparation support
- **Finance (fin-accounting, fin-tax):** Anti-corruption controls integration, FCPA books and records requirements, sanctions screening of payments. Tax compliance reporting
- **HR (hr-compliance):** Employee compliance training delivery, code of conduct enforcement, conflicts of interest disclosure process, whistleblower protection
- **Legal (lgl-employment, lgl-contracts):** Compliance terms in contracts, employment law compliance, labor law compliance integration
- **Revenue Operations (sls-revenue-ops):** Anti-bribery compliance for channel partners and sales agents, commission compliance, gift and entertainment policy enforcement
- **Product (prd-manager):** Regulatory-by-design product development, compliance requirements for new features and market launches
- **Procurement (fin-procurement):** Third-party due diligence integration in vendor onboarding, supplier code of conduct enforcement
- **Executive (exec-coo, exec-ceo):** Compliance risk reporting, board-level compliance updates, material incident escalation
- **International Operations:** Regional compliance coordination, local regulatory expertise, language-specific training development

## Regional Nuances
- **NA:** Mature and well-defined regulatory environment with aggressive enforcement by DOJ, SEC, OFAC, and state regulators. Deferred prosecution agreements are a common enforcement tool. Whistleblower bounty programs (SEC, CFTC) drive high reporting rates. Compliance programs modeled on DOJ Evaluation of Corporate Compliance Programs guidance. Compensation $100K-$250K range.
- **EMEA:** Complex multi-jurisdictional environment with national regulators plus EU-level regulations. GDPR enforcement is aggressive. German and French regulators have distinct expectations. Works Council involvement in compliance policy implementation. UK Bribery Act is the gold standard for anti-corruption. Whistleblower Directive transposed into national laws. Compensation moderate with strong benefits.
- **APAC:** Rapidly evolving regulatory landscape — anti-corruption enforcement increasing in China, Vietnam, and Indonesia. Japan and Korea have well-established compliance regimes. Australia has strong enforcement (APRA, AUSTRAC). Cultural attitudes toward gift-giving and facilitation payments create compliance challenges. Regulatory fragmentation across jurisdictions. Compensation highly variable by market.
- **LATAM:** Historically challenged by corruption, but enforcement is increasing significantly — Brazil's Lava Jato cleanup, Mexico's anti-corruption reforms, Chile's strong regulatory tradition. LGPD enforcement in Brazil is growing. Labor compliance is heavily regulated. Tax compliance complexity is high. US FCPA enforcement targeting LATAM operations is active. US-style compliance programs are increasingly expected. Compensation lower than NA but rising.

## Industry Variations
- **SaaS:** Focus on data privacy (GDPR, CCPA), intellectual property compliance, accessibility (ADA, WCAG), and AI governance. Export controls for encryption software (EAR). Lower regulatory density but increasing US state privacy law complexity.
- **Fintech:** Heavy regulatory burden including AML/KYC, money transmitter licensing, consumer lending laws (TILA, ECOA), and banking partnership compliance. State-by-state licensing requirements in the US. Payment Services Directive (PSD2) in EU. Most compliance-intensive industry segment.
- **Healthcare:** Fraud and abuse compliance (Anti-Kickback Statute, Stark Law), HIPAA privacy and security, clinical trial regulations, FDA compliance, and Sunshine Act disclosures. Highly regulated with significant enforcement risk. Compliance officers often have clinical or regulatory affairs background.
- **Manufacturing:** Environmental compliance (EPA, REACH, RoHS), product safety regulations (CPSC, CE marking), supply chain due diligence (conflict minerals, modern slavery), and occupational health and safety (OSHA). Labor and employment compliance complexity. Quality management system integration.
- **E-commerce:** Consumer protection regulation (FTC, CPPA), product safety compliance for marketplace sellers, accessibility compliance, payment card industry (PCI DSS), cross-border trade compliance, and age-restricted product controls. Platform liability concerns under Section 230 and DSA.

## Common Challenges
- Keeping compliance programs current with rapidly changing regulations across multiple jurisdictions simultaneously
- Securing adequate budget and resources for compliance technology, staffing, and training — compliance is often viewed as a cost center
- Driving a strong compliance culture in a fast-growth organization where speed and revenue pressure can overshadow controls
- Managing third-party compliance risk across a global supply chain and channel partner ecosystem
- Navigating conflicts between local legal requirements (e.g., data localization) and global compliance standards
- Ensuring consistent compliance program application across culturally diverse international offices with varying risk appetites

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) — US anti-bribery and books/records provisions
- UK Bribery Act 2010 — stricter anti-bribery law including commercial bribery prohibition
- GDPR and EU data protection framework
- US state privacy laws (CCPA, CPRA, Virginia CDPA, Colorado CPA, Connecticut CTDPA)
- OFAC sanctions programs — country-based and list-based sanctions
- EU sanctions regimes and autonomous sanctions enforcement
- Anti-Money Laundering regulations (Bank Secrecy Act, AML Directives) for financial services
- US Sentencing Guidelines — organizational compliance program effectiveness criteria
- DOJ Evaluation of Corporate Compliance Programs guidance
- Sarbanes-Oxley Act — internal controls, whistleblower protections, and certification requirements
- Competition/antitrust laws (Sherman Act, EU Competition Law, national competition regulations)
- Trade export controls (EAR, ITAR, EU Dual-Use Regulation)
- Whistleblower protection statutes (Dodd-Frank, EU Whistleblower Directive)
- industry-specific regulations depending on sector (FINRA, FDA, EPA, FERC)
