# hr-compliance

**Summary:** Ensures organizational adherence to employment laws and regulations, manages mandatory reporting, conducts compliance audits, and maintains HR policy frameworks to minimize legal and regulatory risk.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** HR Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Monitor and interpret federal, state, and local employment laws to ensure organizational policies and practices remain compliant.
- Prepare and file all required government reports including EEO-1 Component 1, OSHA Form 300A, ACA Forms 1094-C/1095-C, VETS-4212, and state-specific reports.
- Conduct periodic HR compliance audits across payroll classification, I-9 documentation, overtime practices, leave administration, and recordkeeping.
- Maintain and update the employee handbook and all HR policies to reflect changes in law, regulation, and organizational practice.
- Provide guidance to HRBPs and managers on compliance risks related to hiring, promotion, compensation, discipline, termination, and employee data privacy.
- Manage worker classification compliance including exempt/non-exempt FLSA analysis and independent contractor (1099 vs. W-2) determinations using federal and state tests.
- Oversee immigration compliance including I-9 verification, E-Verify, H-1B public access files, PERM recruitment, and visa sponsorship recordkeeping.
- Coordinate with legal counsel on regulatory inquiries, government investigations, and employment litigation response.
- Deliver compliance training to employees and managers on topics including harassment prevention, data privacy, ethics, and workplace safety.
- Track and report on key compliance metrics including audit results, training completion rates, and regulatory filing deadlines.
- Monitor and manage state and local paid leave compliance across all operating jurisdictions including PTO accrual, sick leave, and family leave.
- Manage the compliance calendar ensuring all regulatory filings, training deadlines, and policy reviews are completed on schedule.
- Participate in internal and external audits providing HR compliance documentation and responding to auditor inquiries.

## Core Competencies
- Comprehensive employment law knowledge at federal, state, and local levels
- Regulatory filing and government reporting expertise

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Regulatory filing timeliness: percentage of required filings submitted on or before deadline
- Audit findings severity: number and severity of compliance audit findings
- Compliance training completion rate: percentage of employees completing mandatory training on time
- I-9 compliance score: percentage of I-9 forms with zero errors in internal audits
- Worker classification accuracy: zero misclassification findings in audits or litigation
- Policy acknowledgment rate: percentage of employees who have signed current policies
- Litigation avoidance: number of employment claims filed versus industry benchmark

## Career Progression
- Entry level: HR Compliance Coordinator managing the compliance calendar, organizing filing documentation, and tracking training completions.
- Mid level: HR Compliance Specialist conducting audits, preparing regulatory filings, maintaining policies, and advising on compliance questions.
- Senior level: Senior HR Compliance Analyst overseeing complex filings like ACA and EEO-1, leading audit workstreams, and managing immigration compliance.
- Lead level: HR Compliance Program Manager owning the enterprise compliance framework, managing cross-functional compliance initiatives, and reporting to leadership.
- Manager level: HR Compliance Manager leading a compliance team, developing strategy, and partnering with legal and audit on risk management.
- Director level: Director of HR Compliance or VP of Risk & Compliance responsible for the entire HR compliance function across all regions and business units.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as coordinator, 2-3 years as specialist, 2-3 years as senior/lead, then management track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Review regulatory updates and alerts from law firms, government agencies, and compliance publications. Flag changes impacting current policies or practices.
- Midday: Conduct compliance audits (I-9 spot checks, FLSA classification reviews, wage and hour sample audits), prepare and submit regulatory filings, respond to compliance inquiries from HRBPs.
- Afternoon: Update policy documents, review employee handbooks, meet with legal counsel on complex compliance issues, prepare compliance training materials.
- Weekly: Compliance calendar check for upcoming deadlines, legal/regulatory update review, policy exception requests processing, immigration case status tracking.
- Monthly: Compliance metrics reporting to HR leadership, I-9 audit results, new hire compliance checklist review, state leave law compliance check.
- Quarterly: ACA reporting data validation, EEO-1 data preparation, OSHA 300 log review, compliance training completion dashboard review, worker classification audits.
- Annual peaks: EEO-1 filing (March-June), ACA filing (January-March), OSHA 300A posting (February-April), open enrollment compliance, handbook annual review.
- Rhythms: Compliance has steady periodic demands driven by filing calendars. Urgent reactive work emerges when regulations change or audit findings surface. Strong project management orientation required.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: The most complex regulatory environment with 50+ sets of state employment laws. States like CA, NY, MA are frequent first movers on new employee protections. City-level laws in SF, NYC, Seattle, Chicago add additional layers. Federal contractor OFCCP compliance is a separate heavy lift.
- EMEA: Works council and collective bargaining agreements override standard policies in Germany, France, Netherlands. Data protection is paramount under GDPR. Country-specific labor codes are comprehensive and prescriptive. EU Directives must be transposed into national law creating variation.
- APAC: China's Social Insurance Law requires mandatory coverage across five insurance types. India's complex labor code consolidation (29 codes into 4) creates transition compliance work. Australia's Fair Work Act provides a national employment standards system. Japan's Labor Standards Law requirements differ significantly.
- LATAM: Brazil's CLT is intensely detailed and employee-protective. Labor courts in Brazil routinely rule in favor of employees creating high litigation risk. Mexico's 2019 labor reform significantly changed union relations and subcontracting rules. Argentina's labor law changes frequently with political shifts.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Global compliance for distributed remote teams. Data privacy compliance (GDPR, CCPA) for employee data. Independent contractor classification risk for platform workers. Stock compensation compliance for equity grants across borders.
- Fintech: Regulatory compliance for licensed entities (SEC, FINRA, CFPB). AML and KYC training compliance. Conflicts of interest policies and disclosure requirements. Compensation compliance for regulated roles.
- Healthcare: HIPAA compliance for all employees with patient data access. OIG exclusion screening for hires. Stark Law compliance for physician arrangements. CMS conditions of participation compliance.
- Manufacturing: OSHA compliance is paramount. EPA environmental compliance intersects with HR for hazardous material handling. DOT compliance for drivers. State-specific heat illness prevention and injury reporting.
- E-commerce: Federal and state wage and hour compliance for large hourly workforce. Minor labor law compliance for under-18 workers. Break and meal period compliance. OSHA compliance for warehouse and fulfillment operations.

## Common Challenges
- Keeping up with rapidly changing state and local employment laws that create patchwork compliance requirements.
- Managing I-9 compliance for fully remote workforces where documents must be inspected virtually.
- Ensuring consistent policy application across a global workforce subject to different legal systems.
- Balancing employee privacy rights with organizational monitoring and data collection needs.
- Navigating the intersection of U.S. state paid leave laws with company uniform leave policies.
- Preparing for regulatory investigations and responding to agency inquiries within tight deadlines.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: Title VII Civil Rights Act, ADEA, ADA/ADAAA, FMLA, FLSA, OSHA (General Duty Clause, recordkeeping), EEO-1 reporting, OFCCP Executive Order 11246 (federal contractors), ACA (employer mandate and reporting), COBRA, HIPAA Privacy Rule, WARN Act, USERRA (military leave), NLRA (Section 7 rights), Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act, state paid family/medical leave laws, state paid sick leave laws, state salary history bans, state non-compete restrictions.
- UK: Equality Act 2010, Employment Rights Act 1996, Health and Safety at Work Act 1974, UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, Immigration Act 2016 right to work, National Minimum Wage Act 1998, Pensions Act 2008.
- EU/EEA: EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), EU Working Time Directive, Equal Treatment Framework Directive, Acquired Rights Directive (TUPE equivalent), Posted Workers Directive, country-specific works council/constitutional laws.
- Canada: Canada Labour Code (federally regulated), provincial Employment Standards Acts, provincial Human Rights Codes, Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA), provincial Occupational Health and Safety Acts, Pay Equity Acts (ON, QC), Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA).
- Australia: Fair Work Act 2009, Privacy Act 1988, Workplace Health and Safety Acts (state-based), Workers Compensation Acts (state-based), state-based long service leave legislation, Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992, modern awards and enterprise agreements.
- Brazil: CLT (Consolidacao das Leis do Trabalho), LGPD (Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados), INSS social security regulations, FGTS Law, regulatory standards (NRs) for occupational safety, Labor Court jurisprudence (Súmulas do TST).
