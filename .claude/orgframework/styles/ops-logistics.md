# ops-logistics

**Summary:** Manages shipping, receiving, warehousing, distribution, and transportation operations to ensure efficient, cost-effective, and on-time movement of goods across the supply chain.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** Supply Chain / Ops Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Coordinate inbound and outbound shipping schedules, carrier assignments, and freight consolidation to optimize transportation efficiency and cost.
- Manage receiving operations including shipment inspection, quality verification, put-away, labeling, and system receipt posting.
- Oversee warehouse operations including storage, pick/pack, value-added services, and shipping preparation to meet fulfillment SLAs.
- Manage order fulfillment processes ensuring accurate picking, packing, and shipping with real-time tracking and customer communication.
- Track in-transit shipments, proactively identify and resolve delays, and provide status updates to internal stakeholders and customers.
- Optimize transportation methods and carrier selection (LTL, FTL, parcel, ocean, air, intermodal) based on cost, speed, and service requirements.
- Manage freight audit and payment processes, verifying carrier invoices against contracted rates and resolving billing discrepancies.
- Negotiate carrier rates and service agreements to optimize freight spend while maintaining service quality.
- Maintain warehouse management system (WMS) data accuracy including inventory locations, lot tracking, and bin management.
- Manage inventory cycle counting programs and physical inventory processes to ensure inventory accuracy.
- Coordinate import and export logistics including customs documentation, duties classification, broker management, and trade compliance.
- Manage reverse logistics and returns processing including inspection, restocking, repair, recycling, or disposal.
- Develop and maintain logistics KPIs including on-time delivery, cost-per-order, order accuracy, and warehouse productivity.
- Ensure compliance with hazardous materials (HazMat) handling, shipping, and storage regulations.
- Plan and manage seasonal logistics capacity including peak demand surge planning and temporary warehouse staffing.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- On-time delivery rate: percentage of shipments delivered by the committed delivery date
- Order accuracy rate: percentage of orders picked, packed, and shipped without errors
- Cost per order shipped: total logistics cost divided by number of orders fulfilled
- Dock-to-stock time: average hours from receipt to inventory availability
- Inventory accuracy: percentage location and quantity match between system and physical count
- Warehouse productivity: units picked per labor hour
- Freight cost as percentage of revenue: total transportation cost relative to revenue
- Damage rate: percentage of shipments damaged during transit
- Carrier performance score: average carrier performance across cost, time, and quality metrics

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Logistics Coordinator or Shipping Clerk managing daily shipment scheduling, carrier communication, and tracking updates.
- Mid level: Logistics Analyst or Fulfillment Supervisor managing carrier relationships, analyzing transportation costs, and overseeing warehouse shifts.
- Senior level: Logistics Manager managing a distribution center, regional logistics network, or transportation function with a team of supervisors.
- Lead level: Senior Logistics Manager overseeing multiple distribution centers or regional logistics operations, managing the logistics budget, and driving optimization projects.
- Director level: Director of Logistics owning the entire logistics and distribution network, strategy, budget, and team performance.
- VP level: VP of Logistics or VP of Supply Chain responsible for global logistics strategy, network design, and logistics technology investment.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as coordinator, 2-3 years as analyst/supervisor, 2-4 years as manager, then director/VP track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Check inbound and outbound shipping schedules, review overnight shipments for delays, prioritize urgent customer orders, address carrier issues or weather disruptions.
- Midday: Warehouse operations walk-through, pick/pack observation, carrier call for pickup scheduling, receiving verification, resolve inventory discrepancies.
- Afternoon: Order fulfillment status review, shipment tracking and customer communication, carrier invoice audit, warehouse staffing adjustments, logistics reporting.
- Weekly: Carrier performance review, inventory cycle counting completion check, warehouse productivity analysis, replenishment planning, freight cost tracking.
- Monthly: Freight invoice audit and approval, logistics KPI reporting, inventory reconciliation, carrier rate review, warehouse operations review.
- Quarterly: Peak capacity planning, carrier quarterly business reviews, logistics network optimization analysis, warehouse labor planning review.
- Annual: Carrier contract negotiations, logistics budget planning, warehouse technology evaluation, logistics network strategy review.
- Rhythms: Daily shipping schedules create a strong operational rhythm. Weekly peaks (order cut-off days) and monthly close periods create predictable cycles. Seasonal peaks (holiday, back-to-school, etc.) require advanced capacity planning.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: Extensive road network makes trucking dominant (72% of freight by weight). Parcel carriers (UPS, FedEx, USPS) are critical for e-commerce. Last-mile delivery innovation is intense. Warehousing capacity is tight in major markets. FMCSA regulations govern trucking. USPS is a government service with specific rate structures.
- EMEA: Cross-border logistics within EU is more efficient than US due to smaller distances, but customs post-Brexit adds UK friction. Strong rail freight network in continental Europe. Last-mile is often bike/cargo bike in urban European centers. Cabotage rules restrict intra-country trucking for non-resident carriers. Night delivery restrictions vary by country.
- APAC: China's logistics infrastructure is world-class in major cities but uneven in rural areas. India's GST simplified cross-state logistics but infrastructure gaps persist. Japan's logistics is highly efficient with advanced automation. Same-day delivery is common in China and parts of SE Asia. Congested ports are a persistent challenge.
- LATAM: Infrastructure gaps in roads, ports, and warehousing increase logistics costs. Brazil's complex tax system makes cross-state shipments administratively burdensome. Port congestion is common, especially in Brazil and Argentina. Security concerns (cargo theft) require enhanced tracking and security in some markets. Courier networks are less developed in rural areas.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: No physical logistics for core product. May have hardware logistics for kiosks, terminals, or IoT devices. Marketing materials and swag distribution. Data center hardware logistics for infrastructure companies.
- Fintech: Card production and distribution logistics. Hardware POS terminal logistics for payments companies. ATM cash logistics for banking. Document and check processing logistics. No core physical supply chain.
- Healthcare: Cold chain logistics for temperature-sensitive products (pharmaceuticals, biologics, vaccines). Lot tracking and serialization for traceability. Hospital supply chain and medical device logistics. Just-in-time inventory for hospital supplies. Sterile packaging and handling requirements.
- Manufacturing: Inbound raw material logistics from suppliers. Movement of WIP between production stages. Finished goods to distribution centers. Heavy equipment logistics require specialized carriers. Spare parts logistics for aftermarket service. Import/export logistics for global supply chains.
- E-commerce: Fast fulfillment is a core differentiator (same-day/next-day). Parcel shipping at massive scale. Returns logistics is a major operation (reverse logistics). Last-mile delivery innovation (lockers, drones, in-car). Seasonal capacity surge planning is critical. Multiple fulfillment center network for geographic coverage.

## Common Challenges
- Managing carrier capacity and rate increases during peak seasons when demand exceeds available capacity.
- Recruiting and retaining warehouse staff in a tight labor market with competitive wages.
- Managing last-mile delivery expectations for speed (next-day/same-day) while controlling cost.
- Navigating customs delays, trade policy changes, and documentation requirements for international shipments.
- Maintaining inventory accuracy in high-volume fulfillment environments with continuous movement.
- Balancing cost per order with speed requirements when customers demand fast, free shipping.
- Managing logistics disruptions from weather events, carrier strikes, port congestion, and fuel price volatility.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: FMCSA hours of service (HOS) rules for truck drivers, DOT hazmat regulations (49 CFR for hazardous materials), USMCA/USMCA rules of origin for cross-border, CBP import/export regulations, PIP/TATP (CTPAT) security certification for importers, FDA food and drug shipping requirements, FSMA Sanitary Transportation of Human and Animal Food rule, FAA for air cargo, Jones Act for domestic water transportation.
- UK: Health and Safety at Work Act (warehouse safety), CDG regulations for dangerous goods transport, UKCA marking for product conformity, Environment Agency waste shipping regulations, Driver CPC requirements for professional drivers, Working Time Directive enforcement for drivers.
- EU: EU working time directive for drivers (including mandatory breaks and rest periods), ADR (European Agreement concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Road), Community Customs Code for EU cross-border, EU deforestation regulation supply chain due diligence, Eurovignette Directive for road charging, REACH for chemical transport, GDP (Good Distribution Practices) for pharmaceutical logistics.
- Canada: Canadian Transportation Act, Motor Vehicle Transport Act for interprovincial trucking, TDG (Transportation of Dangerous Goods) Regulations, CBSA customs regulations, C-TPAT partnership for border security, Food Safety Act for food transportation, provincial trucking regulations and cabotage rules.
- Brazil: ANTT (National Land Transportation Agency) trucking regulations, INMETRO certification for packaging and logistics equipment, CONTRAN traffic and transportation regulations for cargo, MAPA agricultural product transportation requirements, ANVISA GDP for pharmaceutical logistics, CONFAZ ICMS rules for interstate freight.
- Australia: HVNL (Heavy Vehicle National Law) for trucking, ADG Code for dangerous goods transportation, ABF (Australian Border Force) customs requirements, Biosecurity Act 2015 for imported goods quarantine, NHVR (National Heavy Vehicle Regulator) compliance, Maritime Transport and Offshore Facilities Security Act for ports.
