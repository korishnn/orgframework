# da-governance

**Summary:** Defines data policies, quality standards, and access controls, ensuring organizational data is trustworthy, protected, and used responsibly.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** Data Director / DPO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Develop and enforce data governance policies, standards, and procedures across the organization.
- Define data quality metrics, thresholds, and monitoring processes for ongoing data quality management.
- Establish data access controls, classification frameworks, and entitlement management processes.
- Manage data catalogs, metadata repositories, and data lineage documentation for data discoverability.
- Chair data governance councils, steering committees, and drive data stewardship programs.
- Ensure compliance with data privacy regulations (GDPR, CCPA, LGPD) and internal data policies.
- Define data ownership, stewardship roles, and accountability structures across business domains.
- Implement data classification frameworks for sensitivity labeling and handling requirements.
- Establish data retention and disposal policies aligned with legal and business requirements.
- Lead data governance maturity assessments and improvement roadmaps.
- Develop data governance training and communication programs for organizational adoption.
- Monitor and report on data governance metrics, policy compliance, and remediation progress.

## Core Competencies
- Data governance frameworks and best practices including DAMA DMBOK and DCAM frameworks.
- Data quality management and monitoring with understanding of data quality dimensions (accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, uniqueness, validity).
- Data privacy and regulatory compliance including GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, and emerging regulations.
- Metadata management and data cataloging tools and methodologies.
- Policy development and enforcement with understanding of organizational change management.
- Cross-functional collaboration and change management for driving governance adoption.
- Data architecture and lineage understanding for tracing data flows and dependencies.
- Risk assessment and management for data-related business risks.
- Stakeholder management and facilitation for running governance councils and working groups.
- Audit and compliance reporting for demonstrating governance program effectiveness.
- Business glossary and semantic management for creating shared business definitions.
- Data ethics frameworks for responsible data use and algorithmic fairness.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Data quality scores across key dimensions (completeness, accuracy, timeliness) by domain.
- Data catalog coverage — percentage of enterprise data assets documented and classified.
- Policy compliance rate — percentage of data assets meeting defined governance policies.
- Data incident volume — number of data quality incidents, access violations, or privacy breaches.
- Governance program adoption — active data stewards, governance council participation, training completion.
- Time to resolve data issues — average time from data problem identification to remediation.
- Data access request fulfillment time — speed of provisioning appropriate data access.

## Career Progression
Data governance professionals typically start as data governance analysts or data stewards (0-3 years), progress to governance specialists or leads (3-6 years), data governance managers (6-9 years), and directors or heads of data governance (9+ years). Growth involves expanding from operational governance to strategic program leadership. Senior governance leaders design enterprise governance frameworks and influence data strategy. Some professionals transition from data management, compliance, or risk roles. Data governance is increasingly recognized as a strategic function, not just a compliance role. Certifications (CDMP — Certified Data Management Professional, DGSP — Data Governance and Stewardship Professional) are valuable. Career path may also lead to Chief Data Officer or DPO roles.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A data governance leader's week combines policy work, stakeholder engagement, and operational governance. Governance council preparation and facilitation is a recurring activity — preparing materials, coordinating domain stewards, and driving decisions. Policy development involves researching regulations, drafting policies, conducting impact assessments, and obtaining approvals. Data quality monitoring reviews dashboards, investigates issues, and coordinates remediation with data owners. Data catalog curation ensures metadata is current, business terms are defined, and lineage is documented. Access control reviews verify appropriate permissions and recertify entitlements on a regular cycle. Privacy compliance activities include data mapping, processing record updates, and consent management. Training and communication programs build governance awareness and capability. Incident response for data issues involves investigation, root cause analysis, and process improvement.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **Chief Data Officer / DPO** — Reports to; aligns governance program with data strategy and privacy compliance.
- **Data Engineers** — Implements governance technical controls, data quality rules, and lineage documentation.
- **Data Analysts and Scientists** — Consumes governance outputs; provides feedback on data quality and catalog accuracy.
- **Legal / Privacy Team** — Partners on regulatory interpretation, policy alignment, and incident response.
- **Security Team (GRC)** — Coordinates on data classification, access controls, and compliance frameworks.
- **Compliance and Audit** — Provides governance evidence, supports audits, and remediates findings.
- **Business Domain Stewards** — Owners of data domains; define business terms and data quality rules.
- **IT / Enterprise Architecture** — Collaborates on data architecture governance and technology standards.
- **Risk Management** — Aligns data risk assessment with enterprise risk frameworks.
- **Internal Audit** — Audits governance program effectiveness and control implementation.
- **Procurement** — Reviews data-related vendor contracts for governance and privacy requirements.
- **Regulatory Reporting** — Ensures data quality and lineage for regulatory submissions.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Data governance maturity varies widely — regulated industries (finance, healthcare) have established programs, while tech companies are building them. Strong focus on data monetization alongside compliance. CCPA and state-level privacy laws drive governance requirements. Growing emphasis on AI governance and model risk.
**EMEA:** GDPR is the primary driver of data governance programs. Strong enforcement by data protection authorities creates accountability. EU Data Governance Act and EU AI Act add new requirements. Works Council consultation for employee data governance. Data sovereignty requirements affect multi-region data management.
**APAC:** China's Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) and Data Security Law create strict governance requirements. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act is emerging. Japan has strong data governance traditions in enterprise culture. APAC markets require navigating diverse and rapidly evolving regulatory landscapes.
**LATAM:** Brazil's LGPD is the primary data privacy driver, modeled after GDPR but with local variations. Data governance maturity is growing but still developing in many organizations. Argentina and Mexico have data protection laws. Economic volatility can deprioritize governance investments. Emerging regulatory enforcement in Brazil is increasing compliance focus.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Data governance often focuses on product analytics data, customer data, and usage metrics. Growing emphasis on AI governance as ML features become product differentiators. Data monetization governance for API data products. Tension between governance and data democratization culture.
**Fintech:** Regulatory compliance is the primary governance driver — Basel, AML, KYC, and consumer protection requirements. Model governance (SR 11-7) for credit and risk models is a major focus. Transaction data governance for audit and reconciliation. Customer data governance for privacy and consent.
**Healthcare:** HIPAA governance is foundational — PHI identification, access controls, and audit trails. Clinical data governance for research and treatment quality. Interoperability standards (FHIR) create data sharing governance requirements. Patient consent management for data use and sharing.
**E-commerce:** Customer data governance for marketing and personalization use. Product and catalog data quality governance is critical for search and browse. Transaction data governance for payment and fraud operations. Governance for AI personalization and recommendation systems.

## Common Challenges
- Balancing data governance controls with data accessibility and democratization goals.
- Getting business stakeholder buy-in and participation in governance programs — governance is often seen as a barrier.
- Measuring and demonstrating the business value of governance investments beyond compliance necessity.
- Keeping governance documentation and data catalogs current as data landscapes evolve rapidly.
- Enforcing governance across a decentralized data ecosystem with many tools, platforms, and teams.
- Scaling governance programs from pilot domains to enterprise-wide coverage.
- Navigating the tension between global governance standards and local regulatory variations.
- Managing the people and cultural aspects — governance requires behavior change, not just technology implementation.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- GDPR — data processing records, data protection impact assessments, data subject rights, breach notification.
- CCPA/CPRA — consumer data rights, opt-out mechanisms, data inventory, and deletion request handling.
- HIPAA — PHI governance, minimum necessary standard, data use agreements, and patient rights.
- LGPD (Brazil) — data processing governance, consent management, and DPO requirements.
- PIPL (China) — personal information governance, cross-border data transfer, and consent requirements.
- Data Security Law (China) — data classification, national security reviews, and export controls.
- EU Data Governance Act — data sharing governance, data altruism, and reuse of public sector data.
- EU AI Act — governance for AI systems risk classification, transparency, and human oversight.
- SOX — financial reporting data governance, control frameworks, and audit trail requirements.
- PCI DSS — cardholder data governance, scope reduction, and access controls.
- Basel III — risk data aggregation and reporting governance requirements for banking.
- ISO 8000 — data quality management standard for governance programs.
