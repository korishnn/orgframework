# dsg-motion

**Summary:** Creates animations, transitions, and video content for product and marketing, bringing interfaces and brand expression to life through purposeful motion.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** Design Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Design and produce motion graphics and animations for digital products, websites, and applications.
- Create micro-interactions, transitions, and loading animations that enhance user experience and provide feedback.
- Produce video content for marketing campaigns, product demos, internal communications, and thought leadership.
- Develop motion design guidelines and reusable animation patterns as part of the design system.
- Collaborate with UI designers to integrate motion into design systems and component libraries.
- Optimize motion assets for performance across platforms, devices, and bandwidth conditions.
- Storyboard animation concepts and present motion direction to stakeholders for approval.
- Create animated prototypes and motion comps to communicate interaction dynamics to teams.
- Establish motion design principles, duration guidelines, and easing curves for consistent animation language.
- Stay current with animation techniques, tools, and emerging technologies including Lottie, Rive, and WebGL.
- Edit and produce video content including color grading, sound design, and motion graphics overlays.
- Manage animation asset pipelines, versioning, and delivery formats for engineering consumption.

## Core Competencies
- Motion design and animation principles including timing, spacing, easing, and anticipation.
- Video production and editing across the full post-production pipeline.
- UI animation and micro-interactions for enhancing usability and providing feedback.
- Motion design systems and guidelines for creating scalable, reusable motion language.
- Storyboarding and visual storytelling for communicating motion concepts before production.
- Technical proficiency in animation tools and frameworks (After Effects, Lottie, Rive, Protopie).
- Performance optimization for smooth animations across device capabilities and connection speeds.
- Sound design and audio editing for video and animation projects.
- 3D motion graphics for creating dimensional animations and spatial experiences.
- Creative collaboration and feedback integration across design and engineering teams.

## Tools & Technology
- Motion graphics and animation (Adobe After Effects, Apple Motion, Cinema 4D, Blender)
- Video editing (Adobe Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve)
- Interactive motion tools (Protopie, Principle, Framer, Rive)
- Animation frameworks and exports (LottieFiles, Lottie, SVGA, WebGL)
- Sound design and audio editing (Adobe Audition, Logic Pro, Ableton Live)
- 3D animation and modeling (Blender, Cinema 4D, Spline)
- Motion prototyping and handoff (LottieFiles, Rive, Haiku Animator)

- Storyboarding tools (Boords, Storyboarder, Procreate)

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Motion component reuse rate across products and platforms (reducing redundant animation work).
- Performance metrics — animation frame rate and dropped frame rate in production.
- Motion asset delivery timeliness against project schedules.
- User engagement lift measured through motion A/B tests (completion rates, satisfaction).
- Engineering implementation efficiency — reduction in motion rework during development.
- Motion library coverage — percentage of product UI states with defined motion patterns.
- Creative satisfaction score from stakeholder and team feedback on motion quality.

## Career Progression
Motion designers typically start as junior motion designers (0-2 years), advance to mid-level (2-4 years), senior (4-7 years), and lead/principal (7+ years). Career growth involves expanding from execution to system thinking and creative direction. Senior motion designers lead complex animation systems and direct video production. Lead motion designers establish motion language for brands and product ecosystems. Some motion designers specialize further in 3D animation, VFX, or technical animation. Others transition into creative direction, design management, or interactive art direction. Portfolio impact and technical range are primary advancement factors. The rise of Lottie and programmatic animation has created demand for motion designers with coding and development collaboration skills.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A motion designer's day alternates between creative production and technical implementation. Core morning hours are typically reserved for animation production — keyframes, easing curves, and timeline work. Afternoons involve reviews, iterations, and cross-functional collaboration. Handoff with engineers includes exporting Lottie JSON files, reviewing rendered motion, and troubleshooting implementation issues. Design system motion work involves defining easing curves, durations, and animation specs for component libraries. Video production projects involve editing, sound design, and motion graphics compositing. Weekly design critiques review motion work for quality and consistency. Project planning and storyboarding happen at the start of new initiatives. Performance testing ensures animations run smoothly at target frame rates. Team knowledge sharing through motion guilds or lunch-and-learns builds collective capability.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **UI Designer** — Primary collaborator for defining component animations and micro-interactions.
- **UX Designer** — Partners on transition animations, page flows, and interaction motion design.
- **Brand Designer** — Aligns motion language with brand identity and visual expression.
- **Visual Designer** — Receives static assets for animation; collaborates on motion-compatible designs.
- **Frontend Engineer** — Implements animations in code; closest technical partner for motion handoff.
- **Product Marketing** — Provides briefs for product demo videos and launch assets.
- **Video Producer** — Coordinates on video production, editing, and post-production workflows.
- **Social Media Team** — Creates platform-optimized motion content for social channels.
- **Content Designer** — Collaborates on animated content and explainer video scripts.
- **Developer Relations** — Produces technical demo videos and motion-rich documentation.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Motion design is a mature specialization with dedicated roles in most product design teams. High demand for motion design generalists who also understand video. Lottie and programmatic animation are standard. Strong emphasis on performance optimization and engineering collaboration.
**EMEA:** Strong motion design culture in UK advertising and broadcast. European film and video production heritage influences motion design approaches. Growing demand for motion designers in product design, particularly in Berlin, Amsterdam, and London. Lower specialization than NA — motion designers often cover broader creative roles.
**APAC:** Chinese and Korean motion design emphasizes bold, dynamic animation styles. Japanese motion design culture values subtlety and precision. India has growing motion design talent serving global post-production and animation outsourcing. Mobile-first motion design is critical given dominant mobile usage.
**LATAM:** Motion design is a growing field with talent emerging from design and animation schools. Lower barriers to entry with accessible tools. Many motion designers work as freelancers serving US and European clients. Animation and VFX for broadcast and advertising remain important alongside product motion.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Product motion focuses on micro-interactions, loading states, and transition animations. Performance and accessibility are critical — animations must respect reduced-motion preferences. Marketing motion includes product demos, explainer videos, and social content. Motion design systems are increasingly part of component libraries.
**Fintech:** Motion must be subtle and purposeful — not distracting from financial tasks. Trust-building animations for confirmations and success states. Data visualization animation for financial charts and portfolio views. Compliance required — animations must not obscure disclosures or regulatory information.
**Healthcare:** Calming, reassuring motion for patient-facing applications. Clinical interface motion must not interfere with workflow efficiency. Accessibility-critical — reduced motion support is essential for users with vestibular disorders. Educational animation for patient education and treatment explanation.
**E-commerce:** Performance-critical motion for product pages and checkout flow speed. Delight animations for add-to-cart, wishlist, and purchase confirmation. Seasonal and promotional motion graphics at high volume. Product video and 360-degree product view animation are important purchase drivers.

## Common Challenges
- Balancing creative ambition with performance constraints, especially on lower-end devices and slow connections.
- Maintaining motion consistency across platforms (iOS, Android, Web) with different animation capabilities.
- Integrating motion into design systems that were originally static-only in their component definitions.
- Communicating motion intent and specifications to engineers who implement animations in code.
- Prioritizing motion work against other design and product needs when resources are constrained.
- Supporting accessibility (reduced motion preferences, motion sensitivity) without losing animation value.
- Keeping up with rapidly evolving tools, animation frameworks, and rendering technologies.
- Managing the handoff of animation assets across different file formats, versions, and engineering workflows.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- WCAG 2.1 Guideline 2.3 (Seizures and Physical Reactions) — animations must not flash more than three times per second.
- WCAG 2.1 Success Criterion 2.2.2 — ability to pause, stop, or hide moving content.
- Reduced motion media query support — respecting prefers-reduced-motion user preferences.
- ADA accessibility requirements for video content including captions and audio descriptions.
- GDPR — video consent and cookie requirements for autoplaying video content.
- Copyright compliance for music, sound effects, and stock footage used in productions.
- Broadcast standards and advertising regulations for motion content published on television and digital channels.
- Brand trademark compliance for animated brand logo usage.
