# sec-training

**Summary:** Develops and delivers security training and phishing simulation programs that build a security-conscious culture and reduce human risk.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** CISO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Develop and maintain security awareness training content, curricula, and materials for all employee levels.
- Deliver security training sessions to employees across all levels through live, virtual, and self-paced formats.
- Design and execute phishing simulation campaigns to measure and improve employee security behavior.
- Track training completion rates, measure effectiveness through assessments, and report on program metrics.
- Tailor security messaging for different audiences including executives, technical staff, and frontline teams.
- Stay current on social engineering trends, phishing techniques, and evolving threats for realistic training.
- Manage the security training platform, content library, and user enrollment and scheduling.
- Develop role-specific security training for high-risk roles (developers, IT, finance, executives).
- Create engaging security communications including newsletters, posters, intranet content, and campaign materials.
- Conduct training needs assessments to identify gaps in security knowledge across the organization.
- Collaborate with HR and learning and development teams on training integration and compliance tracking.
- Measure behavior change and risk reduction through metrics, surveys, and simulated attack results.

## Core Competencies
- Instructional design and curriculum development for creating effective, engaging security training content.
- Presentation and training delivery for both in-person and virtual training sessions.
- Phishing simulation and social engineering awareness for realistic, educational simulation campaigns.
- Communication and storytelling for making security topics accessible and memorable for non-technical audiences.
- Program measurement and effectiveness analysis for quantifying training impact and behavior change.
- Employee engagement and behavior change strategies for building a positive security culture.
- Understanding of information security fundamentals across key domains (phishing, password security, data protection, physical security, social engineering).
- Training platform administration for managing LMS/LXP content, enrollment, and reporting.
- Content creation and graphic design for visually engaging training materials and communications.
- Adult learning principles and training methodologies for effective knowledge transfer.
- Analytical skills for extracting insights from training metrics and simulation results.
- Cultural awareness for creating training content appropriate for diverse, global audiences.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Training completion rate — percentage of employees completing required security training on time.
- Phishing simulation click rate — percentage of employees clicking simulated phishing links (target reduction over time).
- Phishing simulation report rate — percentage of employees reporting simulated phishing emails.
- Security knowledge assessment scores — average scores on post-training knowledge assessments.
- Behavior change measured through reduced security incident rates attributable to human error.
- Training engagement score — employee satisfaction ratings with training content and delivery.
- Time to complete mandatory training — average time for new hire training completion.
- Role-specific training compliance — completion rate for specialized training (developers, finance, executives).

## Career Progression
Security training and awareness professionals typically start as training coordinators or awareness specialists (0-2 years), progress to awareness program leads (2-5 years), security awareness managers (5-8 years), and awareness program directors (8+ years). Growth involves expanding from content delivery to program strategy and cultural transformation. Senior awareness leads design organization-wide behavior change programs and influence security culture. Some professionals come from HR training, communications, or instructional design backgrounds and specialize into security. Certifications (CISSP, CISM, Security+, SCP) combined with instructional design credentials are valuable. The field is growing as organizations recognize human risk as a critical security concern. Career branching includes broader security leadership, communications roles, or learning and development leadership.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A security training professional's day balances content creation, program management, and engagement activities. Content creation develops new training modules, updates existing materials, and designs campaign assets. Phishing simulation campaigns follow a cycle — design, schedule, launch, track results, and report. Monthly simulation campaigns keep security top of mind with varied scenarios. Training administration manages platform enrollment, tracks completion, and follows up with overdue employees. Reporting prepares dashboards on training metrics, simulation results, and behavior trends for security leadership. New hire training orientation is a recurring weekly or monthly activity. Awareness campaigns run on a calendar of security themes (Password Day, Cybersecurity Awareness Month, Data Privacy Day). Stakeholder meetings align training priorities with organizational risk areas and current threats. Team coordination with HR L&D integrates security training into broader organizational training programs. Content localization for global audiences requires coordination with regional offices and translation partners.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **CISO** — Reports to; aligns training program with security strategy and risk priorities.
- **HR / Learning and Development** — Partners on training platform, curriculum integration, and compliance tracking.
- **Internal Communications** — Collaborates on security messaging, newsletters, and awareness campaigns.
- **IT Help Desk** — Coordinates on common security issues that training should address (phishing reports, password resets).
- **SOC Team** — Provides input on current threats and phishing scenarios for simulation campaigns.
- **Compliance / GRC** — Aligns training with regulatory requirements and audit evidence needs.
- **People Managers** — Partners for team-level security discussions and accountability for training completion.
- **Executive Team** - Receives tailored training and awareness briefings for executive-level security.
- **Legal / Privacy** - Coordinates on data protection training content and privacy awareness.
- **Developers (via AppSec)** — Partners on secure coding training and developer security awareness.
- **Facilities / Physical Security** - Collaborates on physical security awareness content.
- **Employee Resource Groups (ERGs)** - Tailors awareness content for diverse audiences and inclusive messaging.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Security awareness is well-established with dedicated roles in most organizations. High adoption of training platforms and simulation tools. Regulatory requirements (SOX, HIPAA, PCI DSS) drive mandatory training. Cybersecurity Awareness Month (October) is a major campaign focus. Remote workforce training is standard post-pandemic.
**EMEA:** GDPR requires data protection training for all staff handling personal data. Multi-language training content is essential for European workforces. Works Council consultation for phishing simulation programs involving employee monitoring. EU Digital Skills and cybersecurity awareness programs. Varying data protection authority guidance on training expectations.
**APAC:** Language diversity requires extensive training localization. Cultural attitudes toward authority affect phishing simulation approaches — may be perceived as entrapment in some cultures. Mobile-first training delivery is important given mobile usage patterns. China requires localized content and may not permit certain simulation tools. India has growing security awareness programs in tech companies.
**LATAM:** Security awareness is an emerging practice in many LATAM organizations. Training often delivered in Portuguese (Brazil) and Spanish with localization for regional variations. Budget constraints limit dedicated awareness platform investment. Growing regulatory requirements (LGPD) are driving training program development. Strong social engineering threat landscape makes awareness critical.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Training focuses on cloud security, API security, and customer data protection. Developers are a key audience for secure coding training. Remote workforce security for distributed engineering teams. Customer-facing security awareness programs for multi-tenant platform users. SaaS-specific phishing scenarios.
**Fintech:** Regulatory training requirements are extensive and mandatory. Focus on financial crime awareness, fraud prevention, and transaction security. Customer data protection and privacy training. AML and KYC awareness for relevant roles. Strict tracking and documentation for regulatory audit evidence.
**Healthcare:** HIPAA training is foundational and mandatory for all workforce members. Focus on patient privacy, PHI handling, and medical device security. Clinical staff training must fit into limited availability. Patient safety connection to security awareness. High-stakes consequences make training effectiveness critical.
**E-commerce:** Training focuses on payment card data handling, customer PII protection, and fraud awareness. High seasonal hiring requires scalable new hire training. Third-party and temporary worker security training for seasonal workforce. Social engineering awareness for customer service and support teams.

## Common Challenges
- Getting employees to retain and apply security training beyond the immediate training session.
- Making security awareness engaging and memorable when content is inherently less exciting than other training topics.
- Measuring actual behavior change and risk reduction rather than just completion rates and knowledge scores.
- Keeping training content current with rapidly evolving threats, phishing techniques, and technology changes.
- Balancing training frequency — enough repetition to reinforce learning without causing fatigue and disengagement.
- Training a diverse workforce with different roles, risk profiles, learning preferences, and language needs.
- Getting leadership buy-in for awareness program investment when outcomes are difficult to quantify in financial terms.
- Managing the perception of phishing simulations — employees may feel tricked or resentful if not positioned correctly.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- HIPAA Security Rule — security awareness and training requirement (45 CFR 164.308(a)(5)).
- PCI DSS Requirement 12.6 — security awareness program requirement for cardholder data environments.
- SOX — training requirements for employees involved in financial reporting systems and controls.
- GDPR Article 39 — DPO duties include training staff on data protection obligations.
- GDPR Article 47 — binding corporate rules require employee data protection training.
- NIST SP 800-50 — security awareness and training program guidance.
- ISO 27001 — A.7.2.2 information security awareness, education, and training requirements.
- FINRA — cybersecurity training requirements for financial services firms.
- State privacy laws (CCPA, CPRA) — employee training requirements on consumer rights and privacy practices.
- Federal Trade Commission (FTC) Safeguards Rule — security awareness training requirement for financial institutions.
- CMMC Level 2-3 — security awareness training requirements for defense contractors.
- Cybersecurity Executive Order 14028 — federal contractor security training expected practices.
