# fin-fpa

**Summary:** Leads financial planning, long-range forecasting, strategic modeling, and decision support for executive leadership, driving data-informed business strategy and resource allocation.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** CFO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Lead the annual financial planning and budgeting process including strategic planning, long-range forecasting (3-5 year outlook), and annual operating plan.
- Develop and maintain integrated financial models that link revenue, cost, headcount, capex, and cash flow drivers to forecast financial performance.
- Analyze business performance on a monthly and quarterly basis, identifying trends, risks, and opportunities relative to plan and prior periods.
- Prepare board of directors and investor materials including financial presentations, strategic analysis, and competitive benchmarking.
- Partner with business unit leaders to evaluate the financial impact of strategic initiatives, new product launches, market expansion, and M&A opportunities.
- Build and maintain KPI dashboards and reporting frameworks that provide actionable insights to executive leadership.
- Lead scenario planning and sensitivity analysis to model financial outcomes under various strategic and market assumptions.
- Support the CFO in strategic decision-making with ad hoc financial analysis and modeling for capital allocation and investment prioritization.
- Develop and track key business metrics (ARR, NRR, CAC, LTV, gross margin, EBITDA, free cash flow) in partnership with business intelligence.
- Coordinate quarterly business reviews with department heads, presenting financial results, variance analysis, and forward-looking guidance.
- Drive continuous improvement in financial planning processes, tools, and reporting cadence.
- Manage the financial forecasting and guidance process for quarterly earnings (public companies) or board reporting (private companies).
- Evaluate pricing strategies, unit economics, and profitability analysis across products, channels, and customer segments.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Forecast accuracy: MAPE of revenue and EBITDA forecasts vs. actuals
- Planning cycle completion time: days from kickoff to board approval for annual plan
- Strategic initiative ROI accuracy: variance between projected and realized returns on major initiatives
- Business partner satisfaction: survey score from business leaders on FP&A support quality
- Board/investor feedback: qualitative assessment of financial materials quality
- Variance identification speed: days from close to delivery of insightful variance analysis
- Model error rate: number of model corrections required post-delivery

## Career Progression
- Entry level: FP&A Analyst supporting data gathering, variance reporting, KPI tracking, and model maintenance.
- Mid level: Senior FP&A Analyst owning the forecast for assigned business units, developing models, and presenting to business partners.
- Senior level: FP&A Manager leading the planning cycle for a business unit or region, managing a small team, and advising business leaders on financial strategy.
- Lead level: FP&A Director overseeing the full FP&A function, enterprise planning cycles, and executive decision support.
- VP level: VP of FP&A or Head of Strategic Finance responsible for enterprise strategic planning, M&A analysis, and capital markets support.
- CFO track: CFO or VP Finance responsible for all financial operations, strategy, and investor communications.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as analyst, 2-3 years as senior analyst, 2-3 years as manager, then director or VP track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Monthly (first 5 days): Analyze month-end results, prepare variance analysis vs. plan and forecast, update KPI dashboard, identify key trends and anomalies.
- Monthly (days 5-10): Present results to business partners, refine forward forecast based on actuals, update financial models with latest data, prepare executive summary.
- Monthly (days 10-20): Strategic projects, deep-dive analysis on business issues, model development, scenario planning, ad hoc requests from executive leadership.
- Weekly: Forecast updates as needed, business partner check-ins, KPI monitoring, data quality reviews, executive ad hoc requests.
- Quarterly: Quarterly business review preparation, board meeting materials, earnings guidance support (public companies), long-range forecast refresh.
- Annual (Q3): Annual planning kickoff, strategic planning sessions, long-range model development, board strategy materials, budget consolidation.
- Rhythms: Monthly close and reporting creates a regular cycle. Heavy strategic periods (board cycles, earnings, annual planning) create intense project phases. Requires adaptability for rapid-response executive requests.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: Quarterly earnings guidance creates a dominant rhythm. Focus on growth metrics (ARR, NRR, CAC payback) for SaaS companies. CFO advisory role is highly valued. Strong career progression path from FP&A to CFO. Executive presence and presentation skills are critical.
- EMEA: Works council financial information rights require detailed reporting in Germany, Netherlands. IFRS vs. local GAAP differences must be bridged in reporting. VAT and social costs significantly impact unit economics. Multi-currency financial management is more complex.
- APAC: China's regulatory environment requires specific financial reporting. India's growth trajectory requires investment-heavy financial planning. Japan's yen-based planning with global consolidation. Australia/NZ follow NA-like practices but with local nuances in reporting standards.
- LATAM: Hyperinflation accounting in Argentina affects forecasting methodology. Currency devaluation risk is a major planning assumption in Brazil and Argentina. Local GAAP differences require reconciliation. Interest rate volatility affects cost of capital calculations.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Subscription metric focus (ARR, NRR, churn, LTV/CAC). Landed expansion model creates predictable growth. Usage-based pricing adds model complexity. Multiple revenue streams require segmentation. Stock-based compensation is a significant non-cash cost.
- Fintech: Net interest margin modeling for lending businesses. Transaction volume and take rate analysis for payments. Regulatory capital forecasting for licensed entities. Provision for credit losses estimation (CECL). Compliance cost as percentage of revenue tracking.
- Healthcare: Patient volume and payer mix modeling. Reimbursement rate forecasting across government and commercial payers. Regulatory cost tracking. CapEx planning for medical facilities and equipment. Value-based care financial models.
- Manufacturing: Volume-driven cost modeling. Raw material price sensitivity analysis. Production capacity ROI analysis. Supply chain cost optimization. Inventory carrying cost analysis. Capital allocation across plants and equipment.
- E-commerce: Unit economics per order and per customer. Variable cost structure vs. fixed cost leverage. Seasonal demand forecasting and inventory planning. Logistics cost optimization analysis. Customer cohort analysis for LTV modeling.

## Common Challenges
- Forecasting accurately when market conditions, customer behavior, and competitive dynamics change rapidly.
- Getting timely and accurate data from disparate source systems with inconsistent definitions.
- Balancing the need for detailed bottoms-up modeling with the speed required for executive decision-making.
- Influencing business decisions when you have analytical insight but no operational authority over the business unit.
- Building FP&A capability and credibility in organizations where finance has historically been viewed as a back-office function.
- Managing the volume of ad hoc executive requests while maintaining the monthly reporting and planning cadence.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: SEC Regulation FD (fair disclosure for earnings guidance), SEC Regulation G (non-GAAP financial measures), SOX internal controls over financial reporting, IRS Revenue Procedure for tax reserve forecasting.
- EU: Market Abuse Regulation (MAR) for guidance and price-sensitive information, Transparency Directive periodic financial reporting, ESMA guidelines on Alternative Performance Measures (APMs).
- UK: FCA Disclosure Guidance and Transparency Rules (DTRs), UK MAR for price-sensitive information, FRC guidance on strategic reports, AIM rules for growth companies.
- Canada: National Instrument 51-102 continuous disclosure obligations, CSA guidance on non-GAAP measures, TSX company financial reporting requirements.
- Australia: ASX Listing Rule 4.3A (quarterly reports for mining/oil), ASIC regulatory guide 230 (disclosing non-IFRS information), Continuous disclosure obligations for price-sensitive information.
- Global: Transfer pricing documentation for intercompany financial modeling, IFRS reporting requirements for consolidated group forecasts, country-by-country reporting (OECD BEPS Pillar 2) for multinational groups.
