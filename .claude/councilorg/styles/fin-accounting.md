# fin-accounting

**Summary:** Maintains the general ledger, processes accounts payable and receivable, executes month-end close, and produces accurate financial statements in compliance with GAAP and regulatory requirements.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** Controller
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Maintain the general ledger (GL) ensuring accurate, timely, and complete recording of all financial transactions across all entities.
- Process accounts payable including invoice verification, approval routing, payment scheduling, and vendor statement reconciliation.
- Process accounts receivable including customer invoice generation, payment application, collections follow-up, and aging analysis.
- Execute month-end, quarter-end, and year-end close processes including accruals, deferrals, prepaids, depreciation, and intercompany reconciliations.
- Prepare journal entries for recurring and non-recurring transactions with appropriate supporting documentation and approval.
- Prepare account reconciliations for all balance sheet accounts, investigating and clearing aged items on a monthly basis.
- Produce monthly financial statements including income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement, and supporting schedules.
- Support internal and external audit activities by preparing PBC (Prepared by Client) schedules and responding to auditor inquiries.
- Manage intercompany transactions and reconciliations across multiple legal entities, ensuring elimination entries are accurate.
- Maintain the chart of accounts, ensuring consistent account usage and classification across transactions and reporting entities.
- Prepare sales and use tax returns, personal property tax filings, and business license tax renewals in compliance with state and local requirements.
- Document accounting policies, procedures, and internal controls as part of the SOX compliance framework.
- Assist with technical accounting research on complex transactions including revenue recognition (ASC 606), lease accounting (ASC 842), and business combinations (ASC 805).
- Monitor and manage cash application for customer payments, including ACH, wire, credit card, and lockbox receipts.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Days to close: number of business days to complete month-end close
- Account reconciliation completion rate: percentage of balance sheet accounts reconciled on time each month
- Journal entry error rate: percentage of journal entries requiring correction after posting
- AP aging days: average days from invoice receipt to payment
- AR aging days: average days from invoice date to payment receipt (DSO)
- Audit adjustment count: number and materiality of adjustments required by auditors
- Open items aged over 90 days in reconciliation: percentage of unreconciled items older than 90 days

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Staff Accountant I handling AP, AR, basic journal entries, and bank reconciliations under supervision.
- Mid level: Staff Accountant II or Senior Accountant owning full close cycles for assigned modules, preparing financial statements, and handling technical accounting matters.
- Senior level: Accounting Manager or Lead Accountant overseeing close processes, managing a team of staff accountants, and driving process improvements.
- Manager level: Accounting Manager or Assistant Controller responsible for the full accounting function, consolidations, and team management.
- Director/VP level: Controller or VP of Accounting owning the entire accounting function, technical accounting policy, external audit relationship, and SOX compliance.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as staff accountant, 2-3 years as senior accountant, 2-4 years as manager, then controller track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- AP days: Process incoming invoices through AP system, obtain approvals, schedule payments, reconcile vendor statements, respond to vendor payment inquiries.
- AR days: Generate customer invoices, apply incoming payments, follow up on past-due accounts, reconcile customer statements, resolve billing disputes.
- Mid-month: Prepare and post recurring journal entries (prepaids, depreciation, accruals), reconcile bank accounts, resolve intercompany discrepancies.
- Month-end week (days 1-5): Accruals, deferrals, intercompany entries, prepaid amortization, revenue recognition adjustments. Prepare account reconciliations for all balance sheet accounts. Run preliminary financial statements and review for unusual variances.
- Month-end week (days 3-7): Finalize journal entries, complete all reconciliations, final financial statements, flux analysis vs. prior month and budget, management reporting package.
- Post-close: Sales tax filing, audit support, process improvement projects, technical accounting research, SOX control documentation updates.
- Quarterly: Consolidated financial statements, intercompany eliminations, audit readiness preparation, covenant compliance reporting.
- Rhythms: Monthly close is the dominant rhythm creating a predictable cycle of increasing intensity. Post-close week allows for project work and process improvement.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: US GAAP is the reporting standard. SOX compliance for public companies. State-level sales tax complexity with economic nexus following Wayfair decision. 50+ different state income tax jurisdictions. SEC reporting for public companies includes 10-Q, 10-K, 8-K filings.
- EMEA: IFRS is the primary accounting standard for most countries. VAT/GST filing complexity with country-specific rates and rules. Country-specific statutory accounts required in addition to consolidated reporting. Germany requires annual publication of financial statements in Bundesanzeiger.
- APAC: Conversion differences between local GAAP and IFRS/US GAAP common. Australia AASB (based on IFRS) with additional local requirements. Japan's JGAAP differs significantly from IFRS. India's Ind AS converged with IFRS but with differences. China's CAS has significant differences from IFRS.
- LATAM: Brazil's complex tax system (federal, state, municipal taxes) requires specialized ERP modules. Inflation accounting historically relevant in Argentina. Mexico's NIF standards are IFRS-converged but with local variations. Chile's IFRS adoption is advanced. High frequency of tax law changes creates ongoing compliance work.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: ASC 606 revenue recognition complexity with multiple-element arrangements. Deferred revenue and contract asset/liability accounting. Usage-based billing creates complex revenue schedules. Commission capitalization under ASC 340. Stock-based compensation accounting is significant.
- Fintech: ASC 310/320 for loan and investment accounting. Regulatory capital calculations for licensed entities. CECL (ASC 326) for credit loss estimation. Transaction volume creates high-volume accounting processing. Settlement accounting for payments businesses.
- Healthcare: ASC 606 for patient revenue recognition with complex payer reimbursement. Medicare/Medicaid cost reporting. HIPAA compliance in accounting records. Provider tax accounting and 340B program accounting. Managed care contract accounting.
- Manufacturing: ASC 330 inventory accounting (LIFO, FIFO, standard costing). ASC 360 fixed asset accounting with heavy equipment bases. Job costing and work-in-process accounting. COGS variance analysis is critical. Bill of materials cost roll-ups.
- E-commerce: High-volume, low-dollar transaction processing. Payment processor settlement accounting. Chargeback and refund accounting complexity. Sales tax compliance economic nexus across states. Gift card and loyalty program liability accounting.

## Common Challenges
- Closing the books on an aggressive timeline while ensuring accuracy across all accounts.
- Managing intercompany transaction elimination and reconciliation across multiple legal entities and currencies.
- Staying current with evolving accounting standards (revenue, leases, credit losses) and implementing required changes.
- Maintaining strong internal controls while streamlining processes for efficiency.
- Recruiting and retaining qualified accounting talent in a competitive market for finance professionals.
- Integrating acquired company accounting into the parent company's systems, policies, and chart of accounts.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: US GAAP (ASC codification), SEC Regulation S-X (public company reporting), SOX Sections 302 and 404 (internal control over financial reporting), IRS Circular 230 (tax practice), state Board of Accountancy requirements, FINRA rules for broker-dealers, PCAOB audit standards for public companies.
- UK: UK GAAP (FRS 102/101) or IFRS, Companies Act 2006 filing requirements, HMRC filing deadlines, Charity Commission SORP for non-profits, FCA disclosure requirements.
- EU: IFRS as adopted by EU (EU-IFRS), EU Accounting Directive, country-specific commercial codes for statutory accounts, beneficial ownership registers, country-by-country reporting (DAC6) for large multinationals.
- Canada: IFRS (public companies) or ASPE (private enterprises), Canada Business Corporations Act filing requirements, provincial securities commission reporting, CRA tax filing deadlines, provincial sales tax filing.
- Brazil: Brazilian GAAP (CPC standards converged with IFRS), SPED accounting and tax digital filing, CRC (Regional Accounting Council) registration requirements, CVM requirements for public companies, Lei das S.A. (Corporations Law).
- Australia: Australian Accounting Standards (AASB, IFRS-based), Corporations Act 2001 filing to ASIC, ATO reporting requirements, BAS (Business Activity Statement) GST filing, ASX listing rules for public companies.
