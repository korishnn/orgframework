# sec-grc

**Summary:** Manages security frameworks, risk assessments, and compliance programs, ensuring the organization meets regulatory requirements and security standards.

**Level:** IC or Manager
**Reports to:** CISO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Develop and maintain information security policies, standards, procedures, and guidelines across the organization.
- Conduct risk assessments using established methodologies and manage remediation plans through to closure.
- Oversee compliance programs for regulatory frameworks and security standards (SOC 2, ISO 27001, PCI DSS, HIPAA, FedRAMP).
- Manage third-party security risk assessments, vendor security reviews, and supply chain risk management.
- Prepare and present security metrics, risk reporting, and compliance status to leadership and the board.
- Coordinate internal and external security audits, evidence collection, and audit readiness activities.
- Maintain the information security management system (ISMS) and supporting documentation.
- Develop and deliver security awareness content focused on policy compliance and regulatory requirements.
- Manage security exception processes, risk acceptance, and compensating control documentation.
- Track regulatory changes and industry standards evolution that impact the organization's compliance obligations.
- Lead security due diligence for M&A activities and new business initiatives.
- Establish and chair security governance committees and working groups.

## Core Competencies
- Security frameworks and standards expertise (ISO 27001, SOC 2, NIST CSF, PCI DSS, HIPAA, FedRAMP).
- Risk assessment and management methodologies (NIST RMF, FAIR, OCTAVE, ISO 31000).
- Policy development and documentation with clear, enforceable, and business-practical language.
- Compliance program management for coordinating multiple regulatory requirements and certification cycles.
- Audit coordination and evidence collection for efficient audit execution and finding remediation.
- Cross-functional communication and stakeholder management for driving compliance across departments.
- Third-party risk management for vendor security assessment and supply chain oversight.
- Control testing and effectiveness assessment methodologies.
- Regulatory knowledge across relevant jurisdictions (GDPR, CCPA, SOX, industry-specific regulations).
- Project management for compliance initiatives, certification projects, and remediation programs.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery understanding for operational resilience programs.
- Emerging risk awareness for adapting programs to new technologies (AI, cloud, IoT) and threats.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Compliance audit results — number of findings, severity distribution, and remediation status.
- Risk remediation velocity — time from risk identification to acceptance or mitigation closure.
- Policy attestation rate — percentage of employees completing required policy acknowledgments.
- Third-party risk posture — percentage of vendors assessed and meeting security requirements.
- Certification maintenance — on-time renewals for ISO 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS, etc.
- Control effectiveness — percentage of controls passing testing on first attempt.
- Regulatory change response time — time from new regulation to implemented compliance controls.
- Security exception aging — number and duration of open security exceptions.

## Career Progression
GRC professionals typically start as GRC analysts (0-3 years), progress to GRC specialists or leads (3-6 years), GRC managers (6-10 years), and GRC directors or head of GRC (10+ years). Growth involves expanding from operational compliance tasks to strategic program design and leadership. Senior GRC professionals design compliance programs, lead certification efforts, and advise executive leadership. Many GRC professionals transition from audit, legal, or risk management backgrounds. Certifications are highly valued in GRC (CISSP, CISM, CRISC, CISA, CIPP, CIPM). Some GRC leaders advance to CISO or Chief Compliance Officer roles. The field is growing as regulatory complexity increases across industries. Specialization options include privacy GRC, third-party risk, AI governance, or specific framework expertise.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
GRC work follows audit cycles, risk assessment cadences, and regulatory deadlines. Policy development involves drafting, stakeholder review, approval, communication, and attestation tracking. Risk assessments follow a structured cycle — scope definition, stakeholder interviews, control evaluation, risk scoring, and remediation planning. Audit preparation includes scoping, evidence collection, readiness reviews, and audit day coordination. Control testing validates that security controls are designed effectively and operating as intended. Third-party risk reviews evaluate vendor security programs through questionnaires, evidence review, and assessment scoring. Regulatory tracking monitors new requirements and coordinates impact assessments. GRC committee meetings review risk posture, policy changes, and compliance status. Monthly reporting prepares risk dashboards and compliance metrics for leadership.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are the most common compliance frameworks. FedRAMP is critical for government-adjacent companies. State-level privacy laws (CCPA, CPRA, Colorado, Virginia, Connecticut) create patchwork compliance requirements. SOX compliance for public companies — ITGC requirements. Strong emphasis on compliance automation tools.
**EMEA:** ISO 27001 is the dominant security standard. GDPR has the strongest enforcement of any privacy regulation globally. EU AI Act creates new compliance requirements for AI systems. The EU Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) applies to financial services. Works Council consultation for security policies affecting employee monitoring. Data sovereignty requirements affect cloud security controls.
**APAC:** China's Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), and Cybersecurity Law create complex multi-layer compliance. Japan's Act on Protection of Personal Information (APPI) and FISC guidelines for financial sector. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act emerging framework. Singapore's Cybersecurity Act and MAS TRM for financial services. Varying levels of privacy law maturity and enforcement across APAC.
**LATAM:** Brazil's LGPD is the primary privacy framework, modeled after GDPR. Mexico's Federal Law on Protection of Personal Data. Chile, Colombia, Argentina have data protection laws at varying maturity levels. ISO 27001 certification is valued but less universally adopted than in NA or Europe. Local regulatory expertise is critical given variations across countries.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** SOC 2 Type II is the standard compliance expectation from customers. ISO 27001 is often pursued additionally for international markets. FedRAMP for government sector SaaS. GDPR compliance for EU customer data. Compliance automation tools are popular for streamlining evidence collection.
**Fintech:** Regulatory compliance is the primary GRC driver — regulations are extensive and non-negotiable. PCI DSS for payment processing, SOX for public companies, FINRA/SEC for securities compliance. Regular regulatory examinations in addition to external audits. Capital adequacy and financial crime compliance alongside security GRC.
**Healthcare:** HIPAA/HITECH compliance is foundational. Meaningful Use and HITRUST CSF common for healthcare organizations. FDA cybersecurity guidance for medical devices. Clinical trial data protection and GxP compliance overlap. State-specific healthcare privacy laws (e.g., Washington My Health My Data Act).
**Manufacturing:** NIST CSF and NIST 800-171 for defense contractors (DFARS, CMMC). IT/OT security convergence creates unique compliance challenges. Industrial control system (ICS) security standards (ISA/IEC 62443). Supply chain security compliance for global manufacturing operations. Export control compliance (EAR, ITAR).

## Common Challenges
- Keeping pace with rapidly evolving regulatory landscape — new privacy laws, AI regulations, and industry standards.
- Driving compliance across engineering teams that prioritize feature velocity over security controls and evidence.
- Balancing compliance requirements with operational efficiency — avoiding excessive control burden that slows business.
- Managing audit fatigue when multiple compliance frameworks require similar but not identical controls and evidence.
- Quantifying risk in business-relevant terms that executives and board members can understand and act on.
- Maintaining continuous compliance versus point-in-time audit readiness — transitioning from check-the-box to embedded practices.
- Getting budget and resources for compliance programs that are perceived as cost centers rather than value creators.
- Managing third-party risk across a growing vendor ecosystem with varying security maturity and limited leverage.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- SOC 2 (Service Organization Control) — Trust Services Criteria for security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy.
- ISO 27001 — Information security management system (ISMS) standard requiring certification and annual surveillance audits.
- PCI DSS — Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard for organizations handling cardholder data.
- HIPAA Security and Privacy Rules — healthcare data protection requirements and breach notification.
- GDPR — data protection requirements, data subject rights, breach notification, and cross-border data transfer rules.
- FedRAMP — cloud security authorization for US federal government systems.
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) — security program framework used voluntary and for regulatory compliance.
- SOX — IT general controls (ITGC) for financial reporting systems.
- CCPA/CPRA — California consumer privacy rights and data protection requirements.
- EU AI Act — risk-based AI regulation with transparency, accountability, and high-risk AI system requirements.
- FISMA — Federal Information Security Management Act for US government systems.
- DFARS / CMMC — cybersecurity requirements for defense contractors.
- NIS Directive — security requirements for EU critical infrastructure operators.
