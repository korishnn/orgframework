# hr-recruiting

**Summary:** Sources, screens, assesses, and hires top-tier talent across all departments through strategic sourcing, rigorous evaluation, and exceptional candidate experience.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** HR Director / TA Lead
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Partner with hiring managers to define role requirements, write job descriptions, and establish candidate scorecards aligned with department needs and company culture.
- Source candidates through a multi-channel approach including LinkedIn Recruiter, Indeed, niche job boards, employee referral programs, university recruiting, and direct outreach.
- Screen resumes and conduct phone/video screens to evaluate technical skills, behavioral fit, and compensation alignment before advancing candidates to hiring teams.
- Manage the full recruitment lifecycle including offer letter generation, compensation negotiation, background checks, and closing logistics from acceptance to start date.
- Build and maintain a robust talent pipeline for critical and hard-to-fill roles through proactive sourcing, networking events, and talent community engagement.
- Track and report recruitment metrics including time-to-fill, source-of-hire, offer acceptance rate, and cost-per-hire to optimize sourcing strategy.
- Coordinate interview scheduling, feedback collection, and debrief sessions with interview panels to ensure consistent and bias-free evaluations.
- Champion employer brand through compelling job posts, social media content, candidate testimonials, and representing the company at career fairs and industry events.
- Develop and maintain a diverse candidate slate for every open role in partnership with diversity recruiting initiatives and community organizations.
- Manage candidate relationship management (CRM) data integrity, ensuring all interactions and pipeline stages are accurately recorded for analytics and compliance.
- Conduct compensation benchmarking and market intelligence gathering to inform competitive offer packages and hiring strategy.
- Support internal mobility and transfer processes by posting internal opportunities and facilitating lateral moves across departments.

## Core Competencies
- Full-cycle recruiting across technical and non-technical disciplines
- Behavioral and situational interviewing techniques (e.g., STAR method)
- Passive candidate sourcing and headhunting
- Negotiation and offer closing skills
- Stakeholder management and hiring manager partnership
- Applicant tracking system (ATS) and CRM proficiency
- Employment brand storytelling and content creation
- Diversity recruiting strategy and bias mitigation
- Market intelligence and compensation analysis
- Data-driven recruitment decision-making
- Time management and prioritization across multiple req loads
- Written and verbal communication across all levels

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Time-to-fill: average days from job opening to accepted offer
- Offer acceptance rate: percentage of extended offers accepted
- Source-of-hire mix: percentage breakdown by sourcing channel
- Candidate satisfaction score (CSAT): NPS from candidate experience surveys
- Quality of hire: 6-month and 12-month retention plus manager satisfaction rating
- Cost-per-hire: total recruitment spend divided by hires
- Diversity slate percentage: proportion of diverse candidates per requisition pool

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Recruiting Coordinator supporting scheduling and logistics for a small recruiting team. Gains exposure to full-cycle recruiting fundamentals.
- Mid level: Recruiter managing 8-15 reqs independently across assigned departments. Develops deep sourcing and stakeholder management skills.
- Senior level: Senior Recruiter handling 12-20 reqs including executive and niche roles. Mentors junior team members and leads special projects.
- Lead level: Lead Recruiter or Team Lead overseeing a pod of recruiters, managing allocation, and driving process improvements.
- Manager/Director: TA Manager or Director of Talent Acquisition owning the entire recruiting function, strategy, budget, and team performance.
- Typical timeline: 1-2 years as coordinator, 2-4 years as recruiter, 2-3 years as senior, then lead or management track depending on preference.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Review new applications, screen incoming resumes, respond to candidate emails, and update ATS pipeline statuses.
- Midday: Conduct phone/video screens (3-5 per day during active req loads), meet with hiring managers for new req kickoffs or pipeline reviews.
- Afternoon: Source passive candidates through LinkedIn and CRM tools, submit top candidate profiles for review, coordinate interview scheduling.
- Weekly: Team standup to review open reqs, prioritization, and blockers. Debrief sessions with hiring managers on interviewed candidates.
- Bi-weekly: Pipeline review with TA leadership, recruitment metrics analysis, sourcing strategy adjustments based on conversion data.
- Quarterly: Hiring plan reviews with department heads, budget alignment, and recruitment marketing calendar planning.
- Rhythms: Most work is self-directed with req loads assigned based on priority. Communication happens primarily through Slack, email, and scheduled meetings. Heavy calendar management required for interview coordination.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: Largest recruiting footprint. Heavy emphasis on at-will employment, diverse slate requirements. Competitive compensation environment. Salary transparency laws in NY, CA, CO, WA require pay ranges in job postings.
- EMEA: Strong works council and collective bargaining contexts in Germany, France, Netherlands. Fixed-term contract restrictions. GDPR heavily regulates candidate data processing. Notice periods typically 1-3 months. Mandatory works council involvement in hiring in many countries.
- APAC: At-will less common; contracts with fixed terms prevail. India, Singapore, Philippines have thriving recruitment process outsourcing (RPO) ecosystems. Chinese labor law requires social insurance and housing fund enrollment from day one.
- LATAM: Growing tech talent hubs in Brazil, Mexico, Argentina. CLT regime in Brazil creates unique employer obligations. Mexico has profit-sharing (PTU) requirements. Lower absolute comp but high demand for bilingual talent. Strong preference for local-language job postings.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: High-volume technical recruiting for engineers, product managers, and sales teams. Competitive equity compensation as differentiator. Strong emphasis on culture fit and values alignment.
- Fintech: Heavy compliance background checks required. Regulatory knowledge screening (SOX, AML, KYC). Niche talent pool for blockchain, payments, and risk roles. Compensation heavily weighted toward cash.
- Healthcare: Strict licensing and credential verification for clinical roles. Background check requirements per HIPAA and state regulations. Slower hiring cycles due to compliance checks.
- Manufacturing: High-volume blue-collar recruiting for plant and warehouse roles. Shift work scheduling complexity. Trade school and apprenticeship partnerships. Drug screening requirements vary by state.
- E-commerce: Seasonal hiring surges around peak periods (holiday, Prime Day). Need for flexible staffing models including temporary and gig workers. Heavy volume of warehouse and customer service roles with rapid onboarding cycles.

## Common Challenges
- Balancing speed with quality when multiple urgent reqs open simultaneously across departments.
- Candidate ghosting and last-minute offer declines in a competitive labor market.
- Managing hiring manager expectations around unrealistic candidate profiles or compensation bands.
- Maintaining a diverse candidate pipeline when sourcing from homogeneous talent pools.
- Navigating compensation transparency laws while maintaining internal equity across departments.
- Recruiting for niche or emerging technology roles with shallow talent pools (AI/ML engineers, blockchain developers).

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: EEOC Title VII anti-discrimination, OFCCP compliance for federal contractors, state-specific pay transparency laws (NY, CA, CO, WA), Immigration and Nationality Act (I-9 verification, H-1B sponsorship).
- EU: GDPR Article 9 on processing candidate data, right to be forgotten for applicant data, EU Agency Workers Directive for temporary staffing.
- UK: Equality Act 2010, Immigration Act 2016 right-to-work checks, Rehabilitation of Offenders Act for background disclosures.
- Canada: Provincial human rights codes, Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) for candidate data.
- Brazil: CLT consolidation of labor laws, Lei Geral de Protecao de Dados (LGPD) for data privacy, quota requirements for persons with disabilities.
- Global: ILO core labor standards, sanctions screening for cross-border hiring, anti-bribery considerations in candidate sourcing.
