# sec-risk

**Summary:** Identifies, assesses, and mitigates business and technology risks, providing risk-informed guidance to protect the organization while enabling business objectives.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** CISO / Risk Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Identify and assess security and technology risks across the organization's operations, systems, and third-party relationships.
- Develop risk registers, heat maps, and risk reporting dashboards for leadership and board-level visibility.
- Define risk acceptance criteria, risk appetite statements, and risk treatment strategies.
- Facilitate risk assessment workshops with business and technical stakeholders to identify and evaluate risks.
- Monitor risk mitigation plans and track remediation progress through to closure.
- Provide risk-informed recommendations for strategic business decisions, new initiatives, and technology investments.
- Quantify cybersecurity risk in financial terms (FAIR analysis) for executive decision-making.
- Manage the enterprise security risk management program and methodology.
- Conduct risk assessments for new vendors, partners, and acquisitions.
- Integrate security risk into enterprise risk management (ERM) frameworks.
- Develop risk scenario analysis and stress testing for extreme but plausible risk events.
- Maintain risk policies, procedures, and risk management documentation.

## Core Competencies
- Risk assessment methodologies (FAIR, OCTAVE, NIST RMF, ISO 31000, COSO ERM).
- Risk quantification and analysis for translating technical risk into business impact.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery planning for organizational resilience.
- Stakeholder facilitation and communication for leading risk workshops and presenting findings.
- Reporting and visualization of risk data for dashboards and executive presentations.
- Regulatory and compliance risk awareness for security-related legal obligations.
- Critical thinking and analytical skills for evaluating complex risk scenarios and interdependencies.
- Vendor risk management for assessing third-party and supply chain security risks.
- Project management for risk treatment plans and remediation tracking.
- Deep understanding of security controls, technologies, and threat landscape for informed risk analysis.
- Financial acumen for cost-benefit analysis of risk mitigation investments.
- Communication and negotiation for risk acceptance discussions with business owners.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Risk remediation velocity — time from risk identification to acceptance or mitigation.
- Risk reduction — percentage reduction in aggregate risk exposure year-over-year.
- Critical and high-risk finding count — number and trend of highest-severity risks.
- Risk acceptance aging — number and duration of accepted risks exceeding review periods.
- Risk assessment completion rate — percentage of planned assessments completed on schedule.
- Business stakeholder participation in risk management activities (workshop attendance, action completion).
- Risk quantification maturity — percentage of risks with financial impact quantification.
- Control effectiveness — percentage of controls rated as effective in risk mitigation.

## Career Progression
Risk management professionals typically start as risk analysts (0-3 years), progress to risk specialists or leads (3-6 years), risk managers (6-10 years), and risk directors (10+ years). Growth involves expanding from operational risk assessment to strategic risk program leadership. Senior risk professionals design risk frameworks, manage risk appetite, and advise executive leadership on risk strategy. Many risk professionals come from audit, compliance, or security operations backgrounds. Certifications are highly valued (CRISC, CISSP, CISM, FAIR, FRM, PRM). Some risk leaders advance to CISO or Chief Risk Officer roles. The field is growing with increasing regulatory focus on risk management. Specialization options include third-party risk, operational risk, cyber risk quantification, or business continuity.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A risk manager's week follows assessment cycles and reporting cadences. Risk assessments involve scoping, stakeholder interviews, control evaluation, threat identification, impact analysis, and risk scoring. Assessment outputs feed the risk register, which is continuously maintained with new risks, status updates, and treatment tracking. Monthly risk reporting prepares dashboards and summaries for leadership, highlighting top risks, trends, and remediation progress. Vendor risk assessments follow third-party lifecycle from onboarding through periodic review. Risk acceptance processing reviews exception requests, evaluates compensating controls, and obtains business owner sign-off. Quarterly risk committee meetings review the risk landscape, discuss emerging risks, and approve risk acceptance decisions. Annual risk appetite review updates risk tolerance statements and threshold definitions. Business continuity planning involves BIA updates, plan testing, and exercise facilitation. New initiative risk reviews are integrated into project and change management processes.

- **Product Teams** — Supports security risk assessment for new product features and launches.
- **Executive Leadership** — Receives risk reporting, appetite decisions, and strategic risk guidance.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Risk management is well-established with mature frameworks. FAIR analysis for risk quantification is increasingly common. SEC cybersecurity risk disclosure requirements drive public company risk programs. Board-level risk oversight expectations are high. Cybersecurity insurance market influences risk management practices.
**EMEA:** EU regulatory framework for risk management (DORA for financial services, NIS2 Directive). GDPR risk assessment requirements (Data Protection Impact Assessments). EU AI Act requires risk management for AI systems. Works Council involvement in risk decisions affecting employees. Strong emphasis on operational resilience regulation.
**APAC:** Varying risk management maturity across markets. Singapore has strong regulatory expectations for risk management (MAS TRM). Japan's financial services have well-established risk programs. China's MLPS and Data Security Law create risk assessment requirements. India's CERT-In directives create cyber risk reporting obligations. Growing regulatory expectations across the region.
**LATAM:** Risk management is developing with increasing regulatory focus. Brazil's LGPD requires risk assessment for data processing. Financial sector regulators are increasing cyber risk expectations. Insurance market is growing but less developed than NA. Talent gap for specialized risk management professionals.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Risk management focuses on data security, service availability, and multi-tenant architecture risks. Customer-driven risk assessment requests (vendor questionnaires) are a recurring demand. Third-party and supply chain risks from embedded services. Platform risk for critical customer-facing systems. AI/ML risks from new product features.
**Fintech:** Highest regulatory risk management requirements — capital adequacy, operational resilience, and financial crime compliance. Model risk management for credit, fraud, and financial models (SR 11-7). Concentration risk for critical service providers. Systemic risk implications for large financial institutions. Extensive regulatory examination of risk programs.
**Healthcare:** Patient safety risk is the highest priority alongside data security. Clinical risk management for technology-enabled care delivery. Medical device security risk management throughout product lifecycle. Regulatory risk from HIPAA enforcement, FDA requirements, and state healthcare regulations. Public health risk considerations for large-scale breaches.
**E-commerce:** Payment card risk (PCI DSS scope and compliance). Fraud risk management overlapping with security risk. Supply chain and logistics disruption risks. Platform risk during seasonal peak demand. Customer trust risk as a business-critical concern. Regulatory risk from consumer protection and e-commerce regulations.

## Common Challenges
- Quantifying cybersecurity risk in financial terms that business leaders can understand and act on.
- Getting business units to take ownership of risk acceptance rather than defaulting risk to the security team.
- Maintaining an up-to-date risk register when the risk landscape changes faster than assessment cycles.
- Balancing risk reduction investment against other business priorities and resource constraints.
- Getting reliable risk data from distributed teams and complex systems across the organization.
- Communicating risk to non-technical stakeholders without creating undue alarm or complacency.
- Navigating the tension between enabling business speed and applying necessary risk controls.
- Keeping risk management methodology consistent across different risk types, business units, and geographies.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF) — comprehensive risk management process for federal systems.
- ISO 31000 — risk management principles and guidelines international standard.
- FAIR Institute model — risk quantification standard for financial analysis of information security risk.
- SOX — risk assessment requirements for internal controls over financial reporting.
- GDPR — Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) requirement for high-risk processing activities.
- HIPAA Security Rule — risk analysis requirement (45 CFR 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)).
- PCI DSS Requirement 12 — annual risk assessment requirement for cardholder data environments.
- DORA (EU Digital Operational Resilience Act) — ICT risk management requirements for financial entities.
- NIS2 Directive — risk management measures for EU critical infrastructure and essential services.
- COSO ERM Framework — enterprise risk management integrated framework for strategic risk management.
- FFIEC — risk assessment guidance for financial institutions.
- CMMC — risk management requirements for defense contractors.
- ERM maturity models — organizational capability assessment for risk management programs.
