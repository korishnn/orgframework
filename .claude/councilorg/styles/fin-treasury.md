# fin-treasury

**Summary:** Manages corporate liquidity, cash positioning, banking relationships, debt financing, foreign exchange risk, and capital structure to ensure financial stability and optimize the cost of capital.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** CFO / Treasurer
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Manage daily cash positioning including opening and closing balances across all bank accounts, ensuring sufficient liquidity for operational needs.
- Prepare short-term and medium-term cash flow forecasts to optimize working capital and identify funding requirements in advance.
- Maintain relationships with banking partners including account management, fee negotiation, service optimization, and RFP processes for new banking services.
- Oversee debt facilities including revolving credit facilities, term loans, and lines of credit, managing drawdowns, repayments, and covenant compliance.
- Manage the capital structure including equity/debt mix, share buyback programs, dividend payments, and capital allocation recommendations.
- Execute foreign exchange hedging strategies to manage currency risk on balance sheet exposures, forecasted transactions, and net investment hedges.
- Manage investment of excess cash in accordance with the investment policy, balancing safety, liquidity, and yield objectives.
- Oversee the corporate credit card and p-card programs including card issuance, spending limits, reconciliation, and program compliance.
- Manage intercompany lending and borrowing arrangements including cash pooling, netting, and intercompany loan documentation.
- Monitor and report on financial risk exposures including interest rate risk, counterparty credit risk, and commodity price risk.
- Prepare treasury reports for management and the board including cash position, debt covenant compliance, and counterparty exposure.
- Support M&A transactions including acquisition financing, integration of acquired company bank accounts, and post-merger cash management.
- Maintain treasury policies and procedures including counterparty limits, investment guidelines, signatory authorities, and payment controls.
- Manage treasury technology including TMS implementation, bank portal administration, and payment file automation.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Cash forecast accuracy: variance between forecasted and actual cash position
- Days payable outstanding (DPO): average days to pay vendors
- Days sales outstanding (DSO): average days to collect from customers
- Debt covenant headroom: percentage buffer above minimum covenant thresholds
- FX hedging effectiveness ratio: percentage of FX exposure offset by hedging program
- Bank fee optimization: year-over-year change in total banking fees
- Investment yield: return on invested cash relative to benchmark
- Working capital ratio: current assets divided by current liabilities

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Treasury Analyst supporting cash positioning, bank account administration, payment processing, and reporting.
- Mid level: Senior Treasury Analyst managing cash forecasting, FX risk reporting, covenant tracking, and bank relationship support.
- Senior level: Treasury Manager overseeing daily treasury operations, leading banking projects, and managing risk programs.
- Lead level: Assistant Treasurer managing the full treasury function, leading capital market transactions, and developing treasury strategy.
- Director/VP level: Treasurer / VP of Treasury owning all treasury activities, capital markets relationships, investment management, and treasury technology.
- CFO path: Some Treasurers progress to CFO given their deep corporate finance and capital markets expertise.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as analyst, 2-3 years as senior analyst, 2-3 years as manager, then assistant treasurer or treasurer track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Review opening cash balances across all bank accounts, release prior day transactions, initiate wire transfers, execute FX trades, confirm overnight activity.
- Midday: Update cash forecast with latest AR/AP data, monitor debt facility availability and compliance, manage investment maturities and reinvestments, respond to banking inquiries.
- Afternoon: Approve payments in the TMS, review fraud detection alerts, update treasury reporting, maintain bank account documentation, prepare daily cash report.
- Weekly: Cash forecast rolling update, FX exposure review and hedge execution in accordance with policy, bank relationship calls, debt covenant check.
- Monthly: Month-end cash reconciliation, bank fee analysis and approval, investment portfolio performance review, treasury reporting package, interest accrual calculations.
- Quarterly: Debt covenant compliance certificates, FX hedge mark-to-market and effectiveness testing, banking relationship reviews, board reporting for treasury matters.
- Annual: Bank RFP and relationship review, credit facility renewal process, investment policy review, treasury technology evaluation, bank account reconciliation and cleanup.
- Rhythms: Daily cash management is a steady operational rhythm. Monthly reporting and quarterly compliance create predictable cycles. Capital market transactions (debt issuance, refinancing) are episodic but high intensity.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: USD-centric with TMS sophistication typical of large corporate treasuries. Revolver is standard credit facility structure. Cash pooling and notional pooling are common. ACH and wire payments dominate. SOX 404 compliance applies to public companies. Board engagement on treasury is high.
- EMEA: Cash pooling is highly developed but country-specific regulations vary (Germany, Netherlands permit pooling; some countries restrict). SEPA payments standardize euro transactions across 36 countries. VAT cash flow planning is a significant treasury activity. Negative interest rates have been a recent challenge. Multi-currency cash management is the norm.
- APAC: China's SAFE regulations restrict cross-border cash movement and require extensive documentation. India's foreign exchange management act (FEMA) regulates cross-border transactions. Japan's low/negative rate environment affects investment strategy. Australia and Singapore have sophisticated treasury markets. Different time zones require extended coverage.
- LATAM: Currency controls in Argentina and historically in Venezuela create significant treasury challenges. High inflation requires active cash management to minimize cash holding losses. Brazil's complex tax system affects payment processing. Counterparty risk concerns affect bank relationship strategy.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Recurring subscription revenue creates predictable cash flows. Negative working capital model is common (collect upfront, deliver later). High cash reserves from VC funding or IPO proceeds. Equity compensation administration requires treasury support. FX exposure from global customer base.
- Fintech: Regulatory capital management for licensed entities. Settlement risk management in payments businesses. Liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) and net stable funding ratio (NSFR) for regulated banks. Credit facility management for lending platforms. Real-time payment processing requirements.
- Healthcare: Patient collections and insurance reimbursement create complex AR cash cycles. Government program (Medicare, Medicaid) reimbursement timing is a critical cash flow factor. CapEx planning for medical equipment acquisitions. Hospital/care facilities have large physical asset bases.
- Manufacturing: Working capital-intensive with raw materials, WIP, and finished goods inventory. Supply chain financing programs for supplier payments. Large CapEx requirements for plant and equipment. Commodity price hedging is common. Export-import financing for global operations.
- E-commerce: High transaction volume with payment processor settlement lags. Seasonal cash requirements for inventory building. Chargeback and refund reserves. Cross-border payments infrastructure for global operations. Dynamic discounting for supplier payment optimization.

## Common Challenges
- Forecasting cash flows accurately when AR collections are unpredictable and large payments timing is uncertain.
- Managing FX exposure across 20+ currencies with limited ability to use complex hedging instruments.
- Balancing yield optimization with liquidity and safety when investing excess cash.
- Maintaining banking relationships and service levels while minimizing fees across multiple banking partners.
- Implementing and integrating treasury management systems across acquired companies with different banking structures.
- Managing cross-border cash mobility under varying regulatory regimes (SAFE in China, FEMA in India).

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: SOX 404 internal controls over treasury and cash, SEC cash disclosure requirements, IRS Section 956 and Subpart F for international cash management, Dodd-Frank derivatives reporting (FX, interest rate swaps), FinCEN anti-money laundering requirements, UCC Article 3/4 for payment systems and negotiable instruments.
- EU: European Market Infrastructure Regulation (EMIR) for derivative reporting, Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2), Anti-Money Laundering Directive (AMLD), Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) for bank exposures, EU Benchmarks Regulation (BMR), SEPA Regulation.
- UK: FCA supervision of treasury activities, UK MAR for share buyback programs, Bank of England reporting requirements, UK Money Laundering Regulations.
- Canada: Bank Act (banking regulations), OSFI guidance for liquidity management, Proceeds of Crime and Terrorist Financing Act, Canadian Derivatives Clearing requirements for OTC derivatives.
- Brazil: BCB (Central Bank) regulations for foreign exchange, CVM rules for investment, LGPD for treasury data protection, Central Bank reporting systems (Sisbacen), BACEN Resolution on cash management.
- Australia: APRA prudential standards (for authorized deposit-taking institutions), ASIC market integrity rules, RBA reporting for foreign exchange, AML/CTF Act for payment screening, ATO thin capitalization rules for debt structures.
