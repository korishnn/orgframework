# da-analyst

**Summary:** Analyzes business data, builds dashboards, and delivers actionable insights to stakeholders, enabling data-informed decision-making across the organization.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** Data Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Analyze business data to identify trends, patterns, anomalies, and opportunities for growth and optimization.
- Build and maintain dashboards, reports, and data visualizations that track KPIs and business metrics.
- Deliver actionable insights and recommendations to business stakeholders through presentations and written analyses.
- Perform ad-hoc analyses to support decision-making across product, marketing, sales, and operations teams.
- Define and track KPIs and business metrics in collaboration with stakeholders and leadership.
- Ensure data accuracy and integrity in all analysis and reporting through validation and quality checks.
- Design and evaluate A/B tests and experiments to measure the impact of product and business changes.
- Transform raw data from multiple sources into clean, structured datasets suitable for analysis.
- Document analysis methodologies, metric definitions, and business logic for reproducibility.
- Mentor junior analysts and contribute to data literacy initiatives across the organization.
- Partner with data engineers to improve data quality, availability, and accessibility for analysis.
- Create data storytelling presentations that communicate insights to non-technical audiences.

## Core Competencies
- Data analysis and statistical methods for extracting insights and testing hypotheses from business data.
- Dashboard development and data visualization best practices for communicating data effectively.
- SQL and data querying expertise for accessing and manipulating data from relational databases.
- Business acumen and problem-solving for translating business questions into analytical approaches.
- Communication and stakeholder management for presenting findings and influencing decisions.
- Critical thinking and attention to detail for identifying data quality issues and analytical pitfalls.
- Statistical analysis including descriptive statistics, hypothesis testing, regression analysis, and correlation.
- Data cleaning and preparation for transforming messy raw data into analysis-ready datasets.
- Experimentation and causal inference for A/B test design and results interpretation.
- Data storytelling and presentation for creating compelling narratives from data.

## Tools & Technology
- SQL query tools (DBeaver, DataGrip, MySQL Workbench, PostgreSQL tools)
- Business intelligence platforms (Tableau, Power BI, Looker, Metabase, Mode Analytics)
- Spreadsheet tools (Excel, Google Sheets for quick analysis and stakeholder sharing)
- Programming languages for analysis (Python with pandas, R with tidyverse)
- Statistical analysis packages (Python SciPy/StatsModels, R, JMP, SPSS)
- A/B testing platforms (Optimizely, Google Optimize, Statsig)
- Data warehouse querying (Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, Databricks)
- Collaboration and documentation (Notion, Confluence, GitHub Markdown for analytical documentation)
- Communication tools (Slack, Google Slides, PowerPoint for insight delivery)
- Version control for analysis (Git, GitHub for analytical code and queries)

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Stakeholder satisfaction score from feedback on analysis quality, timeliness, and actionability.
- Time from question to insight — cycle time for ad-hoc analysis requests.
- Dashboard adoption and usage metrics (active users, frequency of use, pages viewed).
- Number of data-informed decisions influenced by analysis output.
- Data quality incidents caught before reaching stakeholders or production reporting.
- Insight win rate — percentage of recommendations adopted by stakeholders.
- Query performance and efficiency improvements over time.

## Career Progression
Data analysts typically start as junior analysts (0-2 years), progress to mid-level (2-4 years), senior (4-7 years), and lead/staff (7+ years). Growth involves expanding from tactical analysis to strategic insight generation. Senior analysts lead complex analytical projects and advise senior stakeholders. Lead analysts define analytical frameworks and mentor teams. Career branching includes moving into data science (more advanced modeling), analytics engineering (more technical infrastructure), or product management (closer to product decisions). Some analysts transition into business operations or strategy roles. Advancement depends on business impact, analytical rigor, and stakeholder trust. Certifications (Google Data Analytics, Microsoft PL-300, Tableau Desktop Specialist) can supplement experience.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A data analyst's week is a mix of planned analysis and ad-hoc requests. Scheduled work includes updating recurring dashboards and reports, investigating metric changes, and deep-dive analysis projects. Ad-hoc requests arrive via Slack or ticketing systems — requiring quick scoping, analysis, and response. Morning hours are typically reserved for focused analytical work — writing SQL queries, coding analysis in Python/R, or building dashboards. Afternoons often include stakeholder meetings to review findings, refine requirements, and present insights. Stand-ups and team meetings coordinate work across the data team. Monthly business reviews present performance trends and insights to leadership. Quarterly deep dives explore specific business questions in depth with thorough analysis and written recommendations. Continuous learning time keeps analytical skills current with new tools and techniques.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **Product Managers** — Primary stakeholders for product analytics, feature performance, and experiment analysis.
- **Marketing Team** — Supports campaign analysis, channel performance, and customer segmentation.
- **Sales Team** — Provides pipeline analysis, conversion metrics, and territory performance reporting.
- **Finance Team** — Collaborates on revenue analysis, forecasting support, and cost analysis.
- **Data Engineers** — Partners on data quality issues, new data source access, and pipeline improvements.
- **Data Scientists** — Supports with data preparation and collaborates on model performance analysis.
- **BI / Reporting Peers** — Shares dashboard best practices and coordinates reporting standards.
- **Customer Success** — Analyzes churn, health scores, and customer behavior patterns.
- **People / HR Team** — Supports workforce analytics, retention analysis, and diversity reporting.
- **Executive Team** — Provides performance reporting, board materials, and strategic analysis support.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Data analysis is a mature field with dedicated roles. Highest compensation globally. Strong emphasis on SQL, Python, and BI tool expertise. Remote analytics teams are standard. Bootcamp graduates have created a large talent pool. Career mobility is high between industries.
**EMEA:** GDPR impacts data access and analysis practices significantly. Stronger emphasis on data privacy in all analysis work. Multi-language and cross-country analysis is common. Lower salaries than NA but strong social benefits. Works councils may influence analytical work involving employee data.
**APAC:** Data analysis is growing rapidly. India has a large talent pool serving global analytics needs. China requires separate data tooling and analytics infrastructure. Japan values precision and detail in analytical reporting. Singapore is a regional analytics hub for multinational companies.
**LATAM:** Data analysis field is expanding with growing talent pools in Brazil, Mexico, and Argentina. Many analysts work in near-shore roles for US companies. Salary arbitrage opportunities with remote US employment. Tool access and data infrastructure can be less mature than NA.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Analytics focuses on subscription metrics (MRR/ARR, churn, expansion revenue, LTV). Cohort analysis for retention and engagement. Funnel analysis for conversion optimization. Product analytics (feature usage, adoption, stickiness) is a core focus. Self-serve analytics culture is common.
**Fintech:** Regulatory compliance analytics and reporting. Transaction monitoring and fraud analysis. Customer lifetime value modeling for financial products. Risk analytics for credit and underwriting decisions. Capital efficiency and unit economics analysis.
**Healthcare:** Clinical outcome analysis and population health analytics. Claims data analysis and payer performance. Regulatory reporting analytics for compliance. Patient acquisition cost and LTV analysis. Provider network performance analytics.
**E-commerce:** Customer segmentation and cohort analysis for targeting. Shopping cart and checkout funnel optimization. Inventory and supply chain analytics. Marketing channel attribution and ROI analysis. A/B testing at high velocity for conversion optimization.
**Manufacturing:** Supply chain and logistics analytics. Production yield and quality analysis. Inventory turnover and demand forecasting. Factory performance and OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) metrics. Cost analysis and margin optimization.

## Common Challenges
- Accessing clean, reliable data when data quality issues and pipeline gaps require significant cleanup time.
- Managing ad-hoc request volume that crowds out time for deeper, more impactful analytical projects.
- Communicating analytical findings to non-technical stakeholders who may not understand statistical concepts or limitations.
- Getting stakeholders to act on data-driven insights when intuition or political considerations drive decisions.
- Keeping analysis reproducible and documentation current when balancing speed and rigor.
- Navigating ambiguous business questions that require refining scope and expectations iteratively.
- Staying current with evolving tools and technologies while delivering ongoing analytical work.
- Balancing depth of analysis with the speed required for timely decision-making.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- GDPR — data minimization, purpose limitation, and consent requirements for analytical data usage.
- CCPA — California consumer privacy rights affecting data used in analysis.
- HIPAA — protected health information handling for healthcare analytics.
- SOX — data integrity and audit trail requirements for financial analysis and reporting.
- PCI DSS — cardholder data protection for e-commerce and payments analysis.
- Data governance policies — access controls, data classification, and usage guidelines.
- Record retention policies for analytical data and analysis documentation.
- Model risk management (SR 11-7) for analytical models used in regulated financial decisions.
