# ops-quality

**Summary:** Establishes and maintains quality management systems, conducts audits and inspections, drives root cause analysis, and ensures compliance with quality standards and certifications across the organization.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** COO / Ops Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Establish, implement, and maintain quality management systems (QMS) aligned with industry standards including ISO 9001, ISO 13485, AS9100, or equivalent.
- Conduct internal quality audits of processes, products, suppliers, and operational outputs against established standards and regulatory requirements.
- Investigate quality issues, non-conformances, and customer complaints using root cause analysis methodologies and drive corrective and preventive actions (CAPA).
- Manage external certifications including audit scheduling, certification body relationships, and ongoing compliance maintenance.
- Develop quality metrics and reporting to track performance against benchmarks and drive continuous improvement.
- Define and monitor product and process quality specifications including tolerances, inspection criteria, and acceptance sampling plans.
- Manage supplier quality programs including incoming inspection, supplier audits, supplier scorecards, and corrective action tracking.
- Lead quality improvement projects using statistical process control (SPC), Six Sigma, Lean, and other quality methodologies.
- Develop and deliver quality training to operations teams, ensuring understanding of quality standards, inspection procedures, and documentation requirements.
- Maintain quality documentation including procedures, work instructions, quality records, and product specifications in a controlled document system.
- Participate in new product development processes to ensure quality requirements are defined and validated before launch.
- Manage calibration programs for inspection and test equipment to ensure measurement accuracy and traceability.
- Lead customer quality issue resolution including formal 8D reports, customer communication, and containment actions.
- Conduct risk assessments (FMEA, PFMEA) for processes and products to identify potential failure modes and implement preventive controls.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- First-pass yield (FPY): percentage of products passing inspection on first attempt without rework
- Customer quality complaints: number of quality-related complaints per million units shipped
- Supplier quality performance: percentage of supplier deliveries meeting quality specifications
- CAPA closure time: average days from identification to closure of corrective actions
- Internal audit finding closure rate: percentage of audit findings closed within target timeframe
- Cost of quality: cost of prevention, appraisal, internal failure, and external failure as percentage of revenue
- Scrap and rework rate: percentage of production output requiring scrap or rework
- Quality certification status: number of active certifications without gaps or lapses

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Quality Inspector or Quality Technician performing product inspection, data collection, and documentation.
- Mid level: Quality Engineer or Quality Specialist managing inspection plans, conducting audits, leading CAPA investigations, and performing SPC analysis.
- Senior level: Senior Quality Engineer or Quality Manager overseeing the QMS, managing the audit program, and leading quality improvement initiatives.
- Lead level: Quality Manager or Quality Systems Manager leading the quality function for a site or business unit, managing certification programs and teams.
- Director level: Director of Quality owning the enterprise quality function, strategy, and quality culture across all locations.
- VP level: VP of Quality / Chief Quality Officer responsible for global quality strategy, regulatory affairs, and quality governance.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as inspector/technician, 2-3 years as engineer/specialist, 2-3 years as manager, then director/VP track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Review overnight quality issues, non-conformance reports, and customer complaints. Prioritize urgent quality events. Check production quality metrics and SPC charts for out-of-control conditions.
- Midday: Conduct quality inspections of production output or incoming materials. Perform internal audits of processes. Investigate non-conformances using root cause analysis. Facilitate CAPA meetings with cross-functional teams.
- Afternoon: Update quality documentation, close out completed CAPAs, review supplier quality data, prepare quality reports, maintain calibration records, train production teams on quality procedures.
- Weekly: Quality metrics review meeting, production quality walkthrough, supplier corrective action status review, internal audit progress tracking, quality improvement team meetings.
- Monthly: Quality management review meeting, quality KPI reporting to leadership, customer complaint trend analysis, audit finding closure verification, calibration program status.
- Quarterly: QMS effectiveness review, quality objectives progress, supplier quality business reviews, certification body surveillance audits, quality improvement project status.
- Annual: Management review of QMS (per ISO requirements), recertification audit, quality system effectiveness evaluation, budget planning for quality programs, quality training needs assessment.
- Rhythms: Daily quality monitoring creates a steady operational rhythm. CAPA cycles follow defined timelines (30-90 day closure targets). Audit schedules follow a quarterly/annual calendar. Certification audits are annual with recertification every 3 years.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: ISO 9001 is the baseline quality standard; strong pressure for industry-specific standards (AS9100 for aerospace, IATF 16949 for automotive). Quality culture is mature with heavy emphasis on data-driven quality. Root cause analysis methodology is well-established. Product liability lawsuits drive focus on quality documentation.
- EMEA: ISO 9001 originated in Europe and is widely adopted. CE marking for European market access requires quality system compliance. Germany's focus on engineering quality (Qualitat made in Germany) sets high expectations. EU MDR for medical devices drives rigorous quality requirements. Product liability directive influences quality practices.
- APAC: Japan's quality culture (Kaizen, TQM, zero defects) sets global benchmarks. Six Sigma is widely adopted in India's manufacturing and services sectors. China's quality perception improvement has been rapid with CCC certification requirements. ISO adoption is widespread across APAC.
- LATAM: ISO certification is common for export-oriented manufacturers. Quality culture is developing but often reactive rather than preventive. Smaller local suppliers may lack mature quality systems. International certification required for export market access. Quality resources are often constrained.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Code quality and software testing processes are the primary quality focus (peer review, automated testing, CI/CD quality gates). UAT (user acceptance testing) as quality validation. Quality is measured through uptime, defect rates, and NPS. No physical quality inspection.
- Fintech: Financial transaction accuracy and data integrity are quality metrics. AML/KYC screening quality is critical. System availability and reliability are quality dimensions. Compliance quality checks are integrated into operations. Audit trail quality and completeness.
- Healthcare: FDA QSR (21 CFR 820) quality system requirements for medical devices. GMP (Good Manufacturing Practices) for pharmaceuticals. CAPA is mandatory for quality events. Design controls (21 CFR 820.30) for product development quality. Clinical quality measurement (HEDIS, CAHPS).
- Manufacturing: ISO 9001 or equivalent is mandatory for most OEM customers. Incoming material quality, in-process inspection, and final quality control are all required. SPC is widely used on production lines. Gage R&R for measurement system analysis. PPAP (Production Part Approval Process) for new products.
- E-commerce: Quality is measured through customer satisfaction rather than traditional manufacturing metrics. Product quality from suppliers is a focus. Packaging quality to prevent damage. Fulfillment accuracy quality checks. Seller quality and marketplace product quality enforcement. Returns data as quality feedback metric.

## Common Challenges
- Balancing quality requirements with production speed and cost pressures from operations management.
- Driving quality improvement in supplier organizations where the company has limited purchasing leverage.
- Investigating root cause when quality issues have multiple contributing factors across different departments.
- Keeping quality documentation current when processes change frequently without formal change control.
- Establishing a preventive quality culture when the organization is accustomed to reactive fire-fighting.
- Managing the transition from manual quality inspection to automated quality control (machine vision, AI inspection).
- Demonstrating the cost of poor quality (COPQ) to justify quality improvement investments.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: FDA 21 CFR 820 (Quality System Regulation for medical devices), FDA 21 CFR 210/211 (cGMP for pharmaceuticals), ISO 9001:2015 voluntary certification, AS9100D for aerospace, IATF 16949 for automotive, ANSI/NCSL Z540 for calibration, NIST traceability for measurement standards, FAA quality requirements for aviation parts, USDA quality standards for agricultural products, Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) for consumer product safety.
- UK: Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (UK MDR), MHRA Good Distribution Practice, UKCA marking quality requirements, BSI quality standards, UKAS accreditation for quality laboratories, British Standards (BSI) product quality specifications.
- EU: EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR 2017/745) for medical device quality, EU GMP (EudraLex Volume 4) for pharmaceuticals, CE marking quality requirements, ISO 9001 certification (national accreditation bodies), EU Quality Assurance Framework for regulated industries, Notified Body oversight for CE marking and MDR compliance.
- Canada: Health Canada Medical Device Regulations (CMDR SOR/98-282), Natural Health Products Regulations quality requirements, Canadian GMP for pharmaceuticals (GUI-0001), SCC (Standards Council of Canada) accreditation, Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) quality standards for food, Cannabis Act quality requirements for cannabis products.
- Brazil: ANVISA RDC 16/2013 (GMP for medical devices), ANVISA RDC 301/2019 for pharmaceutical GMP, INMETRO certification and quality requirements, ANVISA BPF (Good Manufacturing Practices) certification, MAPA quality standards for agricultural and food products, ISO 9001 certification through INMETRO-accredited bodies.
- Australia: TGA conformity assessment and quality requirements for medical devices and pharmaceuticals, Australian GMP Code for pharmaceutical manufacturing, Standards Australia quality standards (AS/NZS ISO 9001), APVMA quality requirements for agricultural and veterinary products, FSANZ food safety quality standards, NATA (National Association of Testing Authorities) laboratory accreditation.
