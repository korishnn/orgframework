# fin-audit

**Summary:** Evaluates internal controls, risk management processes, and governance frameworks across the organization through independent, risk-based audits that drive control improvement and regulatory compliance.

**Level:** IC or Manager
**Reports to:** Audit Committee / CFO
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Develop and execute a risk-based annual audit plan that covers financial, operational, compliance, and IT audit areas across the enterprise.
- Assess the design and operating effectiveness of internal controls over financial reporting (ICFR) in compliance with SOX requirements.
- Conduct operational audits to evaluate process efficiency, resource utilization, and adherence to management policies and procedures.
- Perform compliance audits to verify adherence to regulatory requirements, industry standards, and internal policies.
- Document audit findings, root causes, and recommendations in clear, actionable audit reports distributed to management and the audit committee.
- Track remediation of audit findings through follow-up audits and status reporting until issues are fully resolved.
- Coordinate with external auditors to ensure efficient audit coverage and avoid duplication of audit effort.
- Evaluate the company's risk management framework, including risk identification, assessment, and mitigation processes.
- Conduct fraud risk assessments and investigations in partnership with legal and security teams when allegations arise.
- Participate in the SOX 404 process including scoping, control documentation, walkthroughs, and control testing.
- Review IT general controls including access management, change management, computer operations, and SDLC processes.
- Present audit results to the audit committee, including key findings, risk ratings, and management response status.
- Support the external audit team with evidence requests, walkthroughs, and testing support during the annual financial statement audit.
- Maintain professional audit documentation in accordance with IIA standards and PCAOB requirements.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Audit plan completion rate: percentage of planned audit engagements completed on schedule
- Finding resolution time: average days to close audit findings from report issuance
- Repeat finding rate: percentage of findings that recur after initial remediation
- Control deficiency severity: number of significant deficiencies and material weaknesses
- External audit reliance: percentage of SOX testing relied upon by external auditors
- Audit efficiency: hours per audit vs. budget, improving year over year
- Stakeholder satisfaction: post-audit survey scores from auditees and management
- Fraud detection rate: number of confirmed fraud findings from audit activities

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Internal Auditor I performing audit testing under supervision, documenting workpapers, and supporting SOX testing.
- Mid level: Internal Auditor II or Senior Auditor leading audit engagements independently, managing auditee relationships, and drafting audit reports.
- Senior level: Audit Manager or Lead Auditor managing a portfolio of audits, coaching junior team members, and presenting to the audit committee.
- Manager level: Internal Audit Manager overseeing the SOX program, managing the audit team, and developing the annual audit plan.
- Director level: Director of Internal Audit owning the audit function, audit committee relationship, risk assessment process, and audit strategy.
- VP/Chief Audit Executive: CAE responsible for the entire internal audit function, board reporting, and independent assurance across the enterprise.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as staff auditor, 2-3 years as senior, 2-3 years as manager, then director/CAE track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Planning phase: Scope the audit, understand the process/area through walkthroughs, identify key risks and controls, develop audit program and testing approach.
- Fieldwork phase: Execute testing procedures (samples, data analysis, observation, inquiry), document workpapers, identify findings, discuss with process owners.
- Reporting phase: Draft audit report with findings, root causes, risk ratings, and recommendations. Review with management, finalize report, distribute to stakeholders.
- Remediation phase: Track management action plans, conduct follow-up testing, report remediation status to the audit committee.
- Weekly: Audit team standup, status updates on active engagements, finding tracking and follow-up, planning for upcoming audits.
- Monthly: Audit plan status reporting, time budgeting and actuals review, professional development (CPE tracking).
- Quarterly: Audit committee materials preparation, SOX status reporting, fraud risk assessment updates, audit plan adjustments.
- Annual: Risk assessment update, audit plan development, SOX scoping, external audit coordination, annual audit committee presentation.
- Rhythms: Each audit engagement follows the plan-execute-report-close lifecycle (4-8 weeks typical). Multiple engagements run in parallel. SOX testing follows the company's financial close calendar.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: SOX 404 is the dominant driver for public company audit functions. IIA Standards are the professional framework. PCAOB inspections set quality expectations for internal audit supporting external audit. Strong focus on cybersecurity and IT audit.
- EMEA: EU Audit Directive and Regulation impose additional requirements on audit committees. Works council access to audit reports in Germany. IFRS-driven audit approach. Country-specific corporate governance codes (UK Corporate Governance Code, German GCGC, Dutch Code) influence audit scope.
- APAC: Japan's Companies Act requires statutory auditor roles separate from internal audit. India's Companies Act 2013 mandates internal audit for certain companies. Australia's ASX Corporate Governance Council Principles require audit committee charters. Singapore's ACRA regulation influences audit practices.
- LATAM: Brazil's CVM requirements for listed companies shape audit scope. SOX-like requirements in some countries. Higher fraud risk environment requires more forensic audit focus. Local audit regulations vary significantly by country, requiring country-specific audit expertise.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: SOX 404 for public SaaS companies. Revenue recognition (ASC 606) is a high-risk audit area. ITGC audits are critical given cloud infrastructure. Access management and segregation of duties in a SaaS environment present unique challenges.
- Fintech: Regulatory compliance audits (anti-money laundering, consumer protection) are a major focus. Third-party risk management audits for outsourced operations. Capital adequacy and liquidity audits for licensed entities. Model audit for credit and risk models.
- Healthcare: HIPAA compliance audits are high priority. Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement audit risk is significant. 340B program audits for covered entities. Accreditation (JCI, NCQA) audit alignment.
- Manufacturing: Inventory and cost accounting audits are critical. Fixed asset audits for heavy equipment bases. Environmental compliance audits. Health and safety audit integration. Supply chain and supplier audit programs.
- E-commerce: High-volume transaction processing audit. ITGC audits for e-commerce platforms. Fraud control audits are a high priority. Third-party logistics provider audits. Data privacy audit (CCPA, GDPR) for customer data.

## Common Challenges
- Maintaining independence and objectivity while embedded in the organization and building relationships with auditees.
- Recruiting and retaining audit talent with the right blend of financial, operational, IT, and data analytics skills.
- Keeping pace with evolving technology (cloud, AI, automation) and auditing new and emerging risks.
- Balancing the demand for advisory and consultative audits with the assurance mandate for independence.
- Getting management to prioritize and remediate audit findings when they have competing operational pressures.
- Auditing global processes with different local controls, cultures, and regulatory requirements.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: SOX Sections 302, 404, and 409, PCAOB Auditing Standards (AS 2201 for ICFR), IIA International Professional Practices Framework (IPPF), SEC financial reporting rules, COSO Internal Control Integrated Framework (2013), COSO ERM Framework, GAO Yellow Book for government audits, OMB Uniform Guidance for federal awards.
- UK: UK Corporate Governance Code (audit committee requirements), FRC Revised Ethical Standard for auditors, Companies Act 2006 audit provisions, ICAEW and ACCA professional standards, FCA rules for regulated entities.
- EU: EU Audit Directive (2014/56/EU) and Regulation (537/2014), ESMA guidelines on enforcement of financial information, EC Corporate Governance framework, country-specific audit legislation and audit supervisory authority requirements.
- Canada: CPA Canada Handbook Assurance standards, NI 52-109 (CEO/CFO certification similar to SOX), Canadian Audit Committee best practices from CSA, provincial securities regulation, OSFI guidelines for regulated entities.
- Brazil: CVM Instruction 480 and 481 audit committee requirements, CRC (Conselho Regional de Contabilidade) professional standards for auditors, BACEN guidelines for financial institution audits, CVM requirements for risk management internal controls.
- Australia: ASX Corporate Governance Council Principles and Recommendations, APRA Prudential Standard CPS 220 for risk management, ASIC audit inspection program, Corporations Act 2001 audit provisions, AUASB Auditing Standards.
