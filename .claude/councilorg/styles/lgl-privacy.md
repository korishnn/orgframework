# lgl-privacy

**Summary:** Privacy Counsel responsible for developing, implementing, and maintaining the organization's global data privacy program, including policy development, regulatory compliance, and privacy-by-design product integration.

**Level:** IC (Attorney)
**Reports to:** General Counsel / Chief Privacy Officer
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Develop, maintain, and enforce global data privacy policies, standards, and procedures covering all operational jurisdictions
- Conduct and manage Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) and Legitimate Interest Assessments (LIAs) for new products, features, and data-processing activities
- Own the data subject rights response program including access, deletion, rectification, portability, and objection requests within regulatory timelines
- Advise product and engineering teams on privacy-by-design and by-default requirements throughout the product development lifecycle
- Monitor evolving privacy regulations globally and manage compliance roadmaps for new and amended laws
- Manage cross-border data transfer mechanisms including Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs), and transfer impact assessments
- Coordinate breach notification processes across all jurisdictions, including assessment, notification timing, and regulatory reporting
- Manage the privacy aspects of vendor and third-party risk assessments including data processing agreement reviews
- Serve as primary contact for data protection authorities, handling inquiries, complaints, and regulatory investigations
- Develop and deliver privacy training programs tailored to employee roles, departments, and data handling responsibilities
- Maintain the organization's record of processing activities (ROPA) and data inventory/mapping documentation
- Advise marketing, sales, and HR teams on consent requirements, direct marketing rules, and employee privacy matters

## Core Competencies
- Deep expertise in global data privacy laws including GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and emerging US state privacy laws
- Privacy impact assessment methodology and practical application experience
- Cross-functional collaboration with security, engineering, and product teams
- Regulatory communication and negotiation skills for authority engagement
- Privacy program management at organizational scale
- Technical understanding of data systems, architectures, and data flows
- Data inventory and mapping methodology (Nymity, OneTrust data mapping)
- Breach response and incident management expertise
- Consent management and preference architecture knowledge
- Cross-border data transfer mechanism expertise (SCCs, BCRs, adequacy decisions)
- Written communication for policies, notices, and regulatory filings
- Project management for multi-workstream privacy initiatives across international teams

## Career Progression
- **Privacy Analyst / Associate (0-3 years):** Supports DSR response, assists with DPIAs, reviews cookie compliance, maintains ROPA. Typically holds CIPP or similar certification. Learns privacy operations fundamentals.
- **Privacy Counsel (3-6 years):** Independently manages privacy programs and compliance activities. Leads DPIAs for complex products. Handles regulatory communications. Advises business teams. CIPP/US, CIPP/E, CIPM certifications typical.
- **Senior Privacy Counsel (6-10 years):** Leads privacy strategy for major product areas or international regions. Manages multi-jurisdictional compliance programs. Handles regulatory investigations. Mentors junior team members.
- **Director of Privacy (10-15 years):** Owns the enterprise privacy program. Manages a team of privacy professionals. Serves on data governance board. Partners with product leadership on privacy strategy. May serve as DPO for the organization.
- **Chief Privacy Officer / VP Privacy (15+ years):** Executive-level ownership of privacy function. Reports to board or risk committee. Sets privacy strategy aligned with business strategy. Engages with regulators at leadership level. Represents organization on privacy industry matters.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning review of privacy regulatory alerts and enforcement actions for relevant jurisdictions
- Process incoming data subject rights requests — verify identity, locate data across systems, prepare response within regulatory timeline
- Product team advisory sessions — attend design reviews to provide privacy-by-design input on new features
- Third-party privacy assessment reviews — evaluate vendor data processing practices and approve or flag for remediation
- Cookie compliance monitoring — review consent banner performance, scan for unauthorized tracking scripts
- Weekly privacy incident triage — assess potential breaches, determine notification obligations, coordinate with security
- Monthly privacy metrics reporting — DSR volume, DPIA pipeline, training completion, incident dashboard
- Quarterly compliance review — assess regulatory changes, update policies and roadmaps, refresh ROPA
- Annual privacy program assessment — evaluate program effectiveness, benchmark against industry peers
- Privacy training delivery — develop and refresh training modules, monitor completion rates, target remedial training to at-risk groups
- Communication rhythm includes structured regulatory tracking cadence and ad hoc business advisory

## Regional Nuances
- **NA:** Complex patchwork of state laws — CCPA/CPRA in California, plus Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, and others coming. No single federal comprehensive privacy law. Sectoral laws cover healthcare (HIPAA), finance (GLBA), children (COPPA), and credit reporting (FCRA). Litigation risk under state privacy causes of action and unfair competition laws. Compensation $1600K-$3000K.
- **EMEA:** GDPR is the dominant framework with active enforcement by lead supervisory authorities (Ireland, Luxembourg, Germany, UK). Local data protection authorities have varying enforcement approaches — German DSBs are aggressive, French CNIL is active, UK ICO is more business-friendly. Special categories of data are strictly protected. Employee consent is disfavored; legitimate interest requires careful documentation. DPO appointment required for many organizations.
- **APAC:** Rapidly evolving — China's PIPL (Personal Information Protection Law) and CSL create a comprehensive but restrictive environment with data localization requirements. Japan's APPI has adequacy status from the EU. South Korea's PIPA is one of the strictest in Asia. Australia's Privacy Act is under reform with stronger penalties. India's DPDP Act is in early enforcement stages. Cross-border transfer restrictions are growing across the region.
- **LATAM:** Brazil's LGPD is the dominant framework, modeled closely on GDPR with strong enforcement by ANPD. Argentina has had comprehensive privacy law for longer and holds EU adequacy status. Mexico's LFPDPPP has active enforcement. Colombia has strong privacy protections. Chile is updating its privacy framework. LGPD enforcement is increasing with significant fines being levied. US-style class-action privacy litigation is less common.

## Industry Variations
- **SaaS:** Privacy is a product feature — customers evaluate data handling practices in procurement. Multi-tenant architecture requires careful data isolation. Sub-processor management is complex. Increasing privacy requirements in RFPs and security questionnaires. Data residency commitments are common in SLAs.
- **Fintech:** Highest privacy regulatory density — Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act in US, Payment Services Directive (PSD2) in EU, screen scraping regulations, open banking frameworks. Privacy and security are deeply intertwined. Consumer financial data protections. Biometric data privacy for authentication.
- **Healthcare:** HIPAA Privacy Rule sets baseline for US health data with stricter state laws in some jurisdictions. GDPR special category data for health information. Clinical trial data privacy. Genetic data protections expanding. Patient consent requirements are complex and layered.
- **E-commerce:** Cookie consent and tracking compliance is front and center. Children's privacy for age-restricted products. Cross-border data flows are inherent to the business model. Profiling and personalization privacy requirements. Loyalty program data handling. Platform liability concerns.
- **Manufacturing/IoT:** Employee privacy in monitoring and surveillance. Connected device data collection and processing. Telematics and location data. Customer consent for smart product features. Data retention for product improvement and warranty analysis.

## Common Challenges
- Keeping pace with the rapid expansion of US state privacy laws without a federal preemptive standard
- Operationalizing cross-border data transfer compliance after Schrems II invalidation of Privacy Shield and ongoing adequacy challenges
- Embedding privacy-by-design into agile product development processes without creating friction or delays
- Managing conflicting requirements between privacy laws in different jurisdictions for global products
- Balancing business demand for data-driven personalization and analytics with privacy minimization principles
- Achieving effective data discovery and mapping across complex, legacy, and shadow IT data systems
