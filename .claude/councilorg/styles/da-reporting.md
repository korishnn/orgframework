# da-reporting

**Summary:** Owns recurring business reporting, KPI tracking, and stakeholder communications, ensuring consistent, accurate, and timely delivery of performance insights.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** Data Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Produce and maintain recurring business reports and KPI dashboards on daily, weekly, monthly, and quarterly cycles.
- Track and communicate business performance against targets, goals, and previous periods with variance analysis.
- Develop reporting calendars and ensure timely delivery of all scheduled reports to stakeholders.
- Partner with stakeholders to define, refine, and document reporting requirements and metric definitions.
- Identify trends, anomalies, and insights in regular reporting cycles and proactively communicate findings.
- Standardize reporting formats, templates, and processes across the organization for consistency.
- Create executive summaries and board-level reporting packages that highlight key performance narratives.
- Maintain reporting documentation including data sources, methodologies, and calculation logic.
- Continuously improve reporting processes through automation, simplification, and tooling enhancements.
- Respond to variance questions and provide context for performance changes in reporting cycles.
- Coordinate reporting contributions from multiple departments and consolidate into organization-wide reports.
- Train report consumers on reading and interpreting standard reports effectively.

## Core Competencies
- Business reporting and KPI management with understanding of metric definitions and performance measurement.
- Data analysis and visualization for creating clear, accurate, and insightful reports.
- Attention to detail and accuracy for ensuring data integrity in recurring reporting outputs.
- Stakeholder communication and relationship management for understanding and meeting reporting needs.
- Process organization and calendar management for maintaining reliable reporting schedules.
- Business acumen and contextual understanding for interpreting performance data meaningfully.
- Automation and efficiency improvement for reducing manual reporting effort over time.
- Presentation and narrative writing for crafting executive summaries and performance stories.
- Quality assurance and validation for catching errors before reports reach stakeholders.
- Time management and prioritization for balancing recurring deadlines with ad-hoc requests.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Reporting timeliness — percentage of recurring reports delivered on or before scheduled deadlines.
- Report accuracy — number of errors requiring corrections or re-issuance per reporting cycle.
- Stakeholder satisfaction with report quality, relevance, and clarity (survey score).
- Report consumption — number of views, downloads, and active consumers per report.
- Insights identified — number of actionable insights proactively communicated outside scheduled reporting.
- Cycle time reduction — time saved through reporting automation and process improvements.
- Report coverage — percentage of business decisions supported by standard reporting.

## Career Progression
Reporting professionals typically start as reporting analysts or junior BI analysts (0-2 years), progress to reporting specialists or analysts (2-5 years), senior reporting analysts (5-8 years), and reporting managers or leads (8+ years). Growth involves expanding from report production to reporting strategy and process design. Senior reporting analysts manage complex reporting portfolios and mentor junior team members. Career branching includes transitioning to broader data analysis, business analytics, or BI development roles. Some reporting professionals specialize in specific domains (financial reporting, regulatory reporting, operational reporting). Advancement depends on reporting quality, reliability, and process improvement impact. The role is evolving toward greater automation and self-service enablement.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A reporting professional's week is structured around reporting cycles. Early week focuses on data validation, checking pipeline completeness, and performing quality assurance on upcoming reports. Mid-week involves creating and reviewing report content, adding commentary, and preparing distribution. End of week delivers reports to stakeholders and begins the next cycle's preparation. Monthly close reporting is the most intensive period — consolidating department inputs, performing variance analysis, and preparing executive summaries. Quarterly reporting prepares board materials and deeper performance reviews. Between cycles, time is spent on reporting improvements — automating manual steps, refining templates, and adding new metrics. Stakeholder meetings gather feedback, discuss changing requirements, and align on reporting priorities. Training sessions help report consumers understand and interpret standard reports effectively.

- **Executives (CEO, CFO, COO)** — Primary consumers of executive reporting packages and board materials.
- **Finance Team** — Key partner on financial reporting, budget variance analysis, and forecast reporting.

- **HR / People Analytics** — Partners on workforce reporting headcount, attrition, and diversity metrics.
- **Product Operations** — Coordinates on product performance reporting and feature adoption tracking.
- **Investor Relations** — Supports quarterly earnings preparation and shareholder reporting requirements.
- **Department Heads** — Gathers department-level reporting inputs and ensures cross-functional alignment.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** Reporting is a well-established function with mature processes. Strong emphasis on self-service migration to reduce manual reporting. High stakes for reporting accuracy given public company quarterly earnings focus. Remote coordination across time zones for consolidated reporting.
**EMEA:** Multi-language and multi-currency reporting is standard. Cross-country consolidation requires handling different fiscal calendars and reporting standards (IFRS vs local GAAP). GDPR impacts what employee and customer data can be included in reports. Works Council involvement in workforce reporting.
**APAC:** Regional reporting must account for different public holidays, fiscal years, and business cycles across markets. Japan's fiscal year runs April-March. Currency translation for regional consolidation is complex. China requires separate data infrastructure for reporting given internet restrictions.
**LATAM:** High inflation and currency devaluation complicate financial reporting and variance analysis. Frequent changes in tax and regulatory reporting requirements. Economic volatility requires more frequent forecast updates and scenario reporting. Hyperinflation accounting adjustments are needed in some markets.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Reporting focuses on recurring revenue metrics, cohort retention tables, unit economics (CAC, LTV), and usage/engagement trends. Subscription reporting includes logo churn, dollar churn (net revenue retention), and expansion metrics. Board reporting emphasizes growth efficiency and market penetration.
**Fintech:** Regulatory reporting requirements are extensive and non-negotiable — risk reporting, capital adequacy, and compliance metrics. Transaction reporting with specific formatting and timing requirements for regulators. Credit portfolio performance reporting is critical.
**Healthcare:** Clinical quality reporting (HEDIS, Star Ratings), payer performance metrics, and patient outcome reporting. Regulatory reporting for CMS, state health departments, and accrediting bodies. Population health and value-based care performance reporting.
**E-commerce:** Daily flash sales and inventory reporting, real-time operational dashboards, and peak period (holiday) reporting. Order and fulfillment performance metrics. Customer lifetime value and cohort reporting for marketing optimization. P&L reporting for marketplace operations.

## Common Challenges
- Maintaining reporting accuracy when upstream data sources have quality issues or pipeline delays.
- Meeting tight reporting deadlines during month-end and quarter-end close periods with incomplete data.
- Managing report proliferation — too many reports with overlapping metrics creating confusion.
- Adapting reports to evolving business priorities and metric definitions while maintaining historical comparability.
- Getting stakeholders to use standard reports instead of requesting custom variations and ad-hoc analyses.
- Automating reporting processes when source systems change and break automated workflows.
- Providing meaningful context and narrative for metric movements without introducing bias or speculation.
- Balancing depth and breadth in reporting — enough detail to be useful without overwhelming readers.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- SOX — internal controls over financial reporting, data integrity requirements, and audit trails.
- SEC regulations — public company periodic reporting (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K) requirements for data accuracy.
- GDPR — data minimization and purpose limitation for personal data included in business reporting.
- HIPAA — PHI handling in healthcare reporting and patient data de-identification requirements.
- Basel III — financial reporting standards for banking and financial institutions.
- IFRS and GAAP — accounting standards affecting financial reporting presentation and methodology.
- CCPA — consumer data access and deletion request handling through reporting data.
- Internal control frameworks — segregation of duties, review processes, and approval workflows for reporting.
