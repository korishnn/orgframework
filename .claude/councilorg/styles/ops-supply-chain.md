# ops-supply-chain

**Summary:** Manages end-to-end supply chain operations including strategic sourcing, supplier management, inventory planning, demand forecasting, and logistics optimization to ensure product availability at optimal cost.

**Level:** Manager
**Reports to:** COO / Ops Director
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Manage end-to-end supply chain operations including sourcing, procurement, inventory management, production planning, and distribution.
- Develop and maintain demand forecasts using statistical models, sales inputs, historical data, and market intelligence to drive supply planning.
- Optimize inventory levels across raw materials, work-in-progress, and finished goods to balance service levels with working capital targets.
- Develop and maintain strategic supplier relationships, conducting regular performance reviews and driving continuous improvement.
- Identify and mitigate supply chain risks including single-source dependencies, geopolitical exposure, commodity price volatility, and logistics disruptions.
- Lead the Sales and Operations Planning (S&OP) process, aligning demand, supply, and financial plans across the organization.
- Manage purchase order execution and supplier delivery performance, proactively addressing supply shortages or delays.
- Optimize the supply chain network including warehouse locations, distribution routes, and transportation modes to minimize total delivered cost.
- Implement and maintain supply chain technology including ERP planning modules, demand planning systems, and warehouse management systems.
- Conduct make vs. buy analysis to inform strategic sourcing decisions and vertical integration strategy.
- Manage import/export operations including customs clearance, duties optimization, and trade compliance documentation.
- Drive supply chain sustainability initiatives including carbon footprint reduction, ethical sourcing, and waste reduction programs.
- Develop and monitor supply chain KPIs including on-time delivery, inventory turns, fill rates, and total supply chain cost as percentage of revenue.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- On-time in-full (OTIF) delivery rate: percentage of customer orders delivered on time and complete
- Inventory turnover ratio: annual COGS divided by average inventory value
- Perfect order rate: percentage of orders delivered without damage, errors, or delays
- Cash-to-cash cycle time: days from paying suppliers to collecting from customers
- Forecast accuracy: MAPE of demand forecast vs. actual sales
- Supplier quality rate: percentage of supplier deliveries meeting quality specifications
- Total supply chain cost as percentage of revenue
- Supply chain disruption incidents: number of significant unplanned supply interruptions per quarter

## Career Progression
- Entry level: Supply Chain Analyst supporting demand planning, inventory analysis, supplier communication, and reporting.
- Mid level: Supply Chain Planner or Buyer managing assigned product categories, supplier relationships, and inventory planning independently.
- Senior level: Supply Chain Manager overseeing an end-to-end supply chain process (planning, sourcing, inventory) and leading the S&OP process.
- Lead level: Senior Supply Chain Manager leading a team of planners and buyers, managing the supply chain for a business unit or region.
- Director level: Director of Supply Chain owning the entire supply chain function, network strategy, and cross-functional alignment.
- VP level: VP of Supply Chain or Chief Supply Chain Officer responsible for global supply chain strategy, digital transformation, and risk management.
- Timeline: 1-2 years as analyst, 2-3 years as planner/manager, 2-3 years as senior manager, then director/VP track.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
- Morning: Check supply chain dashboard, review inventory levels against targets, address any overnight supply disruptions or logistics issues, review supplier delivery status.
- Midday: Supplier calls on delivery status, lead time changes, or quality issues. S&OP meetings, production planning review, inventory optimization analysis.
- Afternoon: Demand forecast updates, purchase order approvals, supply chain analytics and reporting, logistics coordination, continuous improvement projects.
- Weekly: S&OP demand and supply review meeting, supplier performance review, inventory analysis and reorder decisions, logistics status review.
- Monthly: S&OP executive review meeting, inventory reconciliation, supply chain KPIs reporting, supplier scorecard updates, demand forecast re-forecast.
- Quarterly: Strategic supplier business reviews, inventory valuation and reserve analysis, supply chain risk assessment update, network optimization analysis.
- Annual: Supply chain strategic plan development, annual supplier negotiations, budget input for supply chain costs, network design review.
- Rhythms: Daily supply monitoring and exception management creates operational rhythm. Weekly S&OP provides tactical planning cadence. Monthly and quarterly reviews drive strategic alignment. Seasonal demand peaks require capacity planning months in advance.

## Regional Nuances
- NA: Just-in-time (JIT) inventory has been partly replaced by just-in-case (JIC) post-COVID disruptions. Focus on near-shoring and Mexico (nearshoring from Asia). Last-mile delivery is a key competitive differentiator. Labor shortages in warehousing and trucking are persistent.
- EMEA: Cross-border supply chain complexity within EU and with UK post-Brexit. Strong environmental regulations affect packaging, emissions, and waste. Germany's industrial supply chain is the backbone of European manufacturing. Eastern Europe is growing as a manufacturing base.
- APAC: China remains the world's factory but supply chains are diversifying to Vietnam, Thailand, India. Japan's supply chain emphasizes quality and long-term supplier relationships (keiretsu). Australia and New Zealand have long lead times due to geographic isolation. Infrastructure quality varies significantly across the region.
- LATAM: Brazil's complex tax system affects cross-state goods movement (ICMS tax). Mexico's proximity to US drives maquiladora and nearshoring growth. Infrastructure gaps in logistics (roads, ports) increase costs and lead times. Customs clearance is slower and more bureaucratic than in other regions.

## Industry Variations
- SaaS: Supply chain is mostly digital (infrastructure capacity planning). No physical inventory in the traditional sense. Data center capacity and cloud service provisioning is the supply chain equivalent. Subscription user growth drives capacity planning.
- Fintech: Card production and delivery supply chain for physical cards. No inventory in traditional sense. Regulatory reporting supply chain for compliance data. Customer onboarding documentation supply chain.
- Healthcare: Cold chain logistics for pharmaceuticals and biologics. Strict lot traceability and serialization requirements. Expiration date management is critical. 340B program supply chain compliance for covered entities. Supplier quality audits are mandatory.
- Manufacturing: Raw material supply chain is the core function. Production scheduling and material requirements planning (MRP) are central. Bill of materials (BOM) management. Supplier development and qualification programs. Plant maintenance supply chain for spare parts.
- E-commerce: Fast fulfillment and last-mile delivery are core competencies. Multi-channel supply chain (online, retail, wholesale). Peak demand capacity planning (holiday surge). Returns management and reverse logistics. Same-day and next-day delivery capability is table stakes.

## Common Challenges
- Managing supply chain disruptions from geopolitical events, natural disasters, logistics bottlenecks, and supplier failures.
- Balancing inventory investment with customer service level requirements across thousands of SKUs.
- Forecasting demand accurately when customer buying patterns are volatile or product lifecycles are short.
- Building supply chain resilience and redundancy without significantly increasing costs.
- Integrating supply chain planning across acquired companies with different systems, processes, and suppliers.
- Recruiting and retaining supply chain talent with both analytical and operational skills.
- Reducing supply chain carbon footprint while maintaining cost and service performance.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- US: Dodd-Frank conflict minerals reporting, UFLPA (Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act) supply chain documentation, FDA regulations for food and drug supply chains, USDA for agricultural products, DOT hazardous materials transportation regulations, CBP customs compliance and ACE filing, FSMA for food supply chain safety, OSHA warehouse safety requirements.
- UK: Modern Slavery Act 2015 transparency in supply chains (Section 54), UKCA marking for product compliance, Custom (Transition) Regulations post-Brexit, Environment Act 2021 waste and packaging regulations.
- EU: EU Conflict Minerals Regulation, EU Forced Labour Regulation (bans products made with forced labor from EU market), EU Deforestation Regulation supply chain due diligence, Waste Framework Directive and Extended Producer Responsibility, REACH chemical supply chain regulations, Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD), EU CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism).
- Canada: Fighting Against Forced Labour and Child Labour in Supply Chains Act, Canada Consumer Product Safety Act, Safe Food for Canadians Regulations, Canadian Environmental Protection Act for chemical supply chains, CBSA customs compliance.
- Brazil: ANVISA health regulation supply chains for medical/pharma products, INMETRO certification for regulated products, IBAMA environmental regulation for certain raw materials, SECEX import/export controls.
- Australia: Modern Slavery Act 2018 supply chain reporting, Biosecurity Act 2015 import requirements, Australian Consumer Law product safety compliance, Therapeutic Goods Administration for pharma supply chains, DAFF biosecurity requirements for agricultural imports.
