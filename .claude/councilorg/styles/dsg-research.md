# dsg-research

**Summary:** Conducts user research, usability studies, and design validation, providing actionable insights that drive user-centered product decisions.

**Level:** IC
**Reports to:** Design Director / Product
**Region variants:** NA, EMEA, APAC, LATAM

## Responsibilities
- Plan and conduct user research studies using qualitative methods (interviews, ethnographic studies, diary studies) and quantitative methods (surveys, analytics analysis, A/B testing).
- Perform usability testing at various stages of the design lifecycle from early concepts to live products.
- Synthesize research findings into actionable insights, recommendations, and design implications.
- Create research artifacts including personas, journey maps, experience maps, and empathy maps.
- Advocate for user needs throughout the product development lifecycle, influencing prioritization and design decisions.
- Build and maintain a research repository, knowledge base, and insights database for team access.
- Design and execute evaluative research to validate design solutions before and after launch.
- Partner with product managers and designers to translate research questions into study plans.
- Conduct competitive research and benchmark studies to understand market positioning.
- Present research findings to stakeholders through written reports, presentations, and readouts.
- Establish research operations including participant recruitment, scheduling, consent, and incentive management.
- Mentor junior researchers and promote research best practices across the organization.

## Core Competencies
- User research methods and study design across formative, summative, generative, and evaluative approaches.
- Usability testing and evaluation including moderated, unmoderated, remote, and in-person methodologies.
- Qualitative data analysis for identifying themes, patterns, and insights from interviews and observations.
- Quantitative data analysis and statistical methods for survey data, behavioral analytics, and experimental results.
- Synthesis and insight communication for translating raw findings into compelling, actionable recommendations.
- Empathy and user advocacy for representing user perspectives in product decisions.
- Cross-functional collaboration and storytelling for influencing product direction with research evidence.
- Research planning and project management for scoping studies, recruiting participants, and managing timelines.
- Domain and industry knowledge for conducting contextually relevant research.
- Ethical research practices including informed consent, privacy protection, and inclusive recruitment.

## KPIs & Success Metrics
- Research impact score — percentage of product decisions influenced by research insights.
- Time from research request to insight delivery (study cycle time).
- Research velocity — number of studies completed per quarter.
- Participant diversity score against target demographics and user segments.
- Stakeholder satisfaction with research quality and actionability (survey score).
- Research repository usage (searches, views, and reuse of past insights).
- Usability improvement measured through System Usability Scale (SUS) before and after research-driven redesigns.

## Career Progression
UX researchers typically start as junior researchers (0-2 years), progress to mid-level (2-4 years), senior (4-7 years), and staff/principal (7+ years). Growth involves expanding research methods expertise and strategic influence. Senior researchers lead complex, multi-method research programs. Staff and principal researchers shape organizational research strategy and methodology. Some researchers transition into product management, UX design, or data science roles. The research career has both IC and management tracks. PhDs in HCI, cognitive psychology, anthropology, or social sciences are common among senior researchers. Advancement depends on research quality, business impact, and methodology innovation. Research operations (ResearchOps) has emerged as a specialization for managing research programs and infrastructure.

## Day-to-Day Workflow
A researcher's week is driven by study cycles. Study preparation involves scoping research questions with stakeholders, designing protocols, and recruiting participants through screeners. Data collection days involve running interview sessions (typically 4-6 per day for multi-day studies), moderating usability tests, or fielding surveys. Synthesis days are intensive analysis periods following data collection — transcribing, coding, affinity mapping, and identifying themes. Reporting and readout sessions present findings to product teams, designers, and executives. Between studies, researchers maintain the research repository, review past insights, and participate in product team planning. Weekly research team meetings discuss methodology, share findings, and coordinate participant recruitment. Stakeholder alignment sessions ensure research priorities match business needs. Operations time includes managing participant panels, updating consent forms, and refining research processes.

## Cross-Functional Relationships
- **UX Designer / Product Designer** — Primary partner; provides research input for design decisions and evaluates design solutions.
- **Product Manager** — Aligns research priorities with product roadmap and translates insights into requirements.
- **Data Analyst / Data Scientist** — Collaborates on mixed-methods research combining qualitative and quantitative data.
- **Content Designer** — Partners on content testing, readability studies, and language comprehension research.
- **Product Marketing** — Provides customer insights for positioning, messaging, and market understanding.
- **Customer Support** — Leverages support data for research questions and participant recruitment.
- **Engineering** — Engages in technical feasibility research and developer experience studies.
- **Data Science** - Collaborates on A/B test design and behavioral analytics interpretation.
- **Legal / Privacy** - Ensures research compliance with data protection and consent requirements.
- **Research Operations (if applicable)** - Manages participant recruitment, tools, and research infrastructure.

## Regional Nuances
**NA:** UX research is well-established with dedicated roles in most product organizations. High compensation for experienced researchers. Strong emphasis on mixed-methods expertise. Remote research is standard post-pandemic. Participant recruitment panels are widely available.
**EMEA:** GDPR significantly impacts research data collection, storage, and participant consent processes. Multi-language research is standard — researchers often conduct studies in multiple European languages. Strong academic research traditions influence industry research practices. Lower salaries than NA but strong research culture.
**APAC:** Recruiting diverse participants across languages and cultures is challenging. Translation and cultural adaptation of research protocols requires expertise. China market requires separate research infrastructure given internet restrictions. High-context communication cultures (Japan, Korea) require adapted moderation techniques.
**LATAM:** UX research is an emerging discipline with limited senior practitioners. Many researchers work as generalists covering both research and design. Participant recruitment is challenging in smaller markets. Remote research helps access distributed participant populations. Lower salaries but growing demand for research skills.

## Industry Variations
**SaaS:** Research focuses on complex B2B workflows, multi-role purchasing decisions, and enterprise user needs. Long sales cycles require research across buyer and user personas. Time-to-value and onboarding research is critical for PLG. Telemetry and product analytics supplement qualitative research.
**Fintech:** Regulatory compliance affects what researchers can test and how. Financial literacy considerations require adapted research approaches. Security and trust research are recurring themes. Sensitive financial behaviors require skilled moderation and participant comfort.
**Healthcare:** HIPAA compliance impacts participant data handling and study documentation. Clinical research ethics (IRB) may apply to user research. Patient populations require sensitive recruitment and moderation. Clinician workflow research requires understanding of medical context and terminology.
**E-commerce:** High-velocity research with quick turnaround for campaign and feature testing. A/B testing and quantitative methods dominate. Cart and checkout research is continuous. Mobile commerce research is essential. Cross-cultural shopping behavior research for global marketplaces.

## Common Challenges
- Getting research integrated early enough in the product development cycle to influence design before implementation begins.
- balancing rigor and speed — conducting valid, reliable research while meeting product team velocity expectations.
- Getting stakeholders to act on research findings, especially when insights challenge existing assumptions or roadmap commitments.
- Recruiting representative, diverse participant panels, particularly for specialized B2B or niche user segments.
- Building a culture of research where decisions routinely reference evidence rather than opinion or authority.
- Scaling research impact across the organization when there are more research needs than researcher capacity.
- Maintaining research data quality and reducing bias in study design, moderation, and analysis.
- Transitioning from project-based research to continuous discovery and research-integrated practices.

## Key Regulations & Compliance
- GDPR — consent requirements for research participant data collection, storage, and processing in EU.
- CCPA — California privacy rights for research participants including data access and deletion.
- HIPAA — healthcare research compliance for studies involving protected health information.
- IRB (Institutional Review Board) requirements for research involving human subjects in academic or clinical contexts.
- COPPA — special consent requirements for research involving children under 13.
- Accessibility standards (Section 508, WCAG) for inclusive research practices and assistive technology testing.
- Deceptive Practices regulations affecting how research findings can be represented in marketing claims.
- Data minimization and retention policies for research data in compliance with privacy regulations.
- Professional research ethics codes (QRCA, ESOMAR) for conduct standards in user research practice.
